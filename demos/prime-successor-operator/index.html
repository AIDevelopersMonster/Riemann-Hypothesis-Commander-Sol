Prime-Successor Operator Lab

A finite interactive model of the two key facts: the standard regular operator lift preserves every permutation of prime coordinates, while an additional operator can be too symmetric, arbitrarily rigid, or so strong that it simply imports ordinary magnitude.

1. Unitary implementation of a prime permutation

We truncate the free commutative monoid on four prime generators to total degree ≤ 3. The left shifts become finite partial shifts. Because prime permutations preserve total degree, the conjugacy identity remains exact in this truncation.

3. Three regimes

full symmetry→rigidity→desired orientation→full magnitude

The target is not merely “trivial stabilizer.” It must recover the standard prime-successor chain by one intrinsic rule, without encoding the whole number line.

4. Why “another operator” must carry genuinely new information

Too weak

DΩ with w(p)=1 sees only multiplicative depth Ω(n). Every prime permutation still stabilizes it.

Rigid but arbitrary

Distinct weights destroy all prime permutations, but the induced ordering can be any ordering we chose in advance.

Too strong

Hlog with w(p)=log p gives E(n)=log n. Correct orientation appears because the full ordinary magnitude was imported.

Open target

D* should break symmetry, give a uniform local/spectral successor rule, and avoid hidden nextPrime or ordinary magnitude.
