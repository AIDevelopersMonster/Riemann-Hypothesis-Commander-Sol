## 2. Mixed cells have no reflection fixed points

### Lemma 2.1

For every \(i,j\ge1\),

\[
\nu^{(2)}(P_i^+,P_j^-)
=(P_i^-,P_j^+)
\ne
(P_i^+,P_j^-).
\]

Likewise every cell in \(M^{-+}\) is exchanged with a distinct cell in \(M^{+-}\).

### Proof

The positive and negative branches are disjoint away from \(P_0\). Hence \(P_i^+\ne P_i^-\) and \(P_j^-\ne P_j^+\). Simultaneous reflection therefore changes both coordinates and cannot fix the ordered pair. \(\square\)

### Corollary 2.2 — even-orbit principle

Every finite reflection-invariant set of genuinely mixed cells is a disjoint union of two-element reflection orbits. Hence its cardinality is even.

In particular, the smallest nonempty reflection-compatible mixed-domain extension contains exactly two ordered cells.

## 3. Minimal symmetric bridge

Fix \(i,j\ge1\). Add one new terminal output orbit. There are again two natural value variants.

### Shared bridge

Introduce one output \(C_{ij}\) fixed by output reflection and define

\[
P_i^+\diamond P_j^-=C_{ij},
\tag{1}
\]

\[
P_i^-\diamond P_j^+=C_{ij}.
\tag{2}
\]

### Split bridge

Introduce outputs \(C_{ij}^+,C_{ij}^-\) exchanged by reflection and define

\[
P_i^+\diamond P_j^-=C_{ij}^+,
\tag{3}
\]

\[
P_i^-\diamond P_j^+=C_{ij}^-.
\tag{4}
\]

Here `diamond` may be a new operation or these two cells may be added to an existing operation extension. The theorem below concerns only the mixed-domain support.

### Theorem 3.1 — exact reflection-compatible support threshold

Among nonempty extensions supported only on genuinely mixed ordered pairs, the minimum number of added base-domain cells compatible with zero reflection is

\[
\boxed{2.}
\]

Both (1)-(2) and (3)-(4) attain the bound.

### Proof

By Lemma 2.1 every mixed cell lies in a reflection orbit of size exactly two. A reflection-invariant domain containing one cell must contain its distinct mirror, proving the lower bound. The displayed bridge constructions use exactly one such orbit and are reflection-equivariant, proving attainability. \(\square\)

## 4. Minimal asymmetric bridge

Now add only one directed mixed cell

\[
P_i^+\diamond P_j^-=C,
\tag{5}

and do **not** add its reflected partner.

### Theorem 4.1 — one-cell reflection breaking

The extension (5) does not admit zero reflection as an automorphism.

### Proof

If \(\nu\) were an automorphism, definedness of (5) would force definedness of its reflected cell

\[
P_i^-\diamond P_j^+,

This is pure domain asymmetry; no output-value distinction is required.

## 5. One-cell rigidity over signed `oplus`

Consider a finite symmetric window \(W_N\) with \(N\ge\max\{i,j\}\). Let \(\oplus^{\rightarrow}_{i,j}\) be signed `oplus` plus exactly one new defined mixed cell

\[
P_i^+\oplus^{\rightarrow}_{i,j}P_j^-=C
\]

with fresh terminal output \(C\), and no reflected partner.

### Theorem 5.1 — exact one-cell rigidity

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Aut}(W_N,\oplus^{\rightarrow}_{i,j})=1.
}
\]

### Proof

Before the new cell is added,

\[
\operatorname{Aut}(W_N,\oplus)=\{\operatorname{id},\nu\}.
\]

The identity preserves the extension. By Theorem 4.1, \(\nu\) does not. There are no other old automorphisms to survive. Hence only the identity remains. \(\square\)

### Corollary 5.2 — exact support cost of breaking residual sign symmetry

Relative to the hostile-audited signed `oplus` baseline, the minimum added mixed-domain support needed to make every finite window containing the chosen bridge coordinates rigid is

\[
\boxed{1.}
\]

if reflection symmetry is allowed to be broken.

If reflection equivariance must be preserved, no such rigidity can be obtained from the domain extension alone because \(\nu\) remains an automorphism.
