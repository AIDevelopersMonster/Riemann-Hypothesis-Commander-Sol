## 1. Audit question

The package claims the chain

\[
\text{rooted ray}
\to
\text{minimal reversible completion}
\to
\text{derived zero reflection}
\to
\text{equivariant extension of the legacy FCOA operation}
\to
\text{radialization}
\to
\text{mixed-sector localization}.
\]

The audit tests whether any step:

1. silently imports ordinary integer addition/multiplication;
2. treats a chosen extension principle as if it were forced by reversibility alone;
3. overstates identity of the old and extended operations;
4. overstates the domain on which radial contraction holds;
5. understates the hypotheses of Mixed-Sector Localization;
6. conflicts with known prior art strongly enough to invalidate the mathematical claim.

## 2. Minimal Reversible Completion

### Verdict: PASS

The proof is correct under the declared definition.

A pointed reversible completion is generated by one full orbit

\[
X=\{T^k x_0:k\in\mathbb Z\}
\]

and contains the old ray injectively as its nonnegative forward orbit. Periodicity is excluded by injectivity of the old embedding. Therefore

\[
k\mapsto T^k x_0
\]

is a bijection from \(\mathbb Z\) to \(X\).

The uniqueness statement is also correct: a pointed shift isomorphism is forced on every integer iterate of the root.

### Prior-art limitation

This theorem is not a novelty claim. Reversible/natural extensions of irreversible dynamical systems are classical, and one-sided/two-sided shift completion is standard. Relevant modern reference:

B. K. Kwaśniewski and A. V. Lebedev, *Reversible extensions of irreversible dynamical systems: the C*-method*, Sbornik: Mathematics 199 (2008), 1621–1648, DOI `10.1070/SM2008v199n11ABEH003975`.

The paper must continue to state explicitly that the publication nucleus starts only when the legacy partial operation is transported.

## 3. Derived reflection

### Verdict: PASS

Once the minimal reversible orbit and root are fixed,

\[
\nu(T^k x_0)=T^{-k}x_0
\]

is well-defined, involutive, root-fixing, and uniquely satisfies

\[
\nu T=T^{-1}\nu.
\]

Thus reflection need not be a primitive step in construction of the second direction.

### Scope

This does **not** imply that every later FCOA operation must be reflection-equivariant. Reflection is canonical for the carrier; equivariance of an operation is an additional extension principle that must be stated separately.

## 4. Correction A — “the same operation” terminology

### Problem

The old operation on the ray and its signed extension cannot literally be the same set-theoretic partial function because their carriers/domains differ.

### Required formulation

Use:

> a single extended operation symbol \(\oplus\) whose restriction to the embedded old ray is exactly the legacy operation \(\oplus_R\).

or:

\[
\oplus\big|_{i(R)^2}=i_*(\oplus_R)
\]

with the appropriate treatment of output sorts.

Then sector-local differences concern restrictions of this **one extended operation** to different regions of the enlarged carrier.

### Severity

Scope/terminology only. No theorem retreat.

## 5. Symmetry-Induced Radialization

### Verdict: PASS

Assume:

1. the old ray law
   \[
   P_n\oplus_R P_0=P_{n-1};
   \]
2. minimal reversible completion \((X,x_0,T)\);
3. derived reflection \(\nu(T^k x_0)=T^{-k}x_0\);
4. a reflection-equivariant extension of the legacy operation preserving the old ray exactly.

Then for \(k>0\),

\[
x_k\oplus x_0=x_{k-1}=T^{-1}x_k,
\]

and for \(k<0\),

\[
x_k\oplus x_0=x_{k+1}=Tx_k.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{x_k\oplus x_0=x_{k-\operatorname{sgn}(k)}}
\qquad(k\ne0).
\]

The result is exact.

## 6. Correction B — radial contraction is punctured, not total

### Problem

The phrase “global radial contraction” can be read as including the root itself, while the legacy M0 cell

\[
P_0\oplus P_0
\]

is undefined.

### Required formulation

The right-zero partial translation

\[
R_{x_0}^{\oplus}:x\mapsto x\oplus x_0
\]

is a radial contraction on

\[
X\setminus\{x_0\}.
\]

It decreases radial depth by one:

\[
|k|\mapsto |k|-1
\qquad(k\ne0).
\]

Do not call it a total self-map of all \(X\) unless the root cell is separately defined in a later extension.

### Severity

Scope correction only.

## 7. Correction C — Mixed-Sector Localization needs the full nonnegative legacy substructure fixed

### Problem

The draft theorem says in places that fixing only the “positive-positive” sector suffices to localize *all* remaining freedom to mixed nonzero sectors.

That is too weak if zero/nonzero cells are not separately assumed fixed: otherwise

\[
(x_0,x_-),\qquad(x_-,x_0)
\]

could still carry independent choices.

### Correct theorem hypothesis

Fix the entire embedded legacy nonnegative substructure

\[
X^{\ge0}=\{x_0\}\cup X^+,
\]

including all defined/undefined cells and values on

\[
(X^{\ge0})^2.
\]

Fix also the output reflection involution and require simultaneous reflection equivariance.

Then:

- all negative-negative cells are forced;
- all zero-negative and negative-zero cells are forced as mirrors of zero-positive and positive-zero cells;
- therefore the only remaining independent base-base reflection orbits lie in
  \[
  (X^+\times X^-)
  \cup
  (X^-\times X^+).
  \]

### Severity

Real theorem-statement correction, but the intended theorem and existing proof strategy remain valid after strengthening the hypothesis.

## 8. Role asymmetry / noncommutativity

### Verdict: PASS

For every nonzero \(x_k\), the extension has

\[
x_0\oplus x_k=x_k
\]

and

\[
x_k\oplus x_0=x_{k-\operatorname{sgn}(k)}\ne x_k.
\]

Thus legacy role asymmetry survives on both sides.

This is enough to prove noncommutativity on every root/nonzero ordered pair where both directions are defined.

## 9. Arithmetic firewall

### Verdict: PASS

The proof uses integer indices \(k\in\mathbb Z\) externally to name orbit positions. This is a coordinatization/isomorphism argument, not insertion of a primitive addition operation into FCOA.

No theorem uses a base operation

\[
Add(x,y,z)
\]

or

\[
Mul(x,y,z).
\]

The symbol \(k-\operatorname{sgn}(k)\) occurs only at the meta-coordinate level.

## 10. Priority audit — strongest nearby precedents

The exact phrase “symmetry-induced radialization of a legacy partial operation on a reversible completion of a rooted numerical ray” was not found in the present broad web/arXiv search.

However several neighboring theories are close enough that novelty language must remain conservative.

### 10.1 Reversible extensions

Kwaśniewski–Lebedev (2008), DOI `10.1070/SM2008v199n11ABEH003975`: reversible extensions of irreversible dynamical systems.

This blocks novelty claims for the ray-to-biinfinite-orbit step itself.

### 10.2 Local/restricted associativity on graded sectors

M. Cederwall and J. Palmkvist, *Tensor Hierarchy Algebras and Restricted Associativity*, Algebras and Representation Theory 28 (2025), 1369–1385, DOI `10.1007/s10468-025-10360-7`.

Their local algebras resemble \(\mathbb Z\)-graded algebras concentrated in degrees \(-1,0,1\), with products not defined on same-degree \(\pm1\) pairs and a restricted associativity law.

This is important prior art for sector-dependent/partial algebraic laws. The distinction from FCOA-Z is that the grading and local algebra are specified as algebraic input, whereas FCOA-Z studies an already-existing ray operation after minimal reversible completion of its coordinate carrier.

### 10.3 Partial associativity

W. T. Gowers and J. Long, *Partial associativity and rough approximate groups*, Geometric and Functional Analysis 30 (2020), 1583–1647, DOI `10.1007/s00039-020-00553-1`.

They study operations for which associativity holds on only a positive proportion of triples, including partially defined binary operations.

This blocks any broad novelty claim for “associativity only on some triples”. FCOA-Z must focus on **geometrically generated sector localization**, not partial associativity in general.

### 10.4 Jordan/associative pairs

O. Loos, *A structure theory of Jordan pairs*, Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. 80 (1974), 67–71, DOI `10.1090/S0002-9904-1974-13355-0`.

G. Aranda Pino and M. Siles Molina, *Associative systems of left quotients*, Journal of Algebra 294 (2005), 408–430, DOI `10.1016/j.jalgebra.2005.02.035`.

These block novelty claims for plus/minus pair systems and alternating-sector products as such.

### 10.5 Geometry-dependent commutativity in physics

Algebraic/local QFT requires local observable algebras associated with spacelike-separated regions to commute while the full operator algebra is noncommutative. This is strong conceptual prior art for geometry-conditioned commutativity, but it is not a numerical-line construction and does not invalidate the FCOA-Z theorem.

## 11. Novelty boundary after audit

### Unsafe claims

Do not claim:

- first sector-dependent algebra;
- first partial associativity theory;
- first two-sided completion of a ray;
- first geometry-dependent commutativity;
- proof that physical associativity/commutativity are non-fundamental.

### Defensible candidate contribution

The narrow candidate contribution is the proved conjunction:

1. start with a fixed role-sensitive partial algebra on a rooted one-sided coordinate ray;
2. construct the minimal reversible completion of the carrier;
3. derive the root reflection from the reversible orbit;
4. extend the legacy operation equivariantly while preserving its old restriction exactly;
5. prove that a one-sided predecessor-type zero rule becomes a two-sided **punctured radial contraction**;
6. prove that the full nonnegative legacy table plus reflection determines every same-sign and root/opposite-sign inherited cell;
7. localize all remaining independent binary base interaction freedom to genuinely mixed nonzero sectors.

No exact prior-art match for this conjunction was located in the present search.

This is a candidate novelty statement, not a legal/absolute priority certification.
