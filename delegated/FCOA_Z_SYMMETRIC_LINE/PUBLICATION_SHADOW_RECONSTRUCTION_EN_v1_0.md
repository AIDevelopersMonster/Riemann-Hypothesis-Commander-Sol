# Classical Algebra as a Resolution-Dependent Shadow of a One-Dimensional Partial Geometry

## Collapse, Matrix Units, and Reconstruction in FCOA-Z

**Version:** 1.0 publication draft  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Status:** PUBLICATION DRAFT / DOI NOT YET ASSIGNED

## Abstract

We study a one-dimensional signed partial algebra arising from the FCOA-Z programme. Its carrier is a rooted reversible line with shift \(T\), reflection \(
u\), and two legacy partial binary operations, denoted \(\oplus\) and \(\otimes\). The first result is a collapse theorem: any associative realization preserving the audited radial base cells of \(\oplus\) identifies all base coordinates. In contrast, composition of translations generated by the same source produces ordinary associative structures without collapsing the carrier. Two legacy \(\otimes\)-translations isolate the root and, after conjugation by the bilateral shift, generate a complete system of matrix units \(E_{ij}\). Their finite-support linear span is \(M_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb Z,K)\), containing \(M_n(K)\) for every finite \(n\), while the source carrier remains one-dimensional. We then prove a reconstruction-resolution theorem. The finitary matrix algebra alone forgets the line geometry, but the enriched shadow \((M_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb Z,K),U,V)\), where \(U\) and \(V\) are the linearized shift and reflection, reconstructs the rooted signed line. Nevertheless, the same generated base algebra does not reconstruct primitive-operation provenance, the distinction between undefined and terminal-valued cells, or terminal-output attachment. The canonical `share` and `split` terminal lifts give an explicit non-reconstruction witness. Thus classical algebra appears here neither as the primitive law nor as a uniformly irreversible quotient, but as a resolution-dependent shadow: sufficiently enriched shadows recover carrier geometry while still forgetting operation-level and terminal information.

## 1. Signed one-dimensional source

Let

\[
X=\{x_k:k\in\mathbb Z\},
\qquad x_k=T^k x_0,
\]

with

\[
T(x_k)=x_{k+1},
\qquad \nu(x_k)=x_{-k},
\qquad \nu T\nu=T^{-1}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The signed legacy \(\oplus\) satisfies, for every \(k\ne0\),

\[
x_0\oplus x_k=x_k,
\tag{1.2}
\]

\[
x_k\oplus x_0=x_{k-\operatorname{sgn}(k)}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

The root/root cell remains undefined. The operation \(\otimes\) contains, among other cells, two partial right translations by \(x_1\) and \(x_{-1}\) that act as identities on opposite tails together with the root.

The entire paper remains one-dimensional: no second independently iterable coordinate is introduced.

## 2. Direct associative collapse

### Theorem 2.1 — Associative Collapse

Let \((S,\cdot)\) be a semigroup and let \(f:X\to S\) preserve the defined base-valued cells (1.2)–(1.3). Then

\[
\boxed{f(x_k)=f(x_0)\quad\forall k\in\mathbb Z.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Proof

Fix \(k,j\ne0\). Associativity and the preserved cells give

\[
(f(x_k)f(x_0))f(x_j)
=f(x_k)(f(x_0)f(x_j)).
\]

Hence

\[
f(\rho(x_k))f(x_j)=f(x_k)f(x_j),
\]

where \(ho(x_k)=x_{k-\operatorname{sgn}(k)}\). Iterating toward the root yields

\[
f(x_k)f(x_j)=f(x_j).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Take \(j=1\). Since \(f(x_1)f(x_0)=f(x_0)\),

\[
f(\rho(x_k))
=f(x_k)f(x_0)
=f(x_k)(f(x_1)f(x_0))
=(f(x_k)f(x_1))f(x_0)
=f(x_1)f(x_0)
=f(x_0).
\]

Every base point is \(ho(x_k)\) for some \(k\ne0\), so all base images equal \(f(x_0)\). \(\square\)

This is a theorem about direct associative realization preserving the audited radial cells; it is not a statement that every associative construction over every enriched FCOA source collapses.

## 3. Operator classicalization without collapse

The translation group generated by \(T\) is

\[
\langle T\rangle\cong\mathbb Z,
\tag{3.1}
\]

and

\[
\langle T,\nu\rangle\cong D_\infty.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Therefore standard group-algebra constructions give

\[
K[\langle T\rangle]\cong K[t,t^{-1}],
\tag{3.3}
\]

and

\[
K[\langle T,\nu\rangle]\cong K[D_\infty].
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus associativity can appear at the level of operator composition even though direct associativization of the primitive radial value law collapses the carrier.

## 4. Root isolation from the legacy \(\otimes\) translations

Let

\[
a_+=R_{x_1}^{\otimes},
\qquad
 a_-=R_{x_{-1}}^{\otimes}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Their domains are

\[
\operatorname{dom}(a_+)=\{x_0\}\cup\{x_k:k\ge2\},
\tag{4.2}
\]

\[
\operatorname{dom}(a_-)=\{x_0\}\cup\{x_k:k\le-2\},
\tag{4.3}
\]

and both maps are identities on their domains.

### Theorem 4.1 — Root Isolation

\[
\boxed{e_0:=a_+a_-=a_-a_+=\operatorname{id}_{\{x_0\}}.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Proof

The composite of two partial identities is the identity on the intersection of their domains. The intersection in (4.2)–(4.3) is exactly \(\{x_0\}\). \(\square\)

This is the key FCOA-specific local mechanism of the paper.

## 5. Matrix units from one line

For \(i,j\in\mathbb Z\), define

\[
E_{ij}=T^ie_0T^{-j}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then \(E_{ij}\) is the singleton partial map \(x_j\mapsto x_i\).

### Theorem 5.1 — Matrix-Unit Theorem

\[
\boxed{
E_{ij}E_{kl}
=
\begin{cases}
E_{il},&j=k,\\
0,&j\ne k.
\end{cases}}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

The first map \(E_{kl}\) sends its only input \(x_l\) to \(x_k\). The second map \(E_{ij}\) is defined only at \(x_j\). Hence the composite is nonempty exactly when \(k=j\), in which case it sends \(x_l\) to \(x_i\). \(\square\)

Let

\[
I=M_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb Z,K)
:=\operatorname{span}_K\{E_{ij}:i,j\in\mathbb Z\}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For every finite \(F\subset\mathbb Z\), \(|F|=n\),

\[
\operatorname{span}_K\{E_{ij}:i,j\in F\}\cong M_n(K).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus matrix rank is unbounded while the FCOA carrier remains one-dimensional.

## 6. Pure matrix erasure

### Theorem 6.1

Every permutation \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb Z)\) induces an algebra automorphism

\[
E_{ij}\mapsto E_{\sigma(i),\sigma(j)}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Therefore \(I\) alone does not canonically determine adjacency, order, the root, or reflection.

### Proof

The matrix-unit multiplication law is invariant under simultaneous permutation of row and column indices. \(\square\)

The matrix ideal remembers local addressability but not the geometry in which the addresses were arranged.

## 7. Reconstructing the oriented line

Let \(U\) be the linearized bilateral shift. Define

\[
\mathscr C_U
=
\{p\in I:\ p\text{ primitive idempotent and }pU^np=0\text{ for all }n\ne0\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

### Theorem 7.1 — Coordinate Projector Characterization

\[
\boxed{\mathscr C_U=\{E_{kk}:k\in\mathbb Z\}.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Proof

Every primitive idempotent of \(I\) has rank one, so write \(p=v\otimes\varphi\) with finite support and \(\varphi(v)=1\). Then

\[
pU^np=\varphi(U^nv)p.
\]

The condition in (7.1) becomes

\[
\varphi(U^nv)=\delta_{n0}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Encoding \(v\) and \(\varphi\) as Laurent polynomials converts (7.3) into

\[
q(z)v(z)=1
\]

in \(K[z,z^{-1}]\). The units of the Laurent polynomial ring are monomials \(cz^m\). Hence \(v\) and \(\varphi\) each have singleton support, so \(p=E_{mm}\). The converse is immediate. \(\square\)

Conjugation

\[
p\mapsto UpU^{-1}
\]

acts by

\[
E_{kk}\mapsto E_{k+1,k+1}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Therefore \((I,U)\) reconstructs the oriented two-sided line up to global translation.

## 8. Reflection reconstructs the root

Let \(V\) be the linearized reflection. Then

\[
VE_{kk}V^{-1}=E_{-k,-k}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

### Theorem 8.1 — Rooted Signed-Line Reconstruction

The reflection action on the coordinate projectors has exactly one fixed point:

\[
\boxed{E_{00}.}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{(I,U,V)}
\]

reconstructs the pointed oriented reflected line up to unique isomorphism preserving the displayed operators.

### Proof

Equation (8.1) gives \(VE_{kk}V^{-1}=E_{kk}\) iff \(-k=k\), hence iff \(k=0\). Iterating \(U\) from the recovered root reconstructs every coordinate projector and the full signed line. \(\square\)

## 9. Primitive-operation provenance is not reconstructed

Let \(\mathcal A_{\otimes}^{base}\) be the algebra generated by \(U,U^{-1},V\), the two \(\otimes\)-tail translations, and the existing \(\otimes\) root-collapse translation.

Every base-valued \(\oplus\)-translation already belongs to this algebra:

\[
L_{x_0}^{\oplus}=I-e_0,
\tag{9.1}
\]

\[
R_{x_0}^{\oplus}=\rho
\]

is generated by the half-line projectors and \(U^{\pm1}\), and the remaining base components are matrix units \(E_{ij}\).

### Theorem 9.1 — Provenance Erasure

Adding the base-valued primitive \(\oplus\)-translations does not enlarge the generated base algebra.

Therefore the bare generated base algebra does not determine whether those operators were primitive \(\oplus\)-translations or derived from the existing \(\otimes\)-kinematic shadow.

### Proof

The formulas above place every base-valued \(\oplus\)-component in the pre-existing algebra. A comparison source with the primitive \(\oplus\) label removed has the same generated base algebra but a different primitive operation table. \(\square\)

## 10. Definedness and terminal information are separate layers

A base-only shadow cannot distinguish an undefined cell from a defined cell whose value lies in an erased terminal sort. Therefore the full primitive domain cannot be reconstructed without a definedness mask or equivalent typed incidence relation.

Likewise, the canonical `ZM0-share` and `ZM0-split` lifts have identical base translations but different terminal reflection geometry. Retaining the terminal involutive set separates them, but one must additionally retain the cell-to-terminal incidence relation to reconstruct which cell produced which terminal output.

This yields a strict reconstruction ladder:

\[
R0<R1<R2<R3<R4<R5,
\tag{10.1}
\]

where:

- \(R0=I\): local addressability only;
- \(R1=(I,U)\): oriented line up to translation;
- \(R2=(I,U,V)\): rooted signed line;
- \(R3\): primitive provenance markers;
- \(R4\): definedness and terminal geometry;
- \(R5\): terminal incidence.

## 11. Classical prior-art boundary

The isolated ingredients of the operator shadow have substantial classical relatives. Brandt semigroups provide matrix-unit systems; algebraic Toeplitz/Jacobson algebras and Leavitt path algebras exhibit finitary matrix ideals and Laurent-polynomial quotients; path/category algebras realize matrix blocks. None of those classical constructions is claimed as new here.

The FCOA-specific contribution claimed in this paper is the following conjunction:

1. the matrix-unit system is generated from the already audited one-dimensional FCOA legacy translations;
2. direct associative realization of the primitive radial law collapses the base, while operator composition does not;
3. the enriched classical shadow \((I,U,V)\) reconstructs the signed carrier geometry;
4. the same shadow provably forgets primitive-operation provenance, definedness, and terminal attachment.

## 12. Discussion

The results replace a defensive question — whether FCOA accidentally degenerates into ordinary algebra — with a sharper structural question: at what resolution does ordinary algebra appear, and what information does that resolution retain?

The answer is neither “everything is lost” nor “everything is recovered.” The finitary matrix algebra alone erases geometry; adjoining the distinguished shift restores orientation and adjacency; adjoining reflection restores the root and sign; yet primitive-law provenance and terminal information remain absent until explicitly enriched.

Thus classical algebra is a **resolution-dependent shadow** of the source. Different enrichments of the same classical algebraic layer have different reconstruction power.

## 13. Dimensional boundary

All constructions in this paper remain on the single signed coordinate line. Matrix indices \(i,j\) label source and target points of operators \(x_j\mapsto x_i\); they do not form a two-dimensional carrier. No plane, second coordinate, or higher-dimensional interpretation is used.

## 14. Conclusion

The one-dimensional FCOA-Z source exhibits a sharp separation between primitive partial law and classical operator algebra. Direct associativization collapses its base coordinates. Operator composition instead produces large classical associative structures, including matrix algebras of arbitrary finite rank. The enriched operator shadow can reconstruct the full rooted signed line, but it cannot reconstruct operation provenance or erased terminal structure. This creates a precise hierarchy of classicalization and reconstruction resolutions.

The next research stage should study the minimal operation-label and terminal-incidence enrichment required for full reconstruction, still within one dimension.
