## Abstract

We study how far the signed one-dimensional Fixed-Carrier Oriented Algebra (FCOA-Z) can reproduce formal structures reminiscent of non-Abelian anyon theory, without making a physical identification. At fixed radial level, the existing terminal output alphabet faithfully encodes the support of the multiplicity-free Ising fusion rules, including the two-channel support of `sigma x sigma = 1 + psi`. A conservative mixed-sector realization preserves every inherited FCOA cell. We prove a sequence of obstructions: primitive function-valued operations cannot intrinsically return a direct sum; terminal outputs cannot form iterated fusion trees without re-entry; collision-free configurations on a strict line have trivial braid fundamental group; and split-output reflection acts on mirror provenance rather than on the fusion-channel factor. This last distinction supplies an explicit corrigendum to an intermediate LC2 interpretation. We further prove that a single-fiber LC2 theory with unary endomorphisms cannot internally formulate the categorical pentagon without an added tensor/fusion-tree address layer. Conditional on adding that layer and choosing the Ising fusion ring, standard Tambara-Yamagami and Ising-category classification leaves two monoidal completions and four braidings on each. The FCOA-specific result is therefore a positive-negative structural boundary: FCOA-Z supports finite fusion-channel shadows, but coherent non-Abelian anyon structure requires independent cross-type channel mixing, fusion-tree/tensor composition, and coherence data.
