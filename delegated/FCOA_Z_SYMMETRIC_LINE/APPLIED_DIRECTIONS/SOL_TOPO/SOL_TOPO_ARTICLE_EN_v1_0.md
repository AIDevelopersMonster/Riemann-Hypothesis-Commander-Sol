## Abstract

We study how far the signed one-dimensional Fixed-Carrier Oriented Algebra (FCOA-Z) can reproduce structural features that, in non-Abelian anyon theory, are described by fusion channels, fusion trees, associators, and braiding. The aim is deliberately limited: no physical identification of FCOA signs, reflections, or outputs with anyonic charges is assumed.

At each fixed radial level, the three existing terminal output types `E_n^+`, `E_n^*`, and `E_n^times` form a three-symbol alphabet that can faithfully encode the **support** of the multiplicity-free Ising fusion rules. In particular, the two-channel support of `sigma x sigma = 1 + psi` can be represented by the two-element typed output fiber `{E_n^+,E_n^times}`. A conservative mixed-sector rule realizes this support without changing any inherited same-sign FCOA cell.

Three sharp obstructions then appear. First, each primitive FCOA operation remains function-valued, so one cell cannot intrinsically return a direct sum of two channels. Second, terminal outputs do not re-enter the old operation signature, so iterated fusion trees and `F`-moves are unavailable. Third, the unordered collision-free configuration space of finitely many points on a strict line is contractible; consequently one-dimensional carrier geometry has trivial braid fundamental group and cannot generate non-Abelian braid memory.

A hostile audit of an intermediate LC2 construction reveals an additional structural distinction. Split output reflection acts on **mirror provenance** `E_n^alpha <-> bar E_n^alpha`, whereas the Ising associator mixes the distinct **fusion-channel types** `E_n^+` and `E_n^times`. The two binary degrees of freedom must not be identified. This yields a block-diagonal channel-mixing no-go: reflection/provenance operators generated from the audited FCOA output structure cannot produce the Ising channel Hadamard.

Finally, we show that a single-fiber LC2 theory with unary endomorphisms cannot even formulate the categorical pentagon without adding a tensor/fusion-tree address layer. Conditional on adding that layer and choosing the Ising fusion ring, standard Tambara-Yamagami and Ising-category classification gives two monoidal completions and four braidings on each, eight braided Ising categories in total. These coherence classes are therefore additional data, not consequences of the current signed line.

The resulting resource ladder is

\[
\boxed{
\text{signed line}
<
\text{typed channels}
<
\text{provenance fiber}
<
\text{linear channel mixing}
<
\text{fusion-tree composition}
<
\text{monoidal class}
<
\text{braided class}.
}
\tag{1}
\]

The main contribution is thus a positive-negative structural boundary: FCOA-Z supports finite fusion-channel shadows, but coherent non-Abelian anyon structure requires independent compositional and coherence resources.

## 1. Scope and claim discipline

The present paper asks a formal comparison question:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Can mixed-sector FCOA interactions and typed outputs encode fusion-channel structure,}
}
\tag{2}
\]

and, if so,

\[
\boxed{
\text{what additional resources are required before }F/R\text{ coherence and braid memory become meaningful?}
}
\tag{3}
\]

The comparison is structural. We do **not** identify

- the two FCOA branches with particle/antiparticle charge;
- FCOA reflection with CPT, exchange, or braiding;
- a mixed-sign cell with annihilation;
- an output type with a physical superselection sector;
- a finite output fiber with a quantum Hilbert space unless linear structure is explicitly added.

This separation is essential because the classical theory of anyons already distinguishes fusion multiplicities, fusion spaces, associators, braidings, twists, and configuration-space topology. The goal is to locate exactly which of these ingredients are present, representable, absent, or independent in FCOA-Z.

## 2. FCOA-Z data used

The signed carrier is

\[
B^{\pm}
=
\{P_0\}
\sqcup
\{P_n^+:n\ge1\}
\sqcup
\{P_n^-:n\ge1\},
\tag{4}
\]

with reflection

\[
\nu(P_0)=P_0,
\qquad
\nu(P_n^+)=P_n^-,
\qquad
\nu(P_n^-)=P_n^+.
\tag{5}
\]

On the inherited positive ray, the terminal outputs include

\[
P_n^+\oplus P_n^+=E_n^+,
\tag{6}
\]

\[
P_n^+\otimes P_0=E_n^*\qquad(n\ge2),
\tag{7}
\]

and

\[
P_n^+\otimes P_n^+=E_n^\times\qquad(n\ge2).
\tag{8}
\]

Negative-negative cells are generated by reflection once a terminal-output reflection lift is fixed. The mixed sectors

\[
(P_i^+,P_j^-),
\qquad
(P_i^-,P_j^+)
\tag{9}
\]

are the unique base-base region not determined by the inherited positive table and simultaneous reflection.

The terminal values `E_n^alpha` are sinks in the legacy signature: there is no general rule allowing them to re-enter `oplus` or `otimes` as new arguments.

Use simple labels

\[
\mathcal I=\{1,\psi,\sigma\}

\]

with the multiplicity-free Ising fusion rules

\[
1\times a=a\times1=a,
\qquad
\psi\times\psi=1,
\qquad
\psi\times\sigma=\sigma\times\psi=\sigma,
\tag{11}

and

\[
\boxed{\sigma\times\sigma=1+\psi.}
\tag{12}

Let `N_ab^c` denote the fusion multiplicities. In particular,

\[
N_{\sigma\sigma}^{1}=N_{\sigma\sigma}^{\psi}=1.
\tag{13}
\]

Fusion support alone is not yet a fusion category: a full anyon model also needs iterated fusion spaces, `F`-moves, `R`-symbols, and pentagon/hexagon coherence.

## 4. Exact finite encoding of fusion support

Fix `n>=2` and define

\[
O_n=\{E_n^+,E_n^*,E_n^\times\}.
\tag{14}
\]

Define a bijection

\[
\chi_n(1)=E_n^+,
\qquad
\chi_n(\sigma)=E_n^*,
\qquad
\chi_n(\psi)=E_n^\times.
\tag{15}
\]

For `a,b in I`, let

\[
\Phi_n(a,b)
=
\{\chi_n(c):N_{ab}^c=1\}.
\tag{16}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — Fusion-support embedding

For all `a,b,c in I`,

\[
\boxed{
N_{ab}^c=1
\iff
\chi_n(c)\in\Phi_n(a,b).
}
\tag{17}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_n(\sigma,\sigma)=\{E_n^+,E_n^\times\}.
}
\tag{18}
\]

### Proof

Equation (16) defines the fiber as precisely the image of the nonzero fusion-support set under the bijection (15). Injectivity prevents channel collapse and surjectivity identifies every chosen FCOA terminal type with exactly one simple Ising label. Equation (18) follows from (12)-(13). `QED`.

### Limitation

The theorem encodes support only. If a target theory had `N_ab^c>1`, one symbol `E_n^alpha` would not distinguish the multiplicity basis states.

## 5. Conservative mixed-sector realization

Use the shared terminal reflection lift and define, uniformly for `n>=2`,

\[
P_n^+\oplus P_n^-=E_n^+,
\qquad
P_n^-\oplus P_n^+=E_n^+,
\tag{19}
\]

\[
P_n^+\otimes P_n^-=E_n^\times,
\qquad
P_n^-\otimes P_n^+=E_n^\times.
\tag{20}
\]

No other undefined cell is opened. For a base pair `(x,y)`, define the bundled terminal channel set

\[
\mathcal C(x,y)
=
\{\omega(x,y):\omega\in\{\oplus,\otimes\},\ \omega(x,y)\text{ is terminal}\}.
\tag{21}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — Conservative two-channel realization

The extension (19)-(20) preserves every legacy FCOA cell and simultaneous reflection, and

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C(P_n^+,P_n^-)
=
\mathcal C(P_n^-,P_n^+)
=
\{E_n^+,E_n^\times\}.
}
\tag{22}
\]

### Proof

Every newly defined cell lies in the previously open mixed sector (9); hence no inherited cell is changed. The rule is uniform in radial depth, reflection exchanges the two ordered mixed pairs, and the shared terminal lift fixes the assigned terminal values. Equation (22) follows directly from (19)-(21). `QED`.

The result is genuinely one-dimensional at the level of channel support. However, the two outputs are obtained by **bundling two primitive operation symbols**, so the construction is weaker than a single intrinsic fusion operation with two channels.

## 6. Functionality and re-entry obstructions

### Theorem 6.1 — Single-operation channel obstruction

A primitive FCOA binary operation is a partial function

\[
\omega:D_\omega\to Y.
\tag{23}
\]

Therefore no one input cell can simultaneously return two distinct terminal values. In particular, no unmodified primitive operation can realize (12) as an intrinsic direct-sum-valued fusion law.

### Proof

Functionality assigns one value to each defined pair. Two distinct values at one pair contradict the definition of a function. `QED`.

### Theorem 6.2 — Terminal-sink obstruction

With the current terminal semantics, no faithful depth-two fusion tree can be formed from legacy operations alone.

### Proof

After a first step

\[
x\,\omega\,y=E_n^\alpha,
\tag{24}
\]

a depth-two tree requires a legal next cell of the form

\[
E_n^\alpha\,\omega'\,z
\quad\text{or}\quad
z\,\omega'\,E_n^\alpha.
\tag{25}
\]

No such general re-entry is present. Hence the intermediate channel cannot participate in a second fusion step, so two parenthesizations cannot be compared by an `F`-move. `QED`.

Thus one-step fusion support is `1D-CLOSED`, while full fusion-tree semantics is blocked by LC2 output re-entry.

## 7. Strict-line braid obstruction

Let

\[
\operatorname{Conf}_m(\mathbb R)
=
\{(x_1,\ldots,x_m):x_i\ne x_j\text{ for }i\ne j\}
\tag{26}
\]

and let

\[
C_m(\mathbb R)
=
\operatorname{Conf}_m(\mathbb R)/S_m
\tag{27}
\]

be the unordered collision-free configuration space.

### Theorem 7.1 — Contractibility of unordered line configurations

For every `m>=1`,

\[
\boxed{C_m(\mathbb R)\text{ is contractible}.}
\tag{28}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{\pi_1(C_m(\mathbb R))=0.}
\tag{29}
\]

### Proof

Every unordered configuration has a unique sorted representative

\[
x_1<x_2<\cdots<x_m.
\tag{30}
\]

Hence `C_m(R)` is homeomorphic to the open chamber

\[
\Delta_m=\{(x_1,\ldots,x_m)\in\mathbb R^m:x_1<\cdots<x_m\}.
\tag{31}
\]

The chamber is convex and therefore contractible. `QED`.

In the plane, by contrast, the unordered configuration space has fundamental group the classical braid group `B_m`. Thus strict one-dimensional carrier geometry cannot generate anyonic braid topology.

### Endpoint-memory witness

For Ising anyons, two exchange channels can acquire different monodromy phases even when final particle positions and total channel labels agree. Therefore any state description that stores endpoints only, without transport/history data, loses physical braid memory.

## 8. Corrigendum to the intermediate LC2 construction

An intermediate report, `SOL_TOPO_LC2_REFLECTION_BRAID_v0_2.md`, activated a split terminal orbit

\[
E_n^\alpha\longleftrightarrow\bar E_n^\alpha
\tag{32}
\]

and correctly observed that, after free linearization, the reflection swap `J` and provenance sign `S` satisfy

\[
J^2=S^2=I,
\qquad
JS=-SJ.
\tag{33}
\]

Hence on that **provenance orbit** one obtains the local Hadamard-type duality

\[
H_{pr}=\frac{J+S}{\sqrt2}.
\tag{34}
\]

The algebraic statement (33)-(34) is retained.

The interpretation that (34) was already the Ising fusion-channel associator is withdrawn. The reason is a type mismatch.

The v0.1 fusion dictionary uses

\[
1\leftrightarrow E_n^+,
\qquad
\psi\leftrightarrow E_n^\times,
\tag{35}
\]

so the Ising channel degree of freedom is `E^+ versus E^times`. Reflection instead acts as `E^alpha versus bar E^alpha` **inside one fixed terminal type**.

The correct decomposition is therefore

\[
\boxed{
H_n\cong H_{ch}\otimes H_{pr},
}
\tag{36}
\]

where

\[
H_{ch}=\operatorname{span}\{|1\rangle,|\psi\rangle\},
\qquad
H_{pr}=\operatorname{span}\{|+\rangle,|-\rangle\}.
\tag{37}
\]

The old reflection/provenance operators act on the second factor, while the Ising associator acts on the first.

This corrigendum does not affect Theorems 4.1, 5.1, 6.1, 6.2, or 7.1.

## 9. Channel/provenance separation

On the corrected four-state space, the old output operators have the form

\[
J_{old}=I_{ch}\otimes X_{pr},
\qquad
S_{old}=I_{ch}\otimes Z_{pr}.
\tag{38}
\]

Thus any algebra generated from them, together with type-preserving scalars and projectors, is block diagonal with respect to

\[
H_n=H_n^+\oplus H_n^\times.
\tag{39}
\]

### Theorem 9.1 — Typed-sort channel-mixing obstruction

If no primitive map connects the `E^+` and `E^times` terminal sorts, then every generated output operator preserves the two channel blocks. Consequently the Ising channel Hadamard

\[
H_{ch}
=
\frac1{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
1&-1
\end{pmatrix}
\tag{40}
\]

cannot be generated by the audited reflection/provenance output algebra.

### Proof

Every allowed generator preserves terminal type. Sums and compositions of block-diagonal operators remain block diagonal. Matrix (40) has nonzero cross-channel entries, so it is outside the generated algebra. `QED`.

### Minimum new resource

A generative route to the Ising associator therefore needs at least one cross-type channel mixer, for example

\[
X_{ch}|1\rangle=|\psi\rangle,
\qquad
X_{ch}|\psi\rangle=|1\rangle.
\tag{41}
\]

No such primitive is present in FCOA-Z.

## 10. Pentagon expressibility barrier

A categorical associator is a family

\[
\alpha_{a,b,c}:(a\otimes b)\otimes c
\to
a\otimes(b\otimes c).
\tag{42}
\]

The pentagon compares composites through the five parenthesizations of a fourfold tensor product.

### Theorem 10.1 — Single-fiber LC2 cannot state the pentagon intrinsically

A theory containing only active finite fibers `H_q` and unary endomorphisms `H_q -> H_q` cannot internally formulate the pentagon equation unless a tensor/fusion-tree composition layer is added.

### Proof

The pentagon requires a binary tensor product on labels, parenthesized fusion-tree objects, tensoring of morphisms with identities, and canonical source/target matching across reassociation paths. None of these typed constructions exists in a family of unrelated fibers with unary endomorphisms only. Therefore the terms of the pentagon are not definable in that signature. `QED`.

The minimum coherence-address resource is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{labels}
+
\text{binary fusion composition}
+
\text{parenthesization addresses}
+
\text{functorial action on morphisms}.
}
\tag{43}
\]

This is compositional memory, not an additional spatial coordinate.

## 11. Conditional coherent completion

To determine what remains after adding the missing tensor layer, suppose formally that the simple-label fusion ring is exactly the Ising ring (11)-(12). The invertible sector is

\[
G=\{1,\psi\}\cong\mathbb Z_2,
\tag{44}
\]

and the fusion rules are the `Z_2` Tambara-Yamagami rules.

The Tambara-Yamagami classification uses a symmetric nondegenerate bicharacter `chi` on `G` and a scalar `tau` with

\[
\tau^2=\frac1{|G|}.
\tag{45}
\]

For `G=Z_2`, nondegeneracy forces

\[
\chi(\psi,\psi)=-1,
\tag{46}
\]

and therefore

\[
\tau=\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt2},
\qquad
\varepsilon\in\{+1,-1\}.
\tag{47}
\]

### Theorem 11.1 — Two monoidal completions

Conditional on the Ising fusion ring and a genuine tensor/fusion-tree layer, there are exactly two fusion-category equivalence classes. In a standard skeletal gauge the nontrivial channel associator has the form

\[
\boxed{
F^{\sigma\sigma\sigma}_{\sigma}
=
\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
1&-1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\varepsilon=\pm1.
}
\tag{48}
\]

This is standard Tambara-Yamagami/Ising classification, not a new FCOA theorem.

For a fixed Ising fusion category, Drinfeld, Gelaki, Nikshych, and Ostrik show that there are four braided structures. If `zeta` is the braiding eigenvalue on the trivial channel, then

\[
\zeta^2+\zeta^{-2}=\lambda,
\qquad
\lambda^2=2,
\tag{49}
\]

and the second `sigma x sigma` channel has eigenvalue `zeta^{-3}`. Across both fusion categories the braided equivalence classes are parametrized by the eight roots

\[
\boxed{\zeta^8=-1.}
\tag{50}
\]

Thus there are eight braided Ising categories in total.

The projective channel ratio is

\[
t=\zeta^{-4},
\tag{51}
\]

so (50) implies

\[
\boxed{t=\pm i.}
\tag{52}
\]

This explains why the abstract two-dimensional braid template studied in v0.2 selected `t=±i`; that calculation recovered a projective relative phase, but did not determine the full categorical braiding class.

## 12. Coherence independence theorem

### Theorem 12.1 — Minimum-resource no-go

The audited FCOA-Z line, its terminal output alphabet, split reflection, and conservative LC2 linear activation do not determine a unique pentagon- or hexagon-complete Ising structure.

More precisely:

1. without a fusion-tree/tensor layer, pentagon coherence is not internally expressible;
2. without a cross-type channel mixer, the Ising channel associator cannot be generated;
3. after the Ising fusion ring and tensor layer are supplied, pentagon coherence still leaves two inequivalent monoidal completions;
4. each monoidal completion admits four braided structures;
5. line reflection acts on provenance and does not select any of these categorical choices.

### Proof

Items 1 and 2 are Theorems 10.1 and 9.1. Items 3 and 4 are the `Z_2` Tambara-Yamagami and Ising braided-category classifications summarized in Section 11. Item 5 follows from the factor separation (36)-(38). `QED`.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\text{full non-Abelian coherence is not derivable from line completion + reflection + typed outputs alone.}
}
\tag{53}
\]

This is a no-go theorem about **generation**, not a claim that FCOA cannot host a braided fusion-category layer once that layer is added explicitly.

## 14. Hostile audit

The final manuscript was tested against the following failure modes.

### 14.1 `+/-` is not particle/antiparticle

No such physical identification is used. Mixed signs are only a convenient location for conservative new rules.

### 14.2 Multiple outputs are not automatically a quantum superposition

A finite set of typed outputs has no amplitudes, inner product, or coherent phase. Linear structure must be added explicitly.

### 14.3 Two primitive operations are not one fusion product

The construction (19)-(22) is a bundled support shadow. The operation tag preselects the output and therefore does not equal intrinsic multichannel fusion.

### 14.4 Reflection is not braiding

Strict-line configuration space has trivial fundamental group. Reflection is an involution of the carrier, not a braid generator.

### 14.5 The v0.2 provenance Hadamard is not the Ising channel `F`

This is the central corrigendum. The two matrices may have the same numerical form while acting on different typed factors.

### 14.6 The braid polynomial does not derive a full Ising category

The condition `t=±i` classifies the nontrivial projective relative phase in one abstract two-dimensional braid template. It does not fix the fusion-category sign, common braiding phase, twist, or full pentagon/hexagon data.

### 14.7 Standard classification is prior art

Tambara-Yamagami classification and the classification of Ising braided categories are used only to identify the residual coherence freedom after the FCOA-specific obstruction has been proved.

## 15. Novelty boundary

The following facts are classical and are **not** claimed as new:

- Ising fusion rules and fusion spaces;
- braid-group representations for non-Abelian anyons;
- Tambara-Yamagami classification;
- the two Ising fusion categories;
- the eight braided Ising equivalence classes;
- standard Ising `F` and `R` matrices.

The FCOA-specific contribution is the conjunction of four structural results:

1. an exact typed-output encoding of one-step Ising fusion support inside the existing FCOA terminal alphabet;
2. a conservative mixed-sector realization that preserves the signed FCOA legacy table;
3. a strict-line braid-topology obstruction and terminal-reentry obstruction;
4. a channel/provenance separation theorem plus a minimum-resource barrier showing why line reflection and split output fibers cannot generate full Ising coherence.

The paper is therefore a structural embedding/no-go note, not a proposal for a new physical anyon model.

## 16. Conclusion

At the one-step level, FCOA-Z contains enough terminal type structure to encode the support of a simple non-Abelian fusion rule:

\[
\boxed{
\sigma\times\sigma=1+\psi
\quad\rightsquigarrow\quad
\{E_n^+,E_n^\times\}.
}
\tag{54}
\]

But each attempt to promote this shadow to a coherent anyon-like theory exposes a distinct missing resource. Function-valued operations block intrinsic direct sums; terminal sinks block fusion trees; strict one-dimensional geometry blocks braid topology; reflection acts on provenance rather than fusion channel; and pentagon coherence requires a tensor/fusion-tree address layer absent from the LC2 single-fiber signature.

Once that categorical layer is supplied, standard classification shows that coherence itself still contains independent monoidal and braided choices.

The strongest valid conclusion is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{FCOA-Z supports fusion-channel shadows, but does not generate non-Abelian anyon coherence.}
}
\tag{55}
\]

The exact minimum additional architecture is

\[
\boxed{
\text{cross-type channel mixing}
+
\text{fusion-tree/tensor composition}
+
\text{monoidal/braided coherence data}.
}
\tag{56}
\]

Spatially, the conclusion remains conservative: finite internal categorical memory can be attached over a one-dimensional carrier, but genuine geometric braiding cannot be derived from the line itself.
