## 1. Executive verdict

The v0.12 theorem must be positioned conservatively.

Several of its ingredients are classical and must **not** be claimed as new:

1. words with the same letter-count vector are Parikh-equivalent / belong to the same commutative class;
2. commutative closure and Parikh images of regular/group languages are standard topics in formal-language theory;
3. the standard two-dimensional representation of \(S_3\) and its character table are classical;
4. \(S_3\) is the Weyl group of type \(A_2\), and equilateral-triangle geometry is classical;
5. three equally spaced unit vectors in \(\mathbb R^2\) form the Mercedes-Benz unit-norm tight frame with frame bound \(3/2\);
6. irreducible finite group orbits provide standard group-frame/tight-frame constructions;
7. \(\mathbb C[S_3]\cong\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C\oplus M_2(\mathbb C)\), Clifford identities in \(M_2\), and finite CAR algebra identities are standard representation/operator algebra.

The candidate contribution is narrower:

> Starting from the FCOA root-comb evaluation rule, the native reconvergence classes become binary Parikh fibers. For **every cardinality-minimal noncommuting history morphism into \(S_3\)**, the collision/co-occurrence relation between images of Parikh-equivalent histories is exactly two disjoint parity triangles \(K_3\sqcup K_3\). Passing the six collision differences through the real standard representation gives two orthogonal \(A_2\) edge frames whose union is a unit-norm tight frame for the full real standard block \(M_2(\mathbb R)\).

A preliminary literature search located the classical surrounding theories but did **not** locate this exact Parikh-collision-to-standard-block tight-frame statement.

Absence from a search is not proof of novelty. Therefore the publication may state only:

\[
\boxed{\text{“we did not locate this exact combined statement in the consulted literature.”}}
\tag{1}
\]

It must not state “first”, “new”, or “previously unknown” without a stronger bibliographic audit.

## 2. Formal-language translation: root-comb classes are Parikh fibers

For the binary alphabet

\[
\Sigma=\{L,R\},
\tag{2}
\]

define the Parikh map

\[
\Psi:\Sigma^*\to\mathbb N^2,
\qquad
\Psi(w)=(\#_L(w),\#_R(w)).
\tag{3}
\]

Two histories \(w,w'\) lie in the same root-comb class \(\mathcal W_{m,r}\) precisely when

\[
\Psi(w)=\Psi(w').
\tag{4}
\]

Thus the “permutation of root actions while preserving the endpoint” language of v0.9–v0.12 is exactly Parikh equivalence on the history alphabet.

This terminology should be adopted in the paper because it connects the construction to established formal-language theory and prevents accidental rediscovery language.

## 3. Standard surrounding result: commutative closure

The commutative closure of a word/language consists of all words with the same Parikh image as an original word. This is classical formal-language theory.

Recent work continues to study automata for commutative closures of regular languages, while older work treats commutative closure of group languages and related permutation automata.

Therefore neither the use of equal letter counts nor the existence of finite-state/group structure after shuffling letters is itself novel.

What is specific here is the exact **collision graph of a minimal non-Abelian history morphism**, and what that graph becomes in the standard matrix representation.

---

## 4. Standard surrounding result: group frames

Finite tight frames arising from group actions are well established. In particular, irreducible finite group orbits are a standard source of tight group frames.

Accordingly, a paper must not present

\[
\text{“group symmetry produces a tight frame”}
\tag{5}
\]

as the contribution.

The v0.12 frame is more specialized:

- its vectors are **differences of co-occurring history images**, not an arbitrary orbit chosen in advance;
- the edge set is generated by Parikh-fiber collisions;
- the complete collision graph turns out to be generator-orbit independent;
- the two resulting triangle edge frames occupy orthogonal parity planes and jointly reconstruct the real standard matrix block.

This provenance is the part that must carry the paper.

## 5. Standard surrounding result: Mercedes-Benz / \(A_2\) frame

A unit-norm tight frame of three equally spaced vectors in \(\mathbb R^2\) is the classical Mercedes-Benz frame. Its tight-frame constant is

\[
\frac32.
\tag{6}
\]

Likewise, the type-\(A_2\) root geometry is classical and the Weyl group is \(S_3\).

Therefore the theorem

\[
\text{“three normalized edges of an equilateral triangle form a }3/2\text{-tight frame”}
\tag{7}
\]

is background only.

The candidate contribution is that the FCOA/Parikh collision relation produces **exactly two such edge systems in orthogonal planes**, filling the real standard block.

The decompositions

\[
\mathbb R[S_3]

\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb R\oplus M_2(\mathbb R)
\tag{8}
\]

and

\[
\mathbb C[S_3]
\cong
\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C\oplus M_2(\mathbb C)
\tag{9}
\]

are classical consequences of finite-group representation theory.

The standard character values

\[
\chi_{\rm std}(e)=2,
\qquad
\chi_{\rm std}(\text{transposition})=0,
\qquad
\chi_{\rm std}(\text{3-cycle})=-1
\tag{10}
\]

are also standard.

The v0.12 use of these facts—to show that same-parity route differences vanish in both scalar blocks and that the collision family spans the matrix block—is an application, not a new representation-theory theorem.

## 7. Standard surrounding result: Clifford and CAR

Once an orthogonal pair of self-adjoint involutions in \(M_2(\mathbb C)\) is given, the corresponding Clifford and one-mode CAR formulas are standard:

\[
\mathrm{Cl}_2(\mathbb C)\cong M_2(\mathbb C),
\qquad
\operatorname{CAR}_1\cong M_2(\mathbb C).
\tag{11}
\]

Similarly,

\[
\operatorname{CAR}_n\cong M_{2^n}(\mathbb C)
\tag{12}
\]

is standard finite CAR theory.

Therefore v0.9–v0.10 can appear only as **conditional structural corollaries** showing what the special Coxeter history realization permits. They should not be the novelty headline.

## 9. Recommended abstract formulation

For publication, the robust mathematical core can be stated without FCOA terminology first, then derived from FCOA as an application.

### Abstract binary-history theorem

Let

\[
h:\{L,R\}^*\to S_3
\tag{14}
\]

be a monoid morphism whose generator images do not commute. Define \(g\sim_h g'\) iff there exist Parikh-equivalent words \(w,w'\) with

\[
h(w)=g,
\qquad
h(w')=g'.
\tag{15}
\]

Then the simple collision graph on \(S_3\) is

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_h=K_3[A_3]\sqcup K_3[S_3\setminus A_3].
}
\tag{16}
\]

In the real standard representation \(\rho:S_3\to O(2)\), the six normalized edge differences

\[
\frac{\rho(g)-\rho(g')}{\sqrt3}
\tag{17}
\]

form a unit-norm \(3/2\)-tight frame for the four-dimensional real matrix space

\[
M_2(\mathbb R)
\tag{18}
\]

with its normalized Hilbert-Schmidt inner product.

### FCOA corollary

The legacy FCOA root-comb endpoint theorem identifies its reconvergence classes with Parikh fibers, so every minimal reversible \(S_3\) history realization inherits the abstract theorem.

This ordering cleanly separates the new candidate combinatorial statement from the motivating FCOA language.

## 10. Why the abstract theorem may be publishable despite elementary ingredients

All ingredients are small and classical, but the theorem combines three usually separate viewpoints:

\[
\boxed{
\text{word combinatorics}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{finite-group collision geometry}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{operator/frame geometry}.
}
\tag{19}
\]

The theorem is finite and explicitly checkable. Its value, if any, is therefore not depth of the separate ingredients but the rigid bridge created by the collision rule.

A publication must be modest about scope and should present:

- an exact classification theorem;
- a short representation-theoretic corollary;
- FCOA as the motivating source of the Parikh fibers;
- Clifford/CAR only as optional special-realization corollaries.
