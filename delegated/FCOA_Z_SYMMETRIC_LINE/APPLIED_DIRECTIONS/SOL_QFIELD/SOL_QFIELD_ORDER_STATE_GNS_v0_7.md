## 3. All states on the full algebra

### Proposition 3.1 — General normalized positive state

Every state on \(A\) has the form

\[
\varphi(z_+,z_-,M)
=
\alpha z_+
+
\beta z_-
+
\operatorname{Tr}(DM),
\tag{25}
\]

where

\[
\alpha,\beta\ge0,
\qquad
D\ge0,
\qquad
\alpha+\beta+\operatorname{Tr}(D)=1.
\tag{26}
\]

### Proof

A positive linear functional on a finite direct sum is the sum of positive functionals on the summands. Positive functionals on a scalar block are multiplication by nonnegative numbers. Every positive functional on \(M_2(\mathbb C)\) is represented by a positive density matrix \(D\) through \(M\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(DM)\). Normalization at the identity gives (26). \(\square\)

Thus positivity alone leaves a large convex state space.

## 4. Conjugation-invariant states

The history group acts on \(A\) by inner automorphisms

\[
\operatorname{Ad}_g(a)=gag^{-1}.
\tag{27}
\]

On the scalar irreducible blocks this action is trivial. On the standard block it is

\[
M\longmapsto \rho_{\rm std}(g)M\rho_{\rm std}(g)^{-1}.
\tag{28}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — Symmetric full-state simplex

A state \(\varphi\) is invariant under all \(\operatorname{Ad}_g\), \(g\in S_3\), if and only if

\[
D=\frac\gamma2 I_2
\tag{29}
\]

for some \(\gamma\ge0\). Hence the invariant states are precisely

\[
\varphi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(z_+,z_-,M)
=
\alpha z_+
+
\beta z_-
+
\gamma\frac12\operatorname{Tr}(M),
\tag{30}
\]

with

\[
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=1.
\tag{31}
\]

### Proof

Invariance of the matrix-block functional gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(D\rho(g)M\rho(g)^{-1}\right)
=
\operatorname{Tr}(DM)
\qquad\forall M,g.
\tag{32}
\]

By cyclicity of trace,

\[
\rho(g)^{-1}D\rho(g)=D
\qquad\forall g.
\tag{33}
\]

The standard representation is irreducible, so Schur's lemma gives

\[
D=cI_2.
\tag{34}
\]

Writing \(\gamma=2c\) gives (30)-(31). Conversely such a functional is manifestly invariant. \(\square\)

### Corollary 4.2 — Reflection cannot rescue uniqueness

The FCOA generator reflection

\[
s\leftrightarrow t
\tag{35}
\]

is induced by an inner automorphism of \(S_3\). Therefore requiring reflection invariance is weaker than Theorem 4.1 and cannot reduce the full state space to a point.

### Corollary 4.3 — Even all group automorphisms do not select one state

Every automorphism of \(S_3\) is inner. Hence invariance under \(\operatorname{Aut}(S_3)\) produces exactly the same simplex (30).

Thus positivity + normalization + full history-group symmetry gives no unique global state.

## 6. Native order residue

Let

\[
a:=st,
\qquad
b:=ts.
\tag{40}
\]

The Coxeter relations imply

\[
a^3=e,
\qquad
b=a^{-1}=a^2.
\tag{41}
\]

Define

\[
\Delta=a-b.
\tag{42}
\]

Then

\[
\Delta^*=b-a=-\Delta.
\tag{43}
\]

Thus \(\Delta\) is skew-adjoint.

### Theorem 6.1 — Order Projection Identity

\[
\boxed{
\Delta^*\Delta
=
2e-a-b
=
3e_{\rm std},
}
\tag{44}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
e_{\rm std}=\frac13(2e-a-b).}
\tag{45}
\]

Moreover \(e_{\rm std}\) is a self-adjoint central idempotent.

### Proof

Using \(ab=ba=e\), \(a^2=b\), and \(b^2=a\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta^*\Delta
&=(b-a)(a-b)\\
&=ba-b^2-a^2+ab\\
&=2e-a-b.
\end{aligned}
\tag{46}
\]

The element \(a+b\) is the sum of the two 3-cycles, hence is a conjugacy-class sum and therefore central.

Put

\[
q:=2e-a-b.
\tag{47}
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
q^2
&=(2e-a-b)^2\\
&=4e+a^2+b^2-4a-4b+ab+ba\\
&=6e-3a-3b\\
&=3q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{48}
\]

Therefore \(e_{\rm std}=q/3\) satisfies

\[
e_{\rm std}^2=e_{\rm std}.
\tag{49}
\]

It is self-adjoint because \(a^*=b\). In the trivial and sign representations both 3-cycles act as \(1\), so (45) acts as zero. In the standard representation the character of a 3-cycle is \(-1\), and the central idempotent acts as the identity. Thus it is exactly the standard-block central projection. \(\square\)

### Corollary 6.2 — Order residue has no scalar component

\[
\boxed{
\Delta=e_{\rm std}\Delta=\Delta e_{\rm std}.
}
\tag{50}
\]

Hence all route-order information is supported on the standard block.

### Corollary 6.3 — Exact order-sensitive ideal

The two-sided ideal generated by \(\Delta\) is

\[
\boxed{
J_\Delta=A\Delta A=Ae_{\rm std}\cong M_2(\mathbb C).
}
\tag{51}
\]

### Proof

Since

\[
e_{\rm std}=\frac13\Delta^*\Delta,
\tag{52}
\]

we have \(e_{\rm std}\in A\Delta A\), so

\[
Ae_{\rm std}\subseteq A\Delta A.
\tag{53}
\]

Conversely \(\Delta=e_{\rm std}\Delta\), so every element of \(A\Delta A\) lies in \(Ae_{\rm std}\). \(\square\)

### Corollary 6.4 — Route-blind quotient

\[
\boxed{
A/J_\Delta
\cong
\mathbb C_{\rm triv}\oplus\mathbb C_{\rm sign}.
}
\tag{54}
\]

Thus the two scalar sectors are exactly the quotient obtained by killing the native route-order residue.
