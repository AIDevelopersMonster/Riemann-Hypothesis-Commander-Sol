## 2. What is definitely standard

### 2.1 Parikh map as monoid abelianization

Formal-language literature routinely treats the Parikh map

\[
\Psi:\Sigma^*\to\mathbb N^{|\Sigma|}
\]

as the commutative/abelianized image of a word.

Modern work on word equations also explicitly describes group abelianization as the group analogue of the Parikh map.

This conceptual analogy must therefore not be claimed as new.

### 2.2 Commutative closure and group languages

There is an established literature on commutative closure of regular languages and, more specifically, of group languages/permutation automata.

These works study questions such as:

- regularity of commutative closure;
- construction of automata recognizing the closure;
- state-complexity bounds;
- closure under shuffle and iterated shuffle.

This is highly relevant context but not the same theorem as classification of the **fibers of a fixed finite-group morphism** under Parikh-equivalent preimages.

### 2.3 Derived subgroup and abelianization

The identity

\[
G_{\rm ab}=G/[G,G]
\]

and the fact that the derived subgroup is normally generated by commutators of a generating set are elementary group theory.

The proof of the collision theorem deliberately uses only these standard facts.

### 2.4 Relative augmentation ideals

For \(N\trianglelefteq G\), the kernel

\[
\ker(kG\to k[G/N])
\]

is the classical relative augmentation ideal associated with \(N\).

Thus the algebraic identity

\[
J_P=\ker\bigl(\mathbb C[G]\to\mathbb C[G_{\rm ab}]\bigr)
\]

becomes standard once the collision relation has been identified with abelianization fibers.

### 2.5 Complete-graph edge tight frames

For each coset of \([G,G]\), the normalized difference vectors of all unordered pairs form the standard complete-graph edge frame on the zero-sum subspace.

Its frame operator follows from

\[
L(K_d)=dI-J.
\]

Hence the frame calculation itself is classical graph/Laplacian geometry.

## 4. Relevant neighboring literature

### 4.1 Commutative closure of group languages

Stefan Hoffmann, *State Complexity Bounds for the Commutative Closure of Group Languages* (2020), studies commutative closure for group languages and automata-size bounds.

This work confirms that Parikh/commutative closure over permutation-group automata is an established subject, but its main object is language recognition/state complexity rather than the derived-subgroup classification of pairwise collision fibers used here.

### 4.2 Shuffle and commutative closure

Stefan Hoffmann, *The Commutative Closure of Shuffle Expressions over Group Languages is Regular* (2020), proves regularity results for commutative closures involving group languages and shuffle operations.

Again, this is adjacent formal-language theory rather than the precise collision-equivalence theorem.

### 4.3 General commutative-closure construction

Becher, Lew Deveali, and Mollo Cunningham, *Automata for the commutative closure of regular sets* (2025/2026), give an algebraic construction of automata for commutative closures when regular.

This reinforces that the publication must position the new result as a structural finite-group fiber theorem, not as a new theory of commutative closure.

### 4.4 Parikh map and group abelianization

Current literature on word equations explicitly states that group abelianization is the group analogue of the free-monoid Parikh map.

That conceptual bridge is therefore background, while the contribution lies in proving the exact induced collision relation under a finite-group history morphism and deriving its consequences.
