## 3. Target-field definitions

Let `K` be a field of characteristic different from `2`.

A super-vector space is a vector space

\[
V=V_{\bar0}\oplus V_{\bar1}.
\tag{4}
\]

A Lie superalgebra has a bracket satisfying

\[
[\mathfrak g_{\bar p},\mathfrak g_{\bar q}]
\subseteq
\mathfrak g_{\overline{p+q}},
\tag{5}
\]

and, for homogeneous elements,

\[
[a,b]=-(-1)^{pq}[b,a]
\tag{6}
\]

plus the graded Jacobi identity.

If an associative algebra is `Z_2`-graded, the standard supercommutator is

\[
[a,b]_s=ab-(-1)^{pq}ba.
\tag{7}
\]

For `p=q=1`, (7) is an anticommutator; if at least one degree is zero, it is the ordinary commutator.

A color Lie algebra separates two pieces of data that are important here:

1. the grading group `Gamma`;
2. a commutation factor / bicharacter `epsilon: Gamma x Gamma -> K^*`.

For the ordinary Lie-super case, `Gamma=Z_2` and

\[
\epsilon(p,q)=(-1)^{pq}.
\tag{8}
\]

This separation will become an obstruction below.

## 5. Theorem A — Reflection-induced `Z_2` decomposition

### Theorem 5.1

Let

\[
V=K^{(X)}
=\operatorname{span}_K\{e_k:k\in\mathbb Z\}
\tag{9}
\]

be the free vector space on the completed FCOA axis, and extend reflection linearly by

\[
J(e_k)=e_{-k}.
\tag{10}
\]

Then `J^2=I`, and

\[
V=V_{\bar0}\oplus V_{\bar1},
\tag{11}
\]

where

\[
V_{\bar0}=\ker(J-I),
\qquad
V_{\bar1}=\ker(J+I).
\tag{12}
\]

Explicitly,

\[
V_{\bar0}=\operatorname{span}\Bigl(
 e_0,\ e_n+e_{-n}:n\ge1
\Bigr),
\tag{13}
\]

\[
V_{\bar1}=\operatorname{span}\Bigl(
 e_n-e_{-n}:n\ge1
\Bigr).
\tag{14}
\]

Hence the completed rooted line canonically induces a super-vector-space structure after free linearization.

### Proof

Equation (2) gives `nu^2=id`, hence (10) gives `J^2=I`. Since `char K != 2`, define

\[
P_{\bar0}=\frac{I+J}{2},
\qquad
P_{\bar1}=\frac{I-J}{2}.
\tag{15}
\]

They satisfy

\[
P_{\bar0}^2=P_{\bar0},
\quad
P_{\bar1}^2=P_{\bar1},
\quad
P_{\bar0}P_{\bar1}=0,
\quad
P_{\bar0}+P_{\bar1}=I.
\tag{16}
\]

Therefore every vector has a unique even-plus-odd decomposition. Applying the projections to `e_n` and `e_{-n}` gives the symmetric and antisymmetric combinations in (13)-(14), while `Je_0=e_0`. QED.

### Interpretation

This is the strongest positive result of the first target. The `Z_2` decomposition is not assigned before the axis exists. The causal order of construction is

\[
\boxed{
\text{rooted ray}
\to
\text{reversible completion}
\to
\text{derived reflection}
\to
\text{reflection eigenspaces}
\to
\mathbb Z_2\text{ grading}.
}
\tag{17}
\]

The only extra modelling step is free linearization over `K`.

## 6. Theorem B — Canonical operator superalgebra shadow

Let

\[
\mathcal A=\operatorname{End}_K(V)
\tag{18}
\]

and define

\[
\alpha(A)=JAJ.
\tag{19}
\]

### Theorem 6.1

The map `alpha` is an involutive associative-algebra automorphism and induces

\[
\mathcal A
=
\mathcal A_{\bar0}\oplus\mathcal A_{\bar1},
\tag{20}
\]

with

\[
\mathcal A_{\bar p}
=
\{A:\alpha(A)=(-1)^pA\}.
\tag{21}
\]

Moreover

\[
\mathcal A_{\bar p}\mathcal A_{\bar q}
\subseteq
\mathcal A_{\overline{p+q}}.
\tag{22}
\]

Consequently, once the standard supercommutator (7) is chosen, `A` becomes a Lie superalgebra.

### Proof

Because `J^2=I`,

\[
\alpha^2(A)=J(JAJ)J=A.
\]

Also

\[
\alpha(AB)=JABJ=(JAJ)(JBJ)=\alpha(A)\alpha(B).
\]

The same projector argument as in Theorem 5.1 yields (20)-(21). If `A` has degree `p` and `B` has degree `q`, then

\[
\alpha(AB)=(-1)^{p+q}AB,
\]

which proves (22). The supercommutator construction for an associative superalgebra then gives the graded skew law and graded Jacobi identity. QED.

### Important scope restriction

Theorem 6.1 says that FCOA-derived reflection supplies the parity automorphism for an associated operator superalgebra. It does **not** say that the original partial operation `oplus` has become the supercommutator.

## 7. Theorem C — Branch sign is not super parity

For `n>0`,

\[
e_n=\frac12\bigl((e_n+e_{-n})+(e_n-e_{-n})\bigr),
\tag{23}
\]

\[
e_{-n}=\frac12\bigl((e_n+e_{-n})-(e_n-e_{-n})\bigr).
\tag{24}
\]

Thus each individual positive or negative basis state contains both reflection parities.

The following no-go statement makes the obstruction sharper.

### Theorem 7.1 — Rooted branch-parity no-go

Suppose a super-vector space `W=W_0+W_1` contains nonzero homogeneous representatives of every FCOA point, with all positive-branch points in one parity and all negative-branch points in the other parity. Suppose also that FCOA reflection is represented by a homogeneous linear involution `N` satisfying

\[
N\iota(x_k)=\iota(x_{-k})
\tag{25}
\]

and

\[
N\iota(x_0)=\iota(x_0).
\tag{26}
\]

Then no such homogeneous `N` exists.

### Proof

If `N` is even, it preserves parity and therefore cannot map a positive homogeneous state to a negative state of the opposite parity.

If `N` is odd, it swaps parity. Let the nonzero root vector lie in `W_p`. Then `N iota(x_0)` lies in `W_{p+1}`, while (26) says it equals the same nonzero vector in `W_p`. Since the grading is a direct sum, `W_p cap W_{p+1}={0}`, contradiction. QED.

### Consequence

The prohibited shortcut

\[
\boxed{X^+=\text{even},\qquad X^-=\text{odd}}
\tag{27}
\]

is not merely unjustified; under a homogeneous rooted reflection it is impossible.

## 8. Proposition D — The super exchange factor is not forced by the line

Theorem 5.1 derives a `Z_2` grading, but a grading and a commutation factor are logically distinct.

On `Z_2` there are at least the two bicharacters

\[
\epsilon_{\mathrm{triv}}(p,q)=1,
\tag{28}
\]

and

\[
\epsilon_{\mathrm{super}}(p,q)=(-1)^{pq}.
\tag{29}
\]

Both are compatible with the same degree addition `p+q mod 2`.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{reflection-derived }\mathbb Z_2\text{ grading}
\not\Rightarrow
\epsilon(p,q)=(-1)^{pq}.
}
\tag{30}
\]

The FCOA line can generate the **carrier of parity information**, but the current core does not yet select the fermionic/super commutation factor.

This is the central obstruction to claiming that SUSY-like exchange statistics are already emergent from FCOA-Z.

## 9. Theorem E — An abstract grading loses FCOA origin data

### Theorem 9.1 — Root non-recoverability

The distinguished FCOA root cannot be recovered canonically from the abstract graded vector space `(V,V_0,V_1)` of Theorem 5.1.

### Proof

An explicit basis of the even sector is

\[
e_0,\ s_1,\ s_2,\ldots,
\qquad
s_n=e_n+e_{-n}.
\tag{31}
\]

There exists a grading-preserving linear automorphism `G` of `V` that swaps `e_0` and `s_1`, fixes the remaining displayed even basis vectors, and fixes an odd basis.

Thus `G` is an automorphism of the abstract graded vector space but does not preserve the distinguished root line `K e_0`. Any datum canonically recoverable from the grading alone must be invariant under every grading automorphism. Hence the root is not recoverable. QED.

### Corollary 9.2

The grading alone also does not recover the rooted successor dynamics or coordinate depth, because those require the missing rooted orbit structure.

Indeed the linearized shift

\[
Ue_k=e_{k+1}
\tag{32}
\]

satisfies

\[
JUJ=U^{-1}.
\tag{33}
\]

The pair `(U,J)` therefore carries the presentation

\[
J^2=I,
\qquad
JUJ=U^{-1},
\tag{34}
\]

which is the infinite-dihedral symmetry relation. The abstract `Z_2` grading retains `J` only as a parity involution and does not select `U`.

### Corollary 9.3

The abstract grading also does not encode:

- which FCOA cells are `UNDEF`;
- output sorts and terminal fibers;
- the graph of `oplus`;
- the positive legacy substructure;
- the mixed-sector freedom of Theorem 8.1 of the FCOA-Z core.

Therefore the FCOA rooted/reflected line is strictly richer than its abstract `Z_2` graded shadow.

## 11. Minimal toy model that survives the audit

Define the intrinsic orientation operator on `V` by

\[
Qe_k=\operatorname{sgn}(k)e_k,
\qquad
Qe_0=0.
\tag{38}
\]

Let `P_0` be the projector onto the root basis line:

\[
P_0 e_0=e_0,
\qquad
P_0e_k=0\quad(k\ne0).
\tag{39}
\]

Then

\[
JQJ=-Q,
\tag{40}
\]

so `Q` is odd in the operator grading, while `P_0` is even. Moreover

\[
Q^2=I-P_0.
\tag{41}
\]

Therefore its supercommutator with itself is

\[
[Q,Q]_s=2Q^2=2(I-P_0),
\tag{42}
\]

which is even.

This is an exact one-dimensional toy relation in which an odd operator generated from branch orientation has an odd-odd bracket landing in an even operator and explicitly remembers the distinguished origin through the defect `P_0`.

It is structurally reminiscent of the SUSY pattern `odd x odd -> even`, but it is **not** a supercharge algebra and `I-P_0` is not momentum.

A second useful decomposition follows from the bilateral shift. Set

\[
C=U+U^{-1},
\qquad
D=U-U^{-1}.
\tag{43}
\]

Using (33),

\[
JCJ=C,
\qquad
JDJ=-D.
\tag{44}
\]

Thus reversible transport itself splits into a reflection-even and reflection-odd channel. Again, the parity is derived from the two-sided completion rather than painted onto the original ray.

## 12. Comparison with supersymmetry

In standard supersymmetry, the even and odd generators form a Lie superalgebra; in super-Poincare examples, anticommutators of odd supercharges close on even translation generators schematically as

\[
\{Q,\bar Q\}\sim P.
\tag{45}
\]

The FCOA toy identity (42) shares only the formal grade pattern

\[
\text{odd}\times\text{odd}\to\text{even}.
\tag{46}
\]

The differences are decisive:

1. FCOA branch sign is not boson/fermion parity.
2. No spinor representation has been constructed.
3. No Poincare algebra or spacetime is present.
4. The super exchange factor is not forced by FCOA reflection.
5. The original FCOA operation `oplus` has not been identified with the operator supercommutator.
6. The FCOA root is extra pointed information absent from a bare super-space.

Hence no physical SUSY identification is licensed.
