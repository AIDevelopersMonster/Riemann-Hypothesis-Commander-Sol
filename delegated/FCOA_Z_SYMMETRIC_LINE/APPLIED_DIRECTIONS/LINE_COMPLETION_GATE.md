# FCOA-Z — Line Completion Gate

**Commander Sol:** programme director  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Status:** MANDATORY GATE BEFORE PLANE/SPACE INTERPRETATIONS

## 1. Principle

Before any serious FCOA-Z claim about a Cartesian plane, complex structure, quantization, spacetime, Hilbert geometry, or physical particles, the one-dimensional signed line must be exhausted as a mathematical object.

The central question is not whether an analogy is attractive. It is:

\[
\boxed{\text{Which currently unrealized interactions can be realized conservatively on the line?}}
\]

Here “unrealized” means either:

1. an `UNDEF` input cell of an existing partial operation;
2. an existing terminal output \(E_n^\alpha\) that currently cannot re-enter any operation;
3. a proposed mixed-sign interaction orbit not yet present in the legacy structure.

---

## 2. Conservative realization

Let \(\mathfrak F\) be the audited signed FCOA-Z structure.

A **conservative realization** of \(\mathfrak F\) is an extension \(\widehat{\mathfrak F}\) such that:

1. every old carrier and old active output element embeds injectively;
2. every previously defined operation cell keeps exactly its old value;
3. every previously undefined cell that remains undefined is still absence of value, not a new null value;
4. the embedded positive legacy ray remains an exact substructure;
5. the signed line and its root/reflection structure are preserved unless the realization explicitly belongs to a symmetry-breaking class;
6. no ordinary integer addition or multiplication is silently inserted as a primitive;
7. every newly realized cell/output is generated by a stated rule rather than a finite hand-written exception table.

The realization may add new values or re-entry laws, but it may not retroactively alter the old theory.

---

## 3. Three realization problems

### LC1 — Cell realization

Given an old `UNDEF` pair \((x,y)\), determine whether there exists an admissible rule assigning

\[
x\star y=z
\]

without violating the conservative conditions.

The answer must be one of:

- `FORCED UNDEF` — no admissible realization exists under the stated axioms;
- `REALIZABLE UNIQUE` — exactly one admissible realization class exists;
- `REALIZABLE FINITE FAMILY` — classify all classes;
- `REALIZABLE WILD` — constraints are too weak and an infinite uncontrolled family remains.

The last verdict is not a success: it means more structure is needed before interpretation.

### LC2 — Output re-entry

For each existing terminal family

\[
E^+,\qquad E^*,\qquad E^\times,
\]

ask whether elements can re-enter the algebra as legal arguments while preserving the legacy structure.

A valid re-entry law must specify:

- input and output sorts;
- definedness domain;
- reflection action;
- composition/association consequences;
- whether the result returns to the base line, remains in an output fiber, or opens a new carrier.

### LC3 — Mixed-sign realization

Classify generated laws on

\[
X^+\times X^-\qquad\text{and}\qquad X^-\times X^+.
\]

The classification must distinguish at least:

- base-valued outputs;
- terminal-fiber outputs;
- branch-swapping outputs;
- root/cancellation outputs;
- genuinely new-sort outputs.

No ordinary signed-addition law is privileged in advance.

---

## 4. No-go theorem target

For each realization class, scientific directors must attempt both directions:

### Construction direction

Produce an explicit conservative extension and prove closure/consistency.

### Obstruction direction

Prove that no extension satisfying the chosen axioms exists.

A useful no-go theorem is as valuable as a construction.

Examples of possible obstruction statements:

\[
\text{reflection-equivariance} + \text{legacy exactness} + \text{one-line closure}
\Longrightarrow \text{mixed family impossible},
\]

or

\[
\text{re-entry of }E^\alpha\text{ into the base sort}
\Longrightarrow Add\text{ or another forbidden memory layer}.
\]

These are research targets, not current theorems.

---

## 5. Meaning requirement

A mathematical realization is not complete merely because a table can be written.

Every surviving realization must answer:

\[
\boxed{\text{What invariant or structure does the newly realized object encode?}}
\]

Acceptable mathematical meanings include, for example:

- radial depth relation;
- orientation/sign relation;
- transport state;
- path memory;
- output channel/fusion class;
- local compatibility relation;
- second coordinate candidate;
- phase or grade state.

A physical interpretation may be proposed only after the mathematical meaning is fixed.

---

## 6. One-dimensional closure test

Before promoting any \(E\)-family to a new dimension, test whether it can be fully represented inside a one-dimensional enriched line.

Three outcomes are distinguished:

### 1D-CLOSED

All new states/interactions can be represented as finite/internal fibers over the line; no independent coordinate is required.

### 1D-OBSTRUCTED

No conservative one-dimensional realization exists under the accepted axioms.

### DIMENSION-FORCING

Every nontrivial conservative realization necessarily introduces an independently iterable transport direction or a second unbounded coordinate.

Only the third outcome licenses the claim that an \(E\)-output is forcing an emergent second dimension.

---

## 7. Publication rule

The future plane/space paper may not say “\(E\)-outputs become a second dimension” unless a DIMENSION-FORCING theorem or an explicitly constructed 2D realization is proved.

Until then:

\[
\boxed{E\text{-outputs are typed transverse candidates, not spatial dimensions.}}
\]

## 8. Gate to later programmes

The following programmes are deliberately locked behind this line-completion gate:

- FCOA-CARTESIAN — independent coordinate directions and product geometry;
- FCOA-COMPLEX — orientation/rotation/phase analogues of \(a+bi\);
- FCOA-QUANTIZED — discrete spectra, compatible partial sums, state/channel structure;
- FCOA-SPACE — three or more independent generated directions.

They may collect analogies now, but they may not promote structural claims until LC1–LC3 have been classified or a precise obstruction is proved.
