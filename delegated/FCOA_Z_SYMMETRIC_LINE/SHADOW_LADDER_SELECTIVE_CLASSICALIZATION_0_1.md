## 1. Why this note exists

The FCOA programme no longer treats the appearance of ordinary groups, rings, or associative algebras as a failure mode.

The correct question is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Which classical structure appears after which FCOA information is deliberately forgotten?}
}
\]

This note proves a first selective-classicalization theorem and introduces a precise replacement for the informal phrase “kernel of information loss”.

The guiding picture is

\[
\text{rich partial geometry}
\longrightarrow
\text{kinematic reduct}
\longrightarrow
\text{operator closure}
\longrightarrow
\text{ordinary algebra}.
\]

The arrows are generally irreversible on isomorphism classes.

## 2. Source class

Fix the signed-line core

\[
C_0=(X,x_0,T,\nu)
\]

with

\[
X=\{x_k:k\in\mathbb Z\},
\qquad
T(x_k)=x_{k+1},
\qquad
\nu(x_k)=x_{-k},
\]

so that

\[
\nu^2=1,
\qquad
\nu T\nu=T^{-1}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Let \(\mathrm{Exp}(C_0)\) denote the class of FCOA-Z expansions of this core obtained by adjoining any of the allowed legacy partial operations, mixed-sector extensions, and typed output families, while leaving \(C_0\) unchanged.

Examples include the already constructed `ZM0-share` and `ZM0-split` lifts.

---

## 3. Erasure operators

For \(F\in\mathrm{Exp}(C_0)\), define two reducts.

### 3.1 Translation reduct

\[
U_T(F):=(X,T).
\]

This forgets, at minimum:

- the distinguished reflection \(\nu\);
- all legacy partial operations;
- all typed \(E\)-output sorts;
- all mixed-sector value laws;
- all role asymmetry carried only by those operations.

### 3.2 Dihedral reduct

\[
U_D(F):=(X,T,\nu).
\]

This retains the reversible signed-line kinematics but forgets all additional operation tables and output fibers.

## 4. Operator shadows

Define

\[
\mathcal S_T(F):=\langle T\rangle\le\operatorname{Sym}(X),
\]

and

\[
\mathcal S_D(F):=\langle T,\nu\rangle\le\operatorname{Sym}(X).
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — Selective Classicalization of the signed line

For every \(F\in\mathrm{Exp}(C_0)\),

\[
\boxed{\mathcal S_T(F)\cong\mathbb Z}
\tag{4.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\mathcal S_D(F)\cong D_\infty\cong\mathbb Z\rtimes C_2.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

These isomorphism types are independent of all additional FCOA operations and output-fiber choices.

### Proof

Equation (4.1) follows because the map

\[
n\mapsto T^n
\]

is surjective onto \(\langle T\rangle\), and \(T^n=1\) implies

\[
x_n=T^n(x_0)=x_0,
\]

hence \(n=0\).

For (4.2), relation (2.1) gives the standard infinite-dihedral presentation

\[
\langle T,\nu\mid \nu^2=1,\ \nu T\nu=T^{-1}\rangle.
\]

Every word reduces to \(T^n\) or \(T^n\nu\), and the signed-line action shows these normal forms are distinct. \(\square\)

## 5. Ring and associative-algebra shadows

Let

\[
G_T:=\mathcal S_T(F)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — Integer ring shadow

As a unital ring,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{End}(G_T)\cong\mathbb Z.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Proof

Every endomorphism of the infinite cyclic group is uniquely determined by

\[
T\mapsto T^n
\]

for some \(n\in\mathbb Z\). Pointwise group multiplication adds the integers and composition multiplies them. Thus the endomorphism ring is the ordinary integer ring. \(\square\)

> **Important correction.** The ring in (5.1) is \(\operatorname{End}(\langle T\rangle)\), not \(\operatorname{End}(D_\infty)\).

Let \(K\) be a field.

### Theorem 5.2 — Associative group-algebra shadows

\[
\boxed{K[\mathcal S_T(F)]\cong K[t,t^{-1}]}
\tag{5.2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{K[\mathcal S_D(F)]\cong K[D_\infty].}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Both are ordinary associative unital \(K\)-algebras.

### Proof

A group algebra is associative because its multiplication is the bilinear extension of associative group composition. Since \(\mathcal S_T(F)\cong\mathbb Z\), its basis elements correspond to \(t^n\), giving the Laurent polynomial algebra. Equation (5.3) is immediate from (4.2). \(\square\)

## 6. Direct associativization versus shadow associativity

The previously proved Associative Saturation Collapse Theorem states that any semigroup-valued map preserving the defined base cells

\[
x_0\oplus x_k=x_k,
\qquad
x_k\oplus x_0=\rho(x_k)
\qquad(k\ne0)
\]

must identify every base point:

\[
f(x_k)=f(x_0)
\qquad\forall k\in\mathbb Z.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Hence there are two sharply different operations:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{force the legacy value law itself to be associative}
&\Rightarrow\text{coordinate collapse},\\[2mm]
\text{compose operators generated by the geometry}
&\Rightarrow\text{non-collapsing associative shadow}.
\end{array}}
\tag{6.2}
\]

This distinction is the first rigorous form of the claim that classical associativity may occur at a coarser structural level without being the primitive law of the FCOA carrier.

## 7. A general translation-category shadow

The operator-shadow mechanism is not restricted to the distinguished shift \(T\).

Let \(F\) be any many-sorted partial FCOA structure. For every primitive binary operation \(\star\), every fixed admissible second argument \(a\), and every pair of source/target sorts for which the value is defined, form the partial right translation

\[
R_a^\star:x\mapsto x\star a.
\]

Similarly form every partial left translation

\[
L_a^\star:x\mapsto a\star x.
\]

Include the primitive unary structural maps such as \(T\), \(T^{-1}\), and \(\nu\) when present.

Let \(\mathcal T(F)\) be the small category generated by these partial maps and identity maps, with composition whenever codomain and domain sorts match.

### Theorem 7.1 — Translation-Category Associativization

\[
\boxed{\mathcal T(F)\text{ is an ordinary category, hence its composition is associative}.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

For any field \(K\), the finite-support category algebra

\[
\boxed{K[\mathcal T(F)]}
\tag{7.2}
\]

is an ordinary associative \(K\)-algebra.

### Proof

Partial maps compose as ordinary functions on the subset where the composite is defined. Function composition is associative. Sorting only restricts which composites are legal and therefore produces a category rather than a one-object semigroup.

The category algebra is the free \(K\)-vector space on the morphisms of \(\mathcal T(F)\), with product equal to categorical composition for composable basis arrows and zero otherwise. Associativity follows directly from associativity of composition in \(\mathcal T(F)\). \(\square\)

### Consequence

Every FCOA admits at least one completely standard associative operator shadow without requiring the primitive FCOA operations themselves to become associative.

This theorem is mathematically generic; it is not claimed as an FCOA novelty. The FCOA-specific problem is to determine which geometry and output information survives in \(\mathcal T(F)\) and its algebra.

## 8. Shadow collisions: a precise information-loss relation

The phrase “kernel of information loss” is useful heuristically but is not automatically an algebraic kernel.

For any shadow construction \(\mathcal S\), define its **collision relation** on source isomorphism classes by

\[
F\equiv_{\mathcal S}F'
\iff
\mathcal S(F)\cong\mathcal S(F').
\tag{8.1}
\]

Large \(\equiv_{\mathcal S}\)-classes mean that the shadow has low resolving power.

### Theorem 8.1 — Share/Split collision

Let \(F_{share}\) and \(F_{split}\) be the established `ZM0-share` and `ZM0-split` signed lifts over the same signed-line core.

Then

\[
F_{share}\not\cong F_{split}
\tag{8.2}
\]

as typed reflection structures, but

\[
F_{share}\equiv_{\mathcal S_T}F_{split}
\tag{8.3}
\]

and

\[
F_{share}\equiv_{\mathcal S_D}F_{split}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Consequently they also collide under

\[
\operatorname{End}(\mathcal S_T(-)),
\quad
K[\mathcal S_T(-)],
\quad
K[\mathcal S_D(-)].
\]

### Proof

The two structures differ in output-reflection geometry: split outputs occur in nontrivial reflection pairs, while shared outputs are identified across reflection. Hence the typed reflection structures are nonisomorphic.

Their base operators \(T\) and \(\nu\) are identical. Therefore the operator groups, endomorphism ring of \(\langle T\rangle\), and group algebras listed above are identical up to canonical isomorphism. \(\square\)

## 9. Non-reconstruction theorem

### Theorem 9.1 — No inverse from the bare classical shadow

Let \(\mathcal S\) be any one of the bare shadows in Theorem 8.1. On any source class containing both \(F_{share}\) and \(F_{split}\), there is no assignment on shadow isomorphism classes

\[
\mathcal R:[\operatorname{im}\mathcal S]\to[\mathrm{Exp}(C_0)]
\]

such that

\[
\mathcal R([\mathcal S(F)])=[F]
\tag{9.1}
\]

for every source \(F\) in that class.

### Proof

By Theorem 8.1,

\[
[\mathcal S(F_{share})]=[\mathcal S(F_{split})].
\]

Equation (9.1) would force the single left-hand side to equal both

\[
[F_{share}]
\quad\text{and}\quad
[F_{split}],
\]

contradicting (8.2). \(\square\)

### Clarification

This does not forbid selecting **some** lift of a classical shadow. It forbids recovering the actual richer source uniquely from the bare shadow.

## 10. What is and is not proved about “forgetful functors”

The present theorem package deliberately avoids three categorical overclaims.

### 10.1 Object collision does not imply non-faithfulness

A functor can be faithful on morphisms while sending nonisomorphic objects to isomorphic reducts. Therefore the share/split collision alone does **not** prove that a future categorical shadow functor is non-faithful.

### 10.2 “Exact” is not currently meaningful

Exactness belongs to categories with suitable exact/additive structure. No such category of FCOA objects has yet been defined. Therefore the statement “the FCOA shadow functor is not exact” is currently undefined, not proved.

### 10.3 “Epimorphism” or “quotient” is not automatic

Our source and target are different kinds of structures. Until a category and a morphism \(F\to\mathcal S(F)\) are formally specified, the safe terms are **shadow**, **derived construction**, and **information-losing projection on isomorphism classes**, not categorical epimorphism.

## 11. Why the present results are not PBW or Tannaka theorems

The analogy with universal enveloping algebras is motivational only.

The construction

\[
\langle T\rangle\mapsto\operatorname{End}(\langle T\rangle)
\]

is not a PBW theorem and is not the universal enveloping algebra of the partial operation `oplus`.

Likewise, a Tannakian reconstruction theorem requires substantial additional structure: a suitable rigid tensor category of representations together with a fiber functor. The bare equality of operator shadows for `share` and `split` does not provide these data.

Therefore no Tannakian reconstruction result is claimed here.

---

## 12. Why the present results are not yet an H^2-classification

Classical group cohomology \(H^2(G,A)\) classifies extensions

\[
1\to A\to E\to G\to1
\]

with an abelian kernel \(A\) and a fixed induced \(G\)-module action, up to equivalence of extensions.

The current `ZM0-share` and `ZM0-split` output fibers have not yet been shown to form such group extensions of \(D_\infty\).

Hence no statement of the form

\[
\{\text{FCOA lifts}\}\cong H^2(D_\infty,A)
\]

is justified at present.

This is a legitimate future test, not a current theorem.

## 13. The selective-classicalization principle

The proved results can now be summarized without defensive language.

### Principle

A richer FCOA structure may admit ordinary classical algebras after a specified erasure of source data and a standard closure construction.

For the present signed-line core:

\[
\boxed{
F
\xrightarrow{\text{erase legacy operations/fibers}}
(X,T)
\xrightarrow{\text{operator closure}}
\mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{End}}
\mathbb Z_{ring}
}
\tag{13.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
F
\xrightarrow{\text{retain }T,\nu}
D_\infty
\xrightarrow{K[-]}
K[D_\infty].
}
\tag{13.2}
\]

More generally,

\[
\boxed{
F
\xrightarrow{\text{partial translations}}
\mathcal T(F)
\xrightarrow{K[-]}
K[\mathcal T(F)].
}
\tag{13.3}
\]

Thus the programme no longer needs to avoid classical algebra. It needs to record the exact **price of classicalization**: which source distinctions were erased before the classical law appeared.

## 14. Current reconstruction target

The next theorem should not immediately attempt full Tannakian reconstruction.

The minimal target is sharper and easier to audit:

\[
\boxed{
\text{What is the least enrichment of the bare shadow that separates }F_{share}\text{ from }F_{split}?
}
\]

Candidate enrichments, in increasing strength, are:

1. the permutation action of \(D_\infty\) on the full typed universe rather than the abstract group alone;
2. a \(D_\infty\)-set of active output fibers;
3. the permutation module \(K[E]\) induced by that action;
4. a graded/category-algebra representation retaining output sorts;
5. only if required, a monoidal representation category with an explicit fiber functor.

The first enrichment that distinguishes `share` from `split` is the correct next rung of the Shadow Ladder.

## 15. Publication boundary

Already proved and publication-safe after hostile audit:

1. direct associative/commutative preservation collapses the signed base;
2. translation and dihedral operator shadows do not collapse it;
3. these shadows generate ordinary groups, the integer endomorphism ring, and ordinary associative group algebras;
4. `share` and `split` are nonisomorphic richer sources with identical bare operator shadows;
5. therefore the bare classical shadows do not uniquely reconstruct the source;
6. every many-sorted partial FCOA has an associative translation-category algebra shadow.

Not yet proved:

- that all classical algebras arise from FCOA;
- that FCOA is a universal source category for ordinary algebra;
- that `share/split` are classified by group cohomology;
- that their module categories are inequivalent;
- any Tannakian reconstruction theorem;
- any physical coarse-graining or renormalization interpretation.

The mathematical programme is now to replace each of those speculative statements by either a theorem or a no-go result.
