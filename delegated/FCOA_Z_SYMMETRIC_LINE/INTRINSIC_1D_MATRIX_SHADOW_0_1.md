## 3. Two legacy tail idempotents

Define the partial right translations

\[
a_+:=R_{x_1}^{\otimes},
\qquad
 a_-:=R_{x_{-1}}^{\otimes}.
\tag{3.1}

\[

\tag{3.2}

\[
\operatorname{dom}(a_-)
=
\{x_0\}\cup\{x_k:k\le-2\}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

On their domains both maps are the identity.

Hence

\[
a_+^2=a_+,
\qquad
 a_-^2=a_-.
\tag{3.4}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — Root Isolation Theorem

The composite

\[
\boxed{e_0:=a_+a_-}
\tag{3.5}
\]

is the singleton partial identity

\[
\boxed{e_0=\operatorname{id}_{\{x_0\}}.}
\tag{3.6}

The same map is obtained from \(a_-a_+\).

### Proof

Since both \(a_+\) and \(a_-\) are identity maps on their domains, their composite is the identity on the intersection of those domains. By (3.2)–(3.3),

\[
\operatorname{dom}(a_+)\cap\operatorname{dom}(a_-)
=\{x_0\}.
\]

Therefore

\[
a_+a_-=a_-a_+=\operatorname{id}_{\{x_0\}}.
\]

\(\square\)

### Interpretation

The old `otimes` table already contains enough one-dimensional information to isolate the distinguished root by pure composition of existing translations.

## 5. Matrix units from the line

For \(i,j\in\mathbb Z\), define the partial transformation

\[
\boxed{
E_{ij}:=T^i e_0T^{-j}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

It has singleton domain and image:

\[
E_{ij}:\{x_j\}\to\{x_i\},
\qquad
E_{ij}(x_j)=x_i.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Let \(0_{\emptyset}\) denote the empty partial transformation.

### Theorem 5.1 — Infinite Matrix-Unit Theorem

For all \(i,j,k,l\in\mathbb Z\),

\[
\boxed{
E_{ij}E_{kl}
=
\begin{cases}
E_{il},&j=k,\\
0_{\emptyset},&j\ne k.
\end{cases}}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Moreover

\[
E_{ii}=e_i.
\tag{5.4}
\]

### Proof

The map \(E_{kl}\) is defined only at \(x_l\) and sends it to \(x_k\). The map \(E_{ij}\) is defined only at \(x_j\).

If \(k=j\), the output of \(E_{kl}\) lies in the domain of \(E_{ij}\), and the composite sends

\[
x_l\mapsto x_k=x_j\mapsto x_i,
\]

which is precisely \(E_{il}\).

If \(k\ne j\), the output \(x_k\) of the first map is outside the singleton domain \(\{x_j\}\) of the second, so the composite has empty domain.

Finally \(E_{ii}\) is the identity on \(\{x_i\}\). \(\square\)

### Corollary 5.2 — Brandt matrix-unit shadow

The partial transformation semigroup generated by the existing FCOA-Z operators contains

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal B_{\mathbb Z}
=
\{0_{\emptyset}\}
\cup
\{E_{ij}:i,j\in\mathbb Z\},
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

with the standard multiplication law of an infinite system of matrix units.

## 10. One-ended coarse algebra without reflection

Let \(D\) be the diagonal algebra generated by all \(P_m\) and the identity.

### Lemma 10.1

\(D\) consists exactly of the diagonal functions

\[
f:\mathbb Z\to K
\]

that are eventually constant at both \(+\infty\) and \(-\infty\).

Let \(D_{fin}\subset D\) be the finite-support diagonal ideal. Then

\[
\boxed{
D/D_{fin}\cong K\oplus K,
}
\tag{10.1}
\]

where the two coordinates are the limiting values at \(-\infty\) and \(+\infty\).

### Proof

A finite linear combination of half-line characteristic functions has only finitely many jumps, hence is eventually constant on both tails. Conversely, every two-ended eventually constant sequence differs from a linear combination of \(I\) and one tail projector by a finitely supported sequence, and every finitely supported diagonal is a finite linear combination of the singleton projectors (9.3).

The quotient therefore remembers only the two eventual constants. \(\square\)

Let

\[
\mathcal A_{end}:=\langle D,U,U^{-1}\rangle_K
\subseteq\operatorname{End}_K(V_K).
\]

The shift preserves the two end limits, so its induced action on \(D/D_{fin}\) is trivial.

### Theorem 10.2 — Two-End Laurent Quotient

The finitary matrix algebra is a two-sided ideal in \(\mathcal A_{end}\), and

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_{end}/M_{fin}(\mathbb Z,K)
\cong
K[t,t^{-1}]\oplus K[t,t^{-1}].
}
\tag{10.2}
\]

### Proof

Conjugation by \(U\) shifts matrix units and therefore preserves \(M_{fin}\). Multiplying a finitary matrix by a diagonal element of \(D\) on either side remains finitary, so \(M_{fin}\) is an ideal.

Modulo finitary diagonal differences, the diagonal algebra is \(K\oplus K\) by Lemma 10.1. Translation acts trivially on these two end values. Therefore the quotient is

\[
(K\oplus K)\otimes_K K[t,t^{-1}],
\]

which is the displayed direct sum of two Laurent polynomial algebras. \(\square\)

### Interpretation

The quotient forgets every finite local feature but retains the two asymptotic ends and translation along the line.

## 11. Reflection converts the two ends into a 2x2 Laurent algebra

Adjoin the reflection operator \(V\). It satisfies

\[
VUV=U^{-1},
\tag{11.1}
\]

and interchanges the two end projections.

Let

\[
\mathcal A_{sym}:=\langle \mathcal A_{end},V\rangle_K.
\]

The finitary matrix ideal remains reflection invariant by (7.4).

### Theorem 11.1 — Reflected Two-End Quotient

There is a natural isomorphism

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_{sym}/M_{fin}(\mathbb Z,K)
\cong
M_2(K[t,t^{-1}]).
}
\tag{11.2}
\]

### Proof

Put

\[
L:=K[t,t^{-1}].
\]

By Theorem 10.2, before adjoining reflection the quotient is

\[
L\oplus L.
\]

Reflection acts by

\[
\sigma(f,g)=(\iota(g),\iota(f)),
\qquad
\iota(t)=t^{-1}.
\tag{11.3}
\]

Hence the reflected quotient is the crossed product

\[
(L\oplus L)\rtimes_\sigma C_2.
\tag{11.4}
\]

Define

\[
\Phi(f,g)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
f&0\\
0&\iota(g)
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\Phi(s)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{11.5}
\]

where \(s\) is the nontrivial element of \(C_2\).

Then

\[
\Phi(s)\Phi(f,g)\Phi(s)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\iota(g)&0\\
0&f
\end{pmatrix}
=
\Phi(\sigma(f,g)).
\]

Thus \(\Phi\) respects the crossed-product relation. Diagonal elements give arbitrary diagonal matrices over \(L\), and multiplication by \(\Phi(s)\) gives arbitrary off-diagonal entries. Hence \(\Phi\) is onto \(M_2(L)\). Both sides decompose into four copies of \(L\) as \(K\)-vector spaces, so \(\Phi\) is injective. \(\square\)

### Meaning

The factor \(2\) here is not a second spatial coordinate. It is the algebraic memory of the **two ends of one line**.

## 13. Simple quotients versus embedded matrix corners

Theorem 6.1 gives matrix algebras of every finite rank as **subalgebras/corners**.

That does not imply matrix algebras of every rank occur as **simple quotients** of the full one-dimensional end algebra.

The distinction can be proved exactly.

### Lemma 13.1 — Finite-dimensional representations kill local jumps

Let

\[
\pi:\mathcal A_{end}\to M_d(K)
\]

be a finite-dimensional representation.

Then

\[
\boxed{
\pi(P_m)=\pi(P_{m+1})
\quad\text{for every }m,
}
\tag{13.1}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\pi(e_m)=0
\quad\text{for every }m.
}
\tag{13.2}
\]

### Proof

All \(P_m\) are conjugate by powers of the invertible matrix \(\pi(U)\), so they have equal rank.

Equation (9.2) gives

\[
P_mP_{m+1}=P_{m+1}=P_{m+1}P_m.
\]

Thus the commuting idempotent \(P_{m+1}\) is a sub-idempotent of \(P_m\). Equal finite rank forces equality.

Then (9.3) gives

\[
\pi(e_m)=\pi(P_m-P_{m+1})=0.
\]

\(\square\)

### Corollary 13.2

Every finite-dimensional representation kills

\[
M_{fin}(\mathbb Z,K).
\]

Therefore every finite-dimensional representation of \(\mathcal A_{sym}\) factors through

\[
M_2(K[t,t^{-1}]).
\]

### Theorem 13.3 — Persistent Simple-Quotient Ceiling

Assume \(K\) is algebraically closed. Every finite-dimensional simple quotient of \(\mathcal A_{sym}\) is isomorphic to

\[
\boxed{M_2(K).}
\tag{13.3}
\]

(up to the degenerate one-dimensional quotients obtainable after collapsing the reflected end action).

In particular there is no simple quotient

\[
M_n(K),\qquad n\ge3.
\tag{13.4}
\]

### Proof

By Corollary 13.2 any finite-dimensional simple quotient factors through

\[
M_2(L),
\qquad
L=K[t,t^{-1}].
\]

A finite-dimensional simple quotient of \(M_2(L)\) is

\[
M_2(L/\mathfrak m)
\]

for a maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\subset L\). Since \(K\) is algebraically closed, every maximal ideal of \(K[t,t^{-1}]\) has the form

\[
(t-\lambda),
\qquad
\lambda\in K^\times,
\]

and

\[
L/\mathfrak m\cong K.
\]

Hence the nondegenerate simple matrix quotient is \(M_2(K)\). \(\square\)

### Central distinction

We therefore obtain simultaneously

\[
\boxed{
\text{embedded matrix rank is unbounded}
}
\tag{13.5}
\]

but

\[
\boxed{
\text{finite-dimensional simple quotient rank remains }2.
}
\tag{13.6}
\]

This is a genuine one-dimensional classicalization dichotomy.

Within the typed translation/category algebra,

\[
\boxed{N_E^2=0.}
\tag{15.1}
