# FCOA Hybrid Memory — Synergy Classes

**Status:** internal research checkpoint; not upstreamed.

## 1. Group-intersection principle

For any two reducts on the same active carrier `X`,

\[
\operatorname{Aut}(\oplus,\otimes)
=
\operatorname{Aut}(\oplus)
\cap
\operatorname{Aut}(\otimes)
\]

as subgroups of `Sym(X)`, with output actions forced by the corresponding domain/value structures.

Thus a balanced hybrid witness is exactly a pair of proper nontrivial symmetry groups with trivial intersection, subject to the stronger FCOA requirement that the groups arise from explicit partial-operation data rather than arbitrary named relations.

## 2. Three synergy classes

### DD — domain-domain synergy

Both operations can be constant-valued. Joint rigidity is caused solely by incompatible definedness geometries:

\[
\operatorname{Aut}(D_\oplus)\neq1,
\quad
\operatorname{Aut}(D_\otimes)\neq1,
\quad
\operatorname{Aut}(D_\oplus,D_\otimes)=1.
\]

The minimal witness is DD-3 from `MINIMAL_WITNESSES.md`.

### DV — domain-value synergy

One operation contributes a nontrivial domain stabilizer and the other contributes a value-fiber stabilizer strictly smaller than its definedness group. A clean signature is

\[
\operatorname{Aut}(D_\otimes)
\supsetneq
\operatorname{Aut}(\otimes),
\]

while the joint domain reduct is still nonrigid but restoring values makes the pair rigid.

The minimal clean witness is DV-3.

### VV — value-value synergy

The pair of operation domains remains highly symmetric, but the two value partitions have trivial common stabilizer:

\[
\operatorname{Aut}(D_\oplus,D_\otimes)
\gg1,
\]

while

\[
\operatorname{Aut}(\oplus)\cap\operatorname{Aut}(\otimes)=1.
\]

The minimal clean witness is VV-3.

## 3. Scalable path family

For every `n>=3`, let

\[
X_n=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}.
\]

Let `P` be the undirected path

\[
0-1-2-\cdots-(n-1),
\]

and let `Q` be the undirected path in the vertex order

\[
1-0-2-3-\cdots-(n-1).
\]

Each path has automorphism group `C_2`, generated by reversal of its own displayed order.

The two reversal permutations are distinct, and for every `n>=3` their generated order-two subgroups intersect trivially. Therefore constant-valued partial operations whose domains are the directed-symmetric edge sets of `P` and `Q` satisfy

\[
\operatorname{Aut}(\oplus)\cong C_2,
\qquad
\operatorname{Aut}(\otimes)\cong C_2,
\qquad
\operatorname{Aut}(\oplus,\otimes)=1.
\]

This gives a DD family for all carrier sizes.

### Explicit checks

For `n=3,4,5`, direct exhaustive permutation checks give group sizes

\[
(2,2,1)
\]

for `(Aut(oplus), Aut(otimes), Aut(joint))` in every case.

## 4. Scalable DV/VV lift

The same pair of path stabilizers can be realized through value partitions rather than domains.

Take the complete active binary domain `X_n^2` and color cells by a two-block anonymous partition whose stabilizer on `X_n` is exactly the reversal group of `P`; do the analogous construction for `Q`. The Fiber-Transport theorem then transfers the problem to the common stabilizer of the two partitions.

This yields scalable value-value witnesses with

\[
C_2\cap C_2=1.
\]

For minimal finite examples, however, the diagonal `1+2` partitions on three active points are strictly cleaner and use fewer cells.

## 5. Structural lesson

Hybrid memory does not require either reduct to approach rigidity individually. It requires only *transverse residual symmetries*.

The correct organizing invariant is therefore not merely

\[
|\operatorname{Aut}(\star)|,
\]

but the position of the reduct groups inside the same ambient permutation group:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Hybrid gain}
\sim
\operatorname{Aut}(\oplus)\cap\operatorname{Aut}(\otimes).
}
\]

Two reducts of equal group size can behave completely differently jointly depending on whether their residual subgroups coincide or are transverse.

## 6. Candidate invariant

For finite active carrier `X`, define provisionally

\[
\operatorname{HRI}(\oplus,\otimes)
=
\frac{|\operatorname{Aut}(\oplus)|\,|\operatorname{Aut}(\otimes)|}
{|\operatorname{Aut}(\oplus,\otimes)|}.
\]

This is **not yet recommended for upstream use**: it is only a bookkeeping quantity and is not normalized against `|Sym(X)|`. The more robust invariant is the subgroup-intersection profile itself, together with the domain/value source of each stabilizer.

## 7. Upstream-worthy theorem candidate

A sharper theorem candidate, subject to hostile audit, is:

> Balanced hybrid rigidity is equivalent to the existence of two nontrivial partial-operation stabilizers with trivial common carrier stabilizer; carrier size three is minimal, and all three FCOA mechanisms DD, DV and VV already occur at that minimum.

The novelty, if any, would be in the FCOA-specific classification and minimal resource accounting, not in the abstract group-intersection observation.
