# FCOA Rigidity Cost — Multicolor Arity Threshold

**Direction:** FCOA — SOL-RIGIDITY — Rigidity Cost & Skeleton Classification  
**Status:** local theorem/computation note for upstream hostile review  
**Scope:** complete off-diagonal generic domain with q anonymous terminal outputs, q>=3  
**Upstream boundary:** this note does not modify G4 or the published M0–G2 checkpoint.

Let

\[
D=G^2\setminus\Delta,
\qquad |G|=n.
\]

Let

\[
c:D\to O,
\qquad |O|=q\ge3,
\]

be a surjective anonymous coloring of all ordered off-diagonal generic cells by q terminal outputs.

The full anonymous carrier group is

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Aut}^{\rm an}(c)
=
\{g\in S_G:\exists\pi\in S_O\text{ with }c(gx,gy)=\pi(c(x,y))\ \forall x\ne y\}.
}
\]

---

## 1. Why the binary ternary phase theorem breaks for q>=3

For q=2, the ternary relation

\[
Q(x,y,z)\iff c(x,y)=c(y,z)
\]

is exact because preserving equality between adjacent cells forces a binary discrepancy bit to be constant on the connected ordered-cell line graph.

For q>=3, inequality no longer determines the image color uniquely. If two adjacent cells have different colors, they may be sent to many different unequal pairs. Thus the local discrepancy is no longer a single Z_2 phase.

The same Q-reduct can therefore identify many globally inequivalent q-colorings.

---

## 2. Maximal ternary equality data still fail at q=3

The failure is not an artifact of choosing a weak ternary formula.

Consider the symmetric subclass in which

\[
c(x,y)=c(y,x).
\]

Such a layer is just an anonymous edge-coloring of the complete graph, duplicated in the two directed cells.

Take vertices

\[
V=\{0,1,2,3,4\}
\]

and three colors `0,1,2`. Color the ten unordered edges as follows:

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccccccc}
\text{edge}&01&02&03&04&12&13&14&23&24&34\\
\hline
c&0&0&0&1&0&0&2&1&0&0.
\end{array}
\]

Equivalently, the color classes are

\[
C_0=\{01,02,03,12,13,24,34\},
\]

\[
C_1=\{04,23\},
\]

\[
C_2=\{14\}.
\]

Let

\[
g=(0\ 1).
\]

Then

\[
g(C_0)=C_0,
\]

but

\[
g(C_1)=\{14,23\},
\qquad
g(C_2)=\{04\}.
\]

Hence no single permutation of the three anonymous colors realizes g: the edge 04 would force colors 1 and 2 to be exchanged, while the fixed edge 23 would force color 1 to remain color 1. Therefore

\[
g\notin\operatorname{Aut}^{\rm an}(c).
\]

However g preserves the **entire anonymous equality pattern on every labeled subset of size at most three**.

Why? The only global inconsistency is between the two disjoint color-1 edges

\[
04\quad\text{and}\quad23.
\]

No three-vertex subset contains both of them. On the triangle 014 the three edge colors are all distinct, so exchanging the local roles of colors 1 and 2 changes no equality relation. On triangles containing 23, the transported equality pattern is unchanged. A direct audit of all ten labeled triangles confirms preservation of every equality/non-equality relation among the six directed cells supported on the triangle.

Thus g lies in the automorphism group of the **maximal ternary anonymous equality reduct**, although it is not an automorphism of the full anonymous color layer.

Exact computation gives

\[
\boxed{
|\operatorname{Aut}^{\rm an}(c)|=2,
\qquad
|\operatorname{Aut}(R_{\le3}^{\rm eq}(c))|=4.
}
\]

The true group is generated by `(2 3)`, while the maximal ternary equality reduct additionally admits `(0 1)`.

Hence:

\[
\boxed{
\text{even all labeled anonymous equality information on }\le3\text{ vertices is not stabilizer-complete for }q=3.
}
\]

## 3. The obstruction extends to every fixed q>=3

The q=3 witness can be extended to any q>3.

Adjoin enough new vertices fixed by the extra permutation g=(0 1). Use q-3 fresh colors on selected edges entirely inside the new fixed vertex set, and color all remaining new incident edges by color 0.

Then:

1. all q colors occur;
2. g still preserves every local anonymous equality pattern on at most three vertices;
3. the original inconsistency between the disjoint edges 04 and 23 remains, so g is still not induced by any single global color permutation.

Therefore for every fixed

\[
q\ge3
\]
there exists a complete q-anonymous-output layer whose maximal ternary equality reduct has a strictly larger carrier stabilizer than the full layer.

So arity three is not universally exact for any q>=3 in this local equality-pattern model.

## 4. Universal four-ary exact reduct

Define

\[
\boxed{
E_\star(x,y,u,v)
\iff
x\star y=u\star v,
}
\]

for

\[
x\ne y,\qquad u\ne v.
\]

This relation is exactly the equality partition of the ordered-cell domain D.

### Theorem

For every finite q>=2 and every surjective anonymous coloring

\[
c:D\to O,
\]

restriction to the carrier gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Aut}(G,E_\star)
=
\operatorname{Aut}^{\rm an}(c).
}
\]

### Proof

Every anonymous color automorphism preserves equality of cell values, so

\[
\operatorname{Aut}^{\rm an}(c)
\le
\operatorname{Aut}(E_\star).
\]

Conversely, if g preserves E, then for all cells p,q in D,

\[
c(p)=c(q)
\iff
c(gp)=c(gq).
\]

Thus g preserves the partition of D into value fibers. Since c is surjective, define

\[
\pi(c(p)):=c(gp).
\]

Preservation of E makes this well-defined, and applying the same argument to g^{-1} makes pi bijective. Hence

\[
\pi\in S_O
\]

and

\[
c(gp)=\pi(c(p))
\]

for every cell p. Therefore

\[
g\in\operatorname{Aut}^{\rm an}(c).
\]

This is exactly the Fiber-Transport principle written as a carrier relation. \(\square\)

## 5. Exact arity phase transition

Combining the binary theorem with the multicolor obstruction gives the following result in the class of reducts determined by anonymous equality patterns on bounded-size carrier subsets.

### Theorem — Arity Phase Transition

For complete off-diagonal anonymous terminal layers:

\[
\boxed{
q=2:\quad k_{\rm exact}=3,
}
\]

while for every fixed

\[
q\ge3,
\]

\[
\boxed{
k_{\rm exact}=4.}
\]

Here `exact` means that the reduct has exactly the same carrier automorphism group as the full anonymous value layer.

The lower bound at q>=3 is supplied by the explicit five-vertex q=3 witness and its q-color extensions. The upper bound is the universal four-ary equality relation E.

Thus binary anonymous value geometry is genuinely exceptional:

\[
\boxed{
\text{two colors admit ternary global phase propagation; three or more colors require direct comparison of disjoint cells.}
}
\]

## 6. Geometric meaning of the lower bound

The q=3 counterexample isolates the exact missing datum.

Three variables can inspect cells whose endpoint supports fit inside one 3-set. They cannot directly compare two disjoint cells such as

\[
(0,4)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(2,3).
\]

With two colors, connected overlap propagates the unique alternative phase, so such a direct comparison is unnecessary.

With q>=3, local color permutations on different overlapping triples can agree on all colors visible in their overlaps yet disagree on colors that are never jointly witnessed. This is a genuine local gauge freedom.

The fourth variable allows the relation E to compare arbitrary disjoint cells and kills this gauge freedom immediately.
