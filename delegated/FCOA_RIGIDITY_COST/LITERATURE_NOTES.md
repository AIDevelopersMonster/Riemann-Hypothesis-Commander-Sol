# FCOA Rigidity Cost — Literature Positioning Notes

**Status:** branch-local prior-art firewall; not a novelty claim.

## Classical territory

The broad theory of asymmetric graphs and prescribed automorphism groups is classical. In particular, this branch must not claim novelty for the existence of rigid/asymmetric graphs or digraphs, for general edge-minimization under automorphism constraints, or for asymmetric tournaments as combinatorial objects.

Representative prior art to keep in view:

- P. Erdős and A. Rényi, **“Asymmetric graphs,”** 1963 — foundational asymmetry work.
- Louis V. Quintas, **“Extrema concerning asymmetric graphs,”** *Journal of Combinatorial Theory* 3(1) (1967), 57–82, DOI `10.1016/S0021-9800(67)80018-8`.
- Louis V. Quintas, **“The least number of edges for graphs having symmetric automorphism group,”** *Journal of Combinatorial Theory* 5(2) (1968), 115–125, DOI `10.1016/S0021-9800(68)80046-8`.
- Pascal Schweitzer and Patrick Schweitzer, **“Minimal asymmetric graphs,”** *Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B* 127 (2017), 215–227, DOI `10.1016/j.jctb.2017.06.003`.
- Frucht-type realization theorems and graphical-regular-representation theory are also directly relevant to prescribed-group and cyclic-action questions.

## FCOA-specific novelty ceiling

The potentially useful content of this direction is not “asymmetric graph theory” itself. It is the way classical rigidity phenomena interact with the existing FCOA operation invariants.

Safe current wording:

> Within the FCOA terminal-layer framework, exact rigidity-cost calculations expose a separation between pure rigidity, successor-memory cost, domain geometry, Association Spectrum, commutation geometry, and fine anonymous value-fiber geometry.

A particularly sharp controlled comparison holds between G4-C and the tournament construction in `RESULTS.md`: complete domain, two anonymous values, balanced fibers, Association Spectrum and commutation can all be held fixed while the full automorphism group changes from `C2` to `1`.

Unsafe wording without a dedicated prior-art audit:

> We introduce the first theory of rigidity cost, asymmetric skeletons, prescribed automorphism minimization, or two-color rigid tournaments.

## Cyclic-action firewall

The undirected regular cyclic obstruction belongs to classical circulant/GRR theory: inversion preserves inverse-closed connection sets. Our cyclic statements are therefore fixed-action FCOA bookkeeping, not new GRR theorems.

## Tournament firewall

Asymmetric and non-self-converse tournaments are classical objects. The concrete `T_5` and recursive family are used here because they provide a self-contained FCOA witness with exact invariant matching to G4-C. No graph-theoretic priority claim is attached to the tournament family itself.

## Publication rule

Before any rigidity-cost result is promoted to a publication novelty claim, perform a dedicated literature audit of the exact invariant/optimization statement being claimed, not merely a keyword search for asymmetric graphs.
