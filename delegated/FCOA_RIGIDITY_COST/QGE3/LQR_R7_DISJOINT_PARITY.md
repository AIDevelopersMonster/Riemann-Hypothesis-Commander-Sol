## 1. Ambient additive problem

Let

\[
V=\mathbb F_2^6.
\]

Let

\[
P,C\subset V\setminus\{0\},
\qquad
|P|=15,
\qquad
|C|=30,
\qquad
P\cap C=\varnothing.
\]

A **`P`-rainbow perfect matching of `C`** is a partition

\[
C=\bigsqcup_{p\in P}\{x_p,y_p\}
\]

such that

\[
x_p+y_p=p
\]

for every `p in P`.

The LQR fifteen-plane problem is a special case: the canonical oriented lines provide one such matching.

---

## 2. Balance is necessary

### Lemma 2.1
If a `P`-rainbow perfect matching of `C` exists, then

\[
\boxed{
\bigoplus_{p\in P}p
=
\bigoplus_{c\in C}c.
}
\]

### Proof
XOR the identities

\[
x_p+y_p=p
\]

over all `p in P`. The left side is the XOR of all points of `C`. \(\square\)

Thus random tests that do not impose the balance condition are mostly vacuous.

In the LQR oriented-line setting the balance identity holds automatically, since every canonical line satisfies

\[
u_a+v_a=p_a.
\]

---

## 3. Translation forms

For each nonzero `p in V`, define the global translation 2-form

\[
\omega_p
=
\sum_{\{x,x+p\}} e_x\wedge e_{x+p}
\]

on the 64-dimensional vector space with basis `\{e_x:x in V\}`.

For a parent set `P`, put

\[
\Omega_P=\prod_{p\in P}\omega_p.
\]

Then for every 30-set `C`, the coefficient of

\[
e_C:=\bigwedge_{c\in C}e_c
\]

in `Omega_P` is exactly the parity of `P`-rainbow perfect matchings of `C`.

Therefore the disjoint parity problem is equivalent to proving that all degree-30 coefficients supported entirely in `V\setminus P` vanish.

Equivalently, after quotienting the exterior algebra by the ideal generated by the parent basis vectors,

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{p\in P}\bar\omega_p=0
}
\]

would imply the desired parity theorem.

The fifteen restricted translation forms are generally linearly independent; a first-order dependence argument is therefore insufficient.

## 4. Pfaffian form

Let

\[
G_C(z)=\left(\sum_{p\in P}z_pT_p\right)_C
\]

be the 30 by 30 principal translation-circulant minor.

Then

\[
\boxed{
[z_{p_1}\cdots z_{p_{15}}]\operatorname{Pf}G_C(z)
}
\]

is exactly the rainbow-matching parity.

The current `J`-adic theorem gives, on the syndrome-zero linear subspace

\[
\sum z_p=0,
\qquad
\sum z_pp=0,
\]

the rank bound

\[
\operatorname{rank}G_C(z)\le26,
\]

hence double Pfaffian vanishing.

This is the first rigorous algebraic constraint toward the full mixed coefficient.

## 5. Disjoint Parent–Child Parity Conjecture

### Conjecture 5.1
Let `P,C` satisfy

\[
|P|=15,
\qquad
|C|=30,
\qquad
P\cap C=\varnothing.
\]

Then the parity of `P`-rainbow perfect matchings of `C` is even.

The conjecture is relevant only on the balanced locus

\[
\bigoplus P=\bigoplus C.
\]

If true, it immediately implies the oriented 15-line parity theorem, and therefore the LQR bound

\[
M_7\le14.
\]

Together with the known synchronizing fourteen-plane construction,

\[
\boxed{M_7=14}.
\]

## 6. Evidence and counterchecks

The following computational checks are evidence only:

- more than 400 randomly generated **balanced** disjoint pairs `(P,C)` in `F_2^6` produced even parity;
- analogous balanced statements fail in dimensions four and five;
- allowing `P cap C != empty` also produces odd examples in dimension six;
- therefore both the ambient dimension six and parent-child disjointness appear essential;
- compatible LQR fifteen-plane families tested so far all lie in the even-parity regime.

This is substantially stronger evidence than unconditioned random testing, because the balance obstruction has been removed.

---

## 7. Relation to prescribed-difference matching literature

The additive matching formulation is closely related to the prescribed-differences problem in `F_2^n`, studied for full-space pairings by Benedek Kovacs and others. Those results address existence for a prescribed multiset of differences on all `2^n` vertices, whereas the present problem concerns parity of rainbow matchings on a 30-point subset with 15 distinct differences.

The translation-circulant/character-minor language is therefore natural, but the parity statement above is not supplied directly by the known full-space existence theorems.
