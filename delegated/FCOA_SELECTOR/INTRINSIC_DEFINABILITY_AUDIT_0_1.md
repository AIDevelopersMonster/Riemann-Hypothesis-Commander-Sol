\[
N_\Sigma(i,j)=i+j,
\qquad

\[

on nonzero base points.

- `N_gap` is generated by synchronous radial cancellation until one endpoint reaches the root;

\[
\{i,j\},\qquad i+j,\qquad |i-j|,\qquad (i+j)\bmod 2.

Thus ordinary binary integer addition or subtraction is not imported as selector data.

However a crucial logical distinction must be maintained:

\[
\boxed{
\text{intrinsically generated / invariantly characterized}
\ne
\text{uniformly parameter-free FO definable}.
}
\]

This note proves intrinsic generation and isomorphism invariance. It does **not** claim uniform FO definability of unbounded path length, exact radial equality, or parity in the weakest reduct.

# 2. Radial histories without numerical coordinates

## Definition 2.1 — contraction history

For a nonzero base point `x`, its contraction history is the finite directed path

\[
H(x):
\quad
x\to\rho(x)\to\rho^2(x)\to\cdots\to P_0.
\]

No integer label is part of this definition.

Two histories are **rooted-path isomorphic** if there is a directed graph isomorphism between them fixing the terminal root.

## Definition 2.2 — intrinsic radial equivalence

Define

\[
x\equiv_r y
\]

iff `H(x)` and `H(y)` are rooted-path isomorphic.

This is an internal structural equivalence: it says only that the two points occupy the same depth in the rooted contraction graph.

### Lemma 2.3 — reflection invariance

\[
x\equiv_r y
\iff
\nu x\equiv_r\nu y.
\]

### Proof

The parent line proves

\[
\rho\nu=\nu\rho.
\]

Hence reflection sends the entire contraction history of `x` isomorphically to the contraction history of `nu x`, preserving the root. `square`

### Coordinate check only

Under the external coordinate identification

\[
P_i^\pm\leftrightarrow \pm i,
\]

one has

\[
P_i^\sigma\equiv_r P_j^\tau
\iff i=j.
\]

This formula verifies the structural definition; it is not used to define it.

# 3. Intrinsic `N_orb`

For a mixed commutative event

\[
p=\{x,y\}
\]

with `x,y` on opposite branches, define its **radial-history multiset**

\[
R(p)=\{[H(x)],[H(y)]\},
\]

where brackets denote rooted-path isomorphism type.

## Definition 3.1

Two events satisfy

\[
p\equiv_{orb}q
\]

iff their radial-history multisets are equal.

This construction uses only:

- the two endpoints of the event;
- the inherited contraction graph;
- unorderedness already imposed at the `F_mix` stage.

### Proposition 3.2

In coordinates,

\[
p_{ij}\equiv_{orb}p_{kl}
\iff
\{i,j\}=\{k,l\}.
\]

Hence this intrinsic quotient is exactly the earlier `N_orb`.

### Proof

A finite rooted directed chain is classified up to rooted-path isomorphism by its number of edges. The two endpoint histories therefore have types corresponding externally to depths `i` and `j`. Equality of unordered history multisets is exactly equality of the unordered depth multisets. `square`

No binary arithmetic is involved.

# 4. Intrinsic bridge-span quotient

For opposite-branch endpoints `x,y`, their contraction histories meet for the first time at the root.

Form the undirected bridge path

\[
B(x,y)
:=
H(x)\cup H(y),
\]

with the common root identified.

This is the unique simple path in the rooted two-arm graph joining `x` to `y`.

## Definition 4.1 — bridge-span equivalence

For mixed events

\[
p=\{x,y\},
\qquad
q=\{u,v\},
\]

define

\[
p\equiv_{span}q
\]

iff the finite rooted-at-center path systems `B(x,y)` and `B(u,v)` have the same **total edge cardinality**.

Equivalent generator formulation: begin with both endpoint histories and concatenate their edge sets at the common root; identify two events exactly when the resulting finite bridge paths contain equally many contraction edges.

This construction is intrinsic to the rooted graph. It does not apply a binary numerical addition operation to endpoint coordinates.

### Theorem 4.2 — coordinate shadow of intrinsic span

For

\[
p_{ij}=\{P_i^+,P_j^-\},
\]

the bridge `B(P_i^+,P_j^-)` contains exactly `i+j` edges. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\equiv_{span}=\ker N_\Sigma.
}
\]

### Proof

The positive endpoint history and negative endpoint history are edge-disjoint except for their common terminal vertex `P0`. The first contains `i` edges and the second `j` edges. Their union therefore contains `i+j` edges. This is a metatheoretic count proving the coordinate representation of an already-defined graph invariant. `square`

### Firewall conclusion

The operation `+` in the displayed coordinate count belongs to the **proof language**, not the FCOA selector signature.

The quotient itself is generated by finite bridge-path isomorphism/cardinality.

# 5. Intrinsic synchronous-cancellation quotient

The parent branch already identified synchronous cancellation as a natural but arithmetic-leaky control process. For SOL-SELECTOR that process can be used as an intrinsic quotient generator even though its resulting information may later be judged too arithmetically rich.

## Definition 5.1 — simultaneous contraction process

Given a mixed event

\[
p=\{x,y\},
\]

define recursively

\[
(x_0,y_0)=(x,y),
\]

and while both coordinates are non-root set

\[
(x_{t+1},y_{t+1})
=(\rho x_t,\rho y_t).
\]

Stop at the first time `t` for which at least one coordinate equals `P0`.

The terminal unordered state has one of the forms

\[
\{P_0,P_0\}
\]

or

\[
\{P_0,z\}
\qquad(z\ne P_0).
\]

Discard branch orientation of `z` using the residual reflection and retain only the rooted history type `[H(z)]`; for the double-root state retain a distinguished zero-residue symbol.

Call the resulting invariant

\[
C(p).
\]

## Definition 5.2 — intrinsic gap equivalence

\[
p\equiv_{gap}q
\iff
C(p)=C(q).
\]

This is defined solely by parallel iteration of the inherited unary contraction and reflection-forgetting at the terminal state.

### Theorem 5.3 — coordinate shadow of synchronous cancellation

For

\[
p_{ij}=\{P_i^+,P_j^-\},
\]

the residual rooted depth is

\[
|i-j|.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\equiv_{gap}=\ker N_\Delta.
}
\]

### Proof

Each simultaneous contraction step removes one edge from each non-root history. The process stops after the shorter history is exhausted. The remaining number of edges in the longer history is exactly the difference between the original history lengths, independently of which branch was longer. Reflection-forgetting removes the sign of that difference. In external coordinates this residual length is `|i-j|`. `square`

Again, subtraction is not used by the construction; it appears only as the coordinate description of the terminal residual history.

# 6. Intrinsic incomparability survives

The earlier incomparability theorem can now be restated without taking coordinate arithmetic as selector input.

## Theorem 6.1 — Intrinsic Span/Gap Incomparability

The intrinsic bridge-span quotient and intrinsic synchronous-cancellation quotient are incomparable in the canonical quotient order.

### Proof

Consider two mixed bridges whose endpoint radial histories have lengths `(1,4)` and `(2,3)` in an external audit labeling. Their bridge paths contain the same total number of contraction edges, so they are `span`-equivalent. Under simultaneous contraction the first leaves a residual history of length `3`, the second one of length `1`, so they are not `gap`-equivalent.

Conversely, histories `(1,2)` and `(2,3)` leave residual histories of equal length `1`, hence are `gap`-equivalent, while their total bridge lengths are distinct, so they are not `span`-equivalent.

The witnesses are described numerically only for compactness. Every statement involved is a statement about finite contraction-path lengths and the synchronous contraction process. `square`

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\theta_{span}\parallel\theta_{gap}
}
\]

is an intrinsic FCOA fact.

This clears the main publication-critical concern raised after `QUOTIENT_POSET_0_1.md`.

# 7. Intrinsic meet

## Theorem 7.1

\[
\boxed{
\theta_{span}\wedge\theta_{gap}
=
\theta_{orb}.
}
\]

### Structural proof

A mixed event supplies two finite rooted histories. Knowing both:

1. the total number of edges in their root-joined bridge, and
2. the residual history type after synchronous cancellation,

uniquely determines the unordered pair of history lengths.

Indeed, starting from the terminal residual history, reverse the synchronous process conceptually by attaching one edge simultaneously to both sides until the prescribed bridge size is reached. Because each reverse synchronous step adds exactly two bridge edges, there is a unique number of such steps compatible with the total bridge size. This reconstructs the unordered pair of rooted histories.

Conversely the unordered pair of histories plainly determines both bridge size and cancellation residue. `square`

This proof uses only edge attachment/removal in finite rooted chains. The familiar half-sum/half-difference formulas are unnecessary.

# 8. Intrinsic parity/common phase quotient

The earlier join `N_par` can also be characterized without primitive arithmetic.

## Definition 8.1 — contraction biphase

Color the rooted contraction graph alternately by distance parity from `P0`, with the root assigned phase `0` and every contraction edge switching phase.

Equivalently, the phase of a finite contraction history is the parity of its number of edges, but no numerical coordinate is required.

For a mixed bridge, define its **bridge phase** as whether its unique endpoint-to-endpoint bridge has even or odd edge length.

## Proposition 8.2

The bridge phase is preserved by both span equivalence and gap equivalence.

### Proof

Span equivalence preserves bridge length and hence its alternating phase.

During synchronous cancellation, each step removes exactly two edges from the full endpoint-to-endpoint bridge. Therefore bridge phase is unchanged, and at termination it equals the phase of the residual history. Thus gap-equivalent events also share bridge phase. `square`

## Theorem 8.3 — intrinsic join theorem

The equivalence relation generated jointly by `theta_span` and `theta_gap` has exactly two classes, the two bridge phases.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\theta_{span}\vee\theta_{gap}=\theta_{phase}.
}
\]

Under external coordinates this is precisely

\[
(i+j)\bmod2.
\]

### Proof

For an even-phase bridge, repeatedly use span-equivalence to replace it by a symmetric bridge whose two radial histories have equal length; all such symmetric bridges have zero cancellation residue and are therefore gap-equivalent to one another.

For an odd-phase bridge, use span-equivalence to replace it by a bridge whose two histories differ by one edge; all such bridges have one-edge cancellation residue and are therefore gap-equivalent to one another.

The two classes cannot merge because both generating equivalences preserve bridge phase. `square`

This is the intrinsic version of the previous parity-join theorem.

# 9. Logical-scope audit: what has NOT been proved

The constructions above are intrinsic in the category-theoretic / structural sense:

- they are built canonically from inherited FCOA maps and finite histories;
- they are invariant under isomorphisms of the rooted/reflected contraction structure;
- they require no primitive binary integer addition, subtraction, multiplication, or signed-coordinate map.

But the following stronger claims are **not** established:

1. that `x equiv_r y` is uniformly first-order definable in the weakest infinite signed-`oplus` reduct;
2. that equality of arbitrary finite bridge lengths is uniformly FO definable there;
3. that the bridge biphase is parameter-free FO definable there;
4. that synchronous cancellation is itself represented by a single first-order formula rather than an externally iterated canonical process.

The parent line already warned that rooted radial memory does not automatically imply uniform FO coordinate definability.

Accordingly the paper must use terminology such as

\[
\boxed{\text{intrinsic structural quotient}}
\]

or

\[
\boxed{\text{canonically generated quotient}}
\]

unless and until a separate FO-definability theorem is proved.

# 11. Arithmetic leakage is a separate question

Passing the primitive-import firewall does **not** mean the quotients are arithmetically harmless.

In particular, the parent line already observed that synchronous cancellation computes the mixed-sign magnitude difference and is therefore an arithmetic-leaky control mechanism.

Thus two different questions must remain separated:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Was arithmetic imported in the definition?}
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\text{How much arithmetic becomes recoverable from the resulting quotient?}
}
\]

For `N_gap`, the answer to the first question is **no**, while the second may be **substantial**.

For `N_span`, leakage still requires a dedicated audit.

This distinction is important for SOL-SELECTOR: universal or intrinsic status does not by itself imply low arithmetic cost.
