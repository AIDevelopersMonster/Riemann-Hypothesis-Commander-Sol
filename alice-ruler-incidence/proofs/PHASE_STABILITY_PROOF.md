# Quantitative Hall/projective phase stability — proof note

Status: publication-core proof, 2026-09-12.

This note isolates the new quantitative layer from the classical STS ingredients. Constants are deliberately crude; the theorem is about an absolute stability bound, not constant optimization.

## 1. Notation

Let `S` be an `STS(v)`, `v>3`.

An independent triple is a three-point set that is not a block. Define its phase:

- `P`: its generated Steiner subsystem is `S_7`;
- `H`: its generated Steiner subsystem is `S_9`;
- `D`: neither.

Let `G_ind(S)` be the graph on independent triples, with adjacency when two triples have exactly two common points.

Put

`V(G_ind)=P sqcup H sqcup D`, `N=|V(G_ind)|=v(v-1)(v-3)/6`,

and

`s=e(P,H)`, `q=min{|P|,|H|}`.

Let `c_A` denote the number of copies of the eight-point, five-line anti-mitre configuration `C_A` of Král–Máčajová–Pór–Sereni (configuration #4 in the Danziger–Mendelsohn–Grannell–Griggs five-line table).

Let `R_7` denote the different residual five-line configuration #7 generated by the mirror-completion experiment. Its count is `r_7`.

The five-line formulas give

`c_A = 2 r_7`.

Thus with the completion-simplex coordinate

`alpha = r_7/(6P(v))`, `P(v)=v(v-1)(v-3)/24`,

we have

`c_A=12P(v) alpha=3N alpha`.

## 2. Spectral phase isoperimetry

### Lemma 2.1

`G_ind(S)` is `3(v-4)`-regular and its Laplacian spectral gap is at least `v-3`.

### Proof

The graph is the principal induced subgraph of the Johnson graph `J(v,3)` obtained by deleting the STS blocks. The adjacency eigenvalues of `J(v,3)` are

`3(v-3), 2v-9, v-7, -3`.

By Cauchy interlacing, the second adjacency eigenvalue of `G_ind` is at most `2v-9`. Since `G_ind` is `3(v-4)`-regular,

`mu_2 >= 3(v-4)-(2v-9)=v-3`.

QED.

### Corollary 2.2

For every `A subset V(G_ind)`,

`e(A,A^c) >= (v-3)|A|(1-|A|/N)`.

In particular, if `|A|<=N/2`,

`e(A,A^c) >= ((v-3)/2)|A|`.

### Theorem 2.3 (phase isoperimetry)

`(v-3)q(1-q/N) <= s + 3(v-4)|D|`.          (PI)

Consequently,

`((v-3)/2)q <= s+3(v-4)|D|`.                (PIc)

### Proof

Apply Corollary 2.2 to the smaller of `P,H`. Its cut consists of direct `P-H` edges plus edges into `D`. The latter are at most `3(v-4)|D|` because `G_ind` has degree `3(v-4)`. QED.

## 3. Correct identification of the five-line defect

The research seed v0.6 incorrectly called the mirror residual `R_7` the anti-mitre `C_A`. They are not isomorphic.

The standard five-line table gives:

- configuration #4 (Král anti-mitre type):
  `012 034 135 236 457`,
  count `c_A=n(v)/2-12p-6m`;
- configuration #7 (mirror residual):
  `012 034 135 246 567`,
  count `r_7=n(v)/4-6p-3m`.

Thus `c_A=2r_7`. In particular `alpha=0` is still equivalent to `C_A`-free.

## 4. Bulk charging: every D-root lies in an anti-mitre

### Lemma 4.1 (root-preserving anti-mitre witness)

Every `D`-phase independent triple `tau={A,B,C}` is contained in a copy of the anti-mitre configuration `C_A`.

### Proof

Use the constructive proof of the local lemma of Král–Máčajová–Pór–Sereni. Put

`a=B⊕C`, `b=A⊕C`, `c=A⊕B`.

The proof splits according to whether `a,b,c` form a block.

#### Branch I: `a,b,c` form a block

Put `m=A⊕a` and `x=m⊕c`. If the required `S_7` identity fails (`x != C`; the alternative `x=b` is excluded by the STS relations used in the classical proof), the five blocks

`BCa, ACb, ABc, Aam, mcx`

form an anti-mitre and contain `A,B,C`.

#### Branch II: `a,b,c` are independent

Put

`A'=b⊕c`, `B'=a⊕c`, `C'=a⊕b`.

If the first required identity `A'=A⊕a` fails, put `y=A⊕a`. Then

`BCa, ACb, ABc, bcA', Aay`

form an anti-mitre containing `A,B,C`. The symmetric failures are identical.

Assume the first-level identities hold. At the second level, if the required identity `B'=A⊕C'` fails, put `x=A⊕C'`. One anti-mitre witness is

`BCa, ACb, abC', BbB', AC'x`,

again containing `A,B,C`. Symmetric failures have the same property.

If no failure occurs, the classical proof closes the root triple to `S_7` or `S_9`, contradicting that the root is `D`.

QED.

### Corollary 4.2 (bulk bound)

`|D| <= 56 c_A`.

### Proof

Charge every `D` root to one anti-mitre copy containing it. An anti-mitre has eight points and therefore contains at most `C(8,3)=56` three-point subsets, hence at most 56 possible roots. QED.

Since `c_A/N=3 alpha`,

`rho_D:=|D|/N <= 168 alpha`.                 (BD)

## 5. Interface charging

The remaining task is to control direct `P-H` edges.

Fix a `P-H` edge. Let its P endpoint generate a Fano subsystem `F ~= S_7`. The H endpoint shares two points with the P endpoint and contains one point `D_ext` outside `F`. Thus the edge canonically determines a mixed pair `(F,D_ext)` up to a bounded number of choices internal to the seven-point Fano plane.

For fixed `(F,D_ext)`, at most 84 `P-H` edges give this pair: choose the shared pair in `F` (at most 21 choices) and the third point of the independent P-root (at most four choices).

The finite local purity proof in the earlier Král–Máčajová–Pór–Sereni report considers only finitely many phase queries. If every query has phase `P` or `H`, the proof reaches a contradiction. Therefore a mixed `(F,D_ext)` produces at least one queried `D` triple.

Write `D_x=D_ext⊕x` for `x in F`. Up to Fano symmetry, every queried triple occurring in that finite proof belongs to one of the following reconstruction classes:

| class | queried root form | reverse reconstruction from a fixed root |
|---|---|---|
| I | `{D_ext,u,v}` | `D_ext,u,v` are fixed; the pair `u,v` fixes its STS block; only `O(v)` Fano subsystems contain that block |
| II | `{D_x,u,v}` | `u,v` are fixed; choose a Fano subsystem through their block (`O(v)` choices), then the finite role of `x` fixes `D_ext=x⊕D_x` |
| III | `{u,D_x,D_y}` | choose `D_ext` (`v` choices); recover `x,y`; in every occurring template `u,x,y` is noncollinear and hence generates `F` uniquely |
| IV | `{D_x,D_y,D_z}` | choose `D_ext` (`v` choices); recover `x,y,z`; in every occurring template they generate `F` uniquely |

For completeness, a fixed STS block lies in at most `(v-3)/4` Fano subsystems: after choosing a point outside the block there is at most one generated Fano subsystem, while each Fano subsystem contributes four outside points.

Because the old proof has a fixed finite list of query templates and the Fano plane has a fixed finite automorphism group, the table proves the following.

### Lemma 5.1 (interface fiber bound)

There exists an absolute constant `K` such that every fixed `D` root is the selected first-defect query for at most `K v` mixed pairs `(F,D_ext)`.

No optimization of `K` is required.

### Corollary 5.2 (interface bound)

There exists an absolute constant `C_I` such that

`s <= C_I v |D|`.                            (IB)

One may take `C_I=84K` from the preceding bookkeeping.

## 6. Quantitative two-phase stability

### Theorem 6.1 (Hall/projective phase stability)

There is an absolute constant `C>0` such that, for every nontrivial Steiner triple system,

`min(rho_P,rho_H) <= C alpha`.                (STAB)

Equivalently, since `c_A/N=3alpha`, the minority pure phase is linearly controlled by the normalized anti-mitre count.

### Proof

From (PIc) and (IB),

`((v-3)/2) q <= [C_I v+3(v-4)] |D|`.

Divide by `N`:

`min(rho_P,rho_H) <= 2[C_I v+3(v-4)]/(v-3) * rho_D`.

For `v>=7`,

`v/(v-3)<=7/4`, `(v-4)/(v-3)<1`.

Hence

`min(rho_P,rho_H) <= [(7/2)C_I+6] rho_D`.

Use `(BD)`, `rho_D<=168alpha`, to obtain (STAB), for example with

`C = 168[(7/2)C_I+6]`.

QED.

### Zero-defect case

If `alpha=0`, then `rho_D=0` and (STAB) gives `min(rho_P,rho_H)=0`. Thus all independent triples have a single phase. This recovers the projective/Hall dichotomy.

## 7. Scope of the theorem

This is **phase-profile stability**, not an edit-distance theorem for the block set of the STS. It says that a small anti-mitre density forces almost all independent triples into one of the two closure regimes (`S_7` or `S_9`). Converting this to an edit-distance statement (“the whole STS is close to an actual projective or Hall STS”) is a separate problem.
