# Abstract

This computational note studies a deliberately coarse geometric encoding of the Riemann zeta function on the critical line. For each interval between consecutive tabulated critical-line zeros, the Argand trajectory of `zeta(1/2 + i t)` defines a closed loop. The observable retains only binary membership of Cartesian integer-lattice points in the loop interior.

Across the first 10,000 loops, the resulting double-centered three-dimensional binary tensor preserves a strong physical-frequency comb near the Dirichlet frequencies `omega = log(m)`. After removal of the principal log-integer dictionary, residual peaks cluster near nonlinear difference combinations `log(m/k)`. An even/odd cross-fit regression using a predeclared dictionary of `log(n)` and small-ratio frequencies reduces out-of-sample variance by about 36.3%.

Static arithmetic controls do not support simple parity, mod-3, mod-5, or robust von-Mangoldt spatial effects. The note also develops a local sampling interpretation in which the Nyquist boundary for a Dirichlet mode `log(m)` occurs at `T = 2*pi*m^2`, with first-fold reciprocal label `m* = T/(2*pi*m)`. This is presented as an aliasing/identifiability analogy, not as a proof or mechanism for the Riemann Hypothesis.

The main contribution is therefore empirical and information-theoretic in spirit: a surprisingly coarse binary geometric observable appears to preserve substantial Dirichlet-frequency structure.
