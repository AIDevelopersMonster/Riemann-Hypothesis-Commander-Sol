## 1. Purpose

The preceding FCOA-Z paper proved that, after reversible completion and reflection-equivariant legacy transfer, all inherited same-sign sectors are forced and all remaining independent binary base freedom lies in the mixed sectors

\[
(X_+\times X_-)\cup(X_-\times X_+).
\]

Its next problem was to find a **natural mixed-sign generator that is not a hand-written case table**.

This note supplies one such generator.

The generator is not claimed to be forced by the previous paper. It is one additional structural principle. The theorem is that, once this principle is chosen, the mixed extension is unique and its local commutation/association laws are forced.

## 2. Fixed inherited data

Let

\[
X=\{x_k:k\in\mathbb Z\},\qquad x_0\text{ the distinguished root},
\]

with reflection

\[
\nu(x_k)=x_{-k}.
\]

Write

\[
X_+=\{x_k:k>0\},\qquad X_-=\{x_k:k<0\}.
\]

Define radial depth

\[
d(x_k)=|k|.
\]

The already-proved punctured radial contraction is

\[
\rho(x_k)=x_{k-\operatorname{sgn}(k)},\qquad k\neq0.
\]

Thus `rho` moves every non-root point exactly one step toward the root and satisfies

\[
\nu\rho=\rho\nu.
\]

We use only the inherited zero-port cells of the signed legacy operation `oplus`:

\[
x_0\oplus x=x\qquad(x\neq x_0),
\]

\[
x\oplus x_0=\rho(x)\qquad(x\neq x_0),
\]

while

\[
x_0\oplus x_0
\]

is UNDEF.

No generic same-sign value law is needed below.

## 3. Simultaneous radial reduction

For a mixed pair `(x,y)` with `x` and `y` on opposite nonzero sides, define one reduction step

\[
(x,y)\longmapsto (\rho x,\rho y)
\]

as long as both coordinates are non-root.

Each step lowers total radial depth by two. Hence the process terminates after exactly

\[
m(x,y)=\min\{d(x),d(y)\}
\]

steps.

### Definition 3.1 — radial normal form

The radial normal form `N(x,y)` of a mixed pair is the unique terminal pair obtained by simultaneous radial reduction.

Thus at least one coordinate of `N(x,y)` is `x_0`. Both are `x_0` exactly when `d(x)=d(y)`.

### Lemma 3.2 — termination and uniqueness

Every mixed pair has exactly one radial normal form.

#### Proof

At every step both coordinates are determined uniquely and total depth decreases by two. Therefore no branching is possible. After `min(d(x),d(y))` steps at least one depth is zero, so reduction terminates. The resulting pair is unique. □

## 4. The generator

### Definition 4.1 — Radial Cancellation Extension

Let `oplus_RC` agree with the inherited signed legacy operation on every previously fixed cell.

For a mixed pair `(x,y)`, compute

\[
N(x,y)=(u,v)
\]

and define

\[
x\oplus_{RC}y := u\oplus v
\]

whenever the inherited boundary cell `u oplus v` is defined. If `N(x,y)=(x_0,x_0)`, the mixed cell remains UNDEF because the inherited root/root cell is UNDEF.

Equivalently, the mixed sector is generated by the single recursion

\[
x\oplus_{RC}y=(\rho x)\oplus_{RC}(\rho y)
\]

until a zero-port boundary cell is reached.

This is the key point: **no mixed value is assigned individually**.

## 5. Existence and uniqueness

### Theorem 5.1 — Mixed Radial Cancellation Extension Theorem

There exists a unique mixed extension satisfying all three conditions:

1. legacy preservation on all inherited cells;
2. simultaneous radial cancellation invariance in the mixed sector;
3. evaluation at the first zero-port boundary using the inherited zero-port law, with root/root remaining UNDEF.

#### Proof

Existence follows from Definition 4.1 and Lemma 3.2.

For uniqueness, fix a mixed pair `(x,y)` and induct on

\[
m=\min\{d(x),d(y)\}.
\]

If `m=1`, one simultaneous contraction reaches a boundary pair and condition 3 fixes the value/undefined status.

For `m>1`, condition 2 reduces the cell to

\[
(\rho x,\rho y),
\]

whose minimum depth is `m-1`. The induction hypothesis uniquely fixes that reduced cell. Therefore the original cell is uniquely fixed. □

## 6. Reflection is automatic

### Theorem 6.1 — Reflection Compatibility

The Radial Cancellation Extension is reflection-equivariant on the mixed sector:

\[
\nu(x\oplus_{RC}y)=\nu x\oplus_{RC}\nu y
\]

whenever the mixed cell is defined, and definedness is reflection-invariant.

#### Proof

Because `nu rho = rho nu`, simultaneous radial reduction commutes with reflection:

\[
N(\nu x,\nu y)=(\nu\times\nu)N(x,y).
\]

The inherited zero-port boundary law is already reflection-equivariant. Hence boundary evaluation commutes with reflection, proving the claim. □

Thus the mixed table does not require a second independent reflection axiom once radial cancellation and the inherited boundary ports are fixed.

## 7. Exact mixed-sector formula

Let `x_a,x_b` be a mixed pair, so `ab<0`.

### Theorem 7.1 — Survivor Formula

1. If `|a|=|b|`, then

\[
x_a\oplus_{RC}x_b
\]

is UNDEF.

2. If `|a|>|b|`, the first argument survives cancellation. Let `q` be that non-root survivor. Then

\[
x_a\oplus_{RC}x_b=\rho(q).
\]

3. If `|a|<|b|`, the second argument survives cancellation, and

\[
x_a\oplus_{RC}x_b=q.
\]

In external integer coordinates this becomes

\[
x_a\oplus_{RC}x_b=
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{UNDEF}, & |a|=|b|,\\
 x_{a+b-\operatorname{sgn}(a)}, & |a|>|b|,\\
 x_{a+b}, & |a|<|b|.
\end{cases}
\]

#### Proof

Simultaneous contraction removes equal radial depth from both inputs. If the depths agree, both reach the root and the inherited root/root cell is UNDEF. Otherwise exactly one non-root survivor remains. If it is first, the terminal boundary cell has the form `(q,x_0)` and the right-zero port gives `rho(q)`. If it is second, the boundary cell is `(x_0,q)` and the left-zero port returns `q`. The coordinate formula is only a closed-form description of this intrinsic reduction; it is not used to define the generator. □

### Corollary 7.2 — one-step deformation of signed coordinate addition

Where defined, the output differs from the coordinate residual `x_{a+b}` by at most one radial step, and the one-step defect occurs exactly when the first argument is the survivor.

This is a comparison statement, not an identification of `oplus_RC` with ordinary addition.

## 8. Mixed commutation defect

### Theorem 8.1 — Adjacent-Output Noncommutation

Let `x,y` be mixed with unequal radial depths, and let `q` be the unique non-root survivor after simultaneous cancellation. Then both oriented products are defined and

\[
\{x\oplus_{RC}y,\ y\oplus_{RC}x\}=\{q,\rho(q)\}.
\]

Hence

\[
x\oplus_{RC}y\neq y\oplus_{RC}x.
\]

If the radial depths are equal, both oriented products are UNDEF.

#### Proof

When the deeper argument is first, boundary evaluation uses the right-zero port and returns `rho(q)`. After swapping the arguments, the same survivor is second and boundary evaluation uses the left-zero port and returns `q`. Since `q` is non-root, `rho(q) != q`. Equal depths reduce to root/root in both orientations. □

The mixed commutation defect is therefore not an arbitrary value difference: it is exactly one radial edge.

## 9. Association status

Use the FCOA association status

\[
\mathcal A(x,y,z)\in\{EQ,NEQ,LEFT,RIGHT,NONE\},
\]

where:

- `EQ`: both bracketings are defined and equal;
- `NEQ`: both are defined and unequal;
- `LEFT`: only `(x oplus y) oplus z` is defined;
- `RIGHT`: only `x oplus (y oplus z)` is defined;
- `NONE`: neither is defined.

The following results use only mixed cells and inherited zero-port cells, so they are independent of all unspecified generic same-sign values.

## 10. Root on the right: four-phase law

Let `x,y` be mixed and put

\[
\delta=d(x)-d(y).
\]

### Theorem 10.1 — Rooted Mixed Associativity Phase Theorem

For the triple `(x,y,x_0)`,

\[
\mathcal A(x,y,x_0)=
\begin{cases}
EQ, & \delta\le -2,\\
LEFT, & \delta=-1,\\
RIGHT, & \delta\in\{0,1\},\\
NEQ, & \delta\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]

#### Proof

We split by the depth surplus.

**Case `delta <= -2`.** The second argument survives mixed cancellation with survivor `q` of depth `-delta >= 2`, so

\[
x\oplus_{RC}y=q.
\]

The left bracketing gives

\[
(x\oplus_{RC}y)\oplus x_0=\rho(q).
\]

On the right, `y oplus x_0 = rho(y)`. The second argument is still deeper than `x`, now by `-delta-1 >=1`, and mixed radial cancellation returns exactly `rho(q)`. Hence `EQ`.

**Case `delta=-1`.** The mixed product is the depth-one survivor `q`, so the left bracketing contracts it to the root. On the right, first contracting `y` makes the mixed depths equal, hence the mixed cell becomes UNDEF. Therefore `LEFT`.

**Case `delta=0`.** The inner mixed product on the left is UNDEF. On the right, contracting `y` makes `x` the first survivor by one radial step; the right-zero port contracts that survivor to the root. Hence only the right bracketing is defined: `RIGHT`.

**Case `delta=1`.** The mixed product on the left is already the root, so the next root/root cell is UNDEF. On the right, contracting `y` makes the first-argument surplus equal to two, and the mixed product is a non-root depth-one point. Hence `RIGHT`.

**Case `delta>=2`.** Let `q` be the first-argument survivor of depth `delta`. The mixed product is `rho(q)`, so the left bracketing is

\[
\rho^2(q).
\]

On the right, first replacing `y` by `rho(y)` increases the first-argument surplus to `delta+1`; mixed cancellation then leaves a survivor of depth `delta+1`, and the right-zero port contracts it once to `q`. Both bracketings are defined, but

\[
\rho^2(q)\neq q
\]

for `d(q)>=2`. Hence `NEQ`. □

This is a genuine local phase law: association status is determined by the relative radial depths and argument roles, not by a manual triple table.

## 11. Root on the left: collision law

### Theorem 11.1 — Left-Root Association Law

For mixed `x,y` with `delta=d(x)-d(y)`,

\[
\mathcal A(x_0,x,y)=
\begin{cases}
NONE, & \delta=0,\\
LEFT, & \delta=1,\\
EQ, & \delta\notin\{0,1\}.
\end{cases}
\]

#### Proof

Because `x_0 oplus x=x`, the left bracketing equals the mixed product `x oplus_RC y` whenever that product is defined.

If `delta=0`, the mixed product is UNDEF, so both bracketings are UNDEF: `NONE`.

If `delta=1`, the mixed product is the root. Thus the left bracketing is defined with value `x_0`, but the right bracketing requires the inherited root/root cell and is UNDEF: `LEFT`.

For every other `delta`, the mixed product is a non-root point `z`. Then

\[
x_0\oplus z=z,
\]

so both bracketings are defined and equal: `EQ`. □

## 12. Five-status realization

### Corollary 12.1 — Complete Association-Status Realization

The single Radial Cancellation Extension realizes all five FCOA association statuses on root-containing mixed triples:

- `EQ` occurs, for example, in Theorem 10.1 for `delta <= -2`;
- `NEQ` occurs for `delta >=2`;
- `LEFT` occurs for `delta=-1` in Theorem 10.1 and for `delta=1` in Theorem 11.1;
- `RIGHT` occurs for `delta=0` or `1` in Theorem 10.1;
- `NONE` occurs for `delta=0` in Theorem 11.1.

Therefore the programme-level hypothesis from the preceding paper crosses an important threshold:

> a single non-tabular mixed interaction generator can force different local associativity laws in different geometric sectors and depth regimes.

This does **not** imply that ordinary global associativity is non-fundamental in general. It is a theorem about this FCOA-Z extension.

## 13. Classical neighbor and novelty boundary

The cancellation step itself has a classical neighbor. The one-sided shift and its partial inverse generate the bicyclic monoid, whose normal-form multiplication cancels exponents by a `min` rule. Therefore **radial cancellation as an abstract cancellation mechanism is not claimed as new**.

The narrower FCOA-Z contribution is the following conjunction:

1. a previously fixed role-sensitive partial operation supplies asymmetric zero ports;
2. a reversible carrier completion supplies two reflected sides and radial contraction;
3. simultaneous mixed radial cancellation is used only as a normal-form generator;
4. the boundary normal form is evaluated through the pre-existing asymmetric FCOA ports;
5. this forces adjacent-output noncommutation and a complete five-status local association spectrum.

A separate literature audit is still required before any external priority claim.
