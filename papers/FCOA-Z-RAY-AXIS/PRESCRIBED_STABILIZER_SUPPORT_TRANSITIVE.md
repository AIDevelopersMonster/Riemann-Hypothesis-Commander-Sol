# FCOA-Z — Exact Prescribed-Stabilizer Support for Transitive Wreath Branch Groups

Status: theorem package v0.2  
Date: 2026-08-31  
Supersedes the 2-transitive restriction in `PRESCRIBED_STABILIZER_SUPPORT.md`.

## 1. Setup

Let `Lambda` be a finite set of size `t>=2`, and let

\[
A\le \operatorname{Sym}(\Lambda)
\]

be **transitive**. Let `b>=2` and let

\[
X=[b]\times\Lambda.
\]

Let

\[
G=A\wr S_b=A^b\rtimes S_b
\]

act in the natural imprimitive way.

Define the ordered cross-branch cell set

\[
S_\times=
\{((r,i),(s,j)):r\neq s\}.
\]

Then

\[
|S_\times|=b(b-1)t^2.
\]

Fix one coherence identification of the internal coordinate sets of all branches and define

\[
F_=\ =
\{((r,i),(s,i)):r\neq s\}.
\]

Thus

\[
|F_=|=b(b-1)t.
\]

Let

\[
H=\Delta A\times S_b,
\qquad
\Delta A=\{(\sigma,\ldots,\sigma):\sigma\in A\}.
\]

## 2. Exact stabilizer of the equality fiber

### Theorem 2.1

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Stab}_G(F_=)=H.}
\]

#### Proof

Every element of `H` preserves equality of internal coordinates, hence preserves `F_=`.

Conversely let

\[
g=(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_b;\pi)\in G
\]

stabilize `F_=`. For every distinct `r,s` and every `i in Lambda`, the equality cell

\[
((r,i),(s,i))
\]

is sent to

\[
((\pi r,\sigma_r i),(\pi s,\sigma_s i)).
\]

For this to lie again in `F_=` one must have

\[
\sigma_r i=\sigma_s i
\]

for every `i`. Hence

\[
\sigma_r=\sigma_s.
\]

As `r,s` are arbitrary distinct branches, all `sigma_r` are equal. The branch permutation `pi` is unrestricted. Therefore

\[
g\in \Delta A\times S_b=H.
\]

□

This proof uses no 2-transitivity.

## 3. General lower bound from transitivity

### Lemma 3.1

Every nonempty orbit of the diagonal action of a transitive permutation group `A` on

\[
\Lambda\times\Lambda
\]

has size at least `t`.

#### Proof

Let `O` be such an orbit. Its projection onto the first coordinate is `A`-invariant. Since `A` is transitive and `O` is nonempty, this projection is all of `Lambda`. Therefore `O` contains at least one point above each of the `t` first coordinates, so

\[
|O|\ge t.
\]

□

The diagonal relation

\[
\Delta_\Lambda=\{(i,i):i\in\Lambda\}
\]

is itself one `A`-orbit of size exactly `t` because `A` is transitive.

## 4. H-orbits on the cross-cell set

The branch-permutation factor `S_b` is transitive on ordered pairs of distinct branches. Therefore every `H`-orbit on `S_x` has the form

\[
O_R=
\{((r,i),(s,j)):r\neq s,\ (i,j)\in R\},
\]

where `R` is one diagonal `A`-orbit on `Lambda^2`.

Hence

\[
|O_R|=b(b-1)|R|.
\]

By Lemma 3.1,

\[
|O_R|\ge b(b-1)t.
\]

The equality fiber `F_=` corresponds to the diagonal orbital `Delta_Lambda` and attains this bound.

## 5. Exact prescribed-support theorem

Recall

\[
m_G(H;S)=\min\{|F|:F\subseteq S,\operatorname{Stab}_G(F)=H\}.
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — Transitive Wreath Support Theorem

For every transitive `A<=Sym(Lambda)` of degree `t>=2` and every `b>=2`,

\[
\boxed{
m_G(H;S_\times)=b(b-1)t.}
\]

#### Proof

Let `F subset S_x` satisfy

\[
\operatorname{Stab}_G(F)=H.
\]

Then `H` stabilizes `F`, so `F` is a union of `H`-orbits. Since `F` cannot be empty, it contains at least one nonempty `H`-orbit. Section 4 shows that every such orbit has size at least

\[
b(b-1)t.
\]

Therefore

\[
|F|\ge b(b-1)t.
\]

On the other hand the equality fiber `F_=` has exactly that size and Theorem 2.1 gives

\[
\operatorname{Stab}_G(F_=)=H.
\]

So the lower bound is attained. □

### Consequence

The exact support formula is independent of the rank, subdegrees, orbital count, primitivity, or 2-transitivity of `A`. Transitivity alone is sufficient.

## 6. Coherence-state count

The number of coherence states generated by the ambient wreath action is

\[
[G:H]
=
\frac{|A|^b b!}{|A|b!}
=
\boxed{|A|^{b-1}}.
\]

Thus the semantic information is

\[
\boxed{I_{\rm coh}=(b-1)\log_2|A|.}
\]

The support cost depends only on the permutation degree `t`, while the semantic state count depends on the group order `|A|`.

This cleanly separates two resources:

\[
\boxed{\text{support geometry depends on degree; semantic multiplicity depends on group size}.}
\]

## 7. Natural-domain cost

Because `A` is transitive, the full wreath product `G=A wr S_b` is transitive on `S_x`: independent branch actions move each internal coordinate arbitrarily within its branch orbit, while `S_b` moves ordered branch pairs.

Therefore any nonempty `G`-invariant domain contained in `S_x` is all of `S_x`.

Hence the connection-independent natural-domain cost remains

\[
\boxed{|D|=b(b-1)t^2.}
\]

The optimal special fiber occupies the exact fraction

\[
\boxed{\frac{m_G(H;S_x)}{|D|}=\frac1t.}
\]

## 8. Anonymous terminal outputs

One terminal output cannot create incremental value-rigidity on a fixed domain, so at least two outputs are necessary when `H<G`.

With two named outputs, color `F_=` by one output and its complement by the other. The active stabilizer is exactly `H`.

For anonymous outputs, an additional carrier automorphism could survive only if it exchanges `F_=` with its complement.

The two fiber sizes are

\[
|F_=|=b(b-1)t,
\]

\[
|F_\neq|=b(b-1)t(t-1).
\]

Thus output exchange is impossible unless

\[
t=t(t-1),
\]

which forces `t=2`.

A transitive degree-two group is necessarily `S_2`. For `b>=3`, mapping equality to inequality on every ordered branch pair would require assigning two branch phases so that every pair of distinct branches receives opposite phases, impossible with three or more branches. For `b=2`, it is possible.

### Theorem 8.1 — Unique anonymous-swap exception

For every transitive branch group `A`,

\[
\boxed{
\exists g\in G:\ gF_=F_\neq
\iff
A=S_2,\ t=2,\ b=2.
}
\]

Therefore the seven-vertex `D_8 -> V_4` example is the unique balanced exception in this entire transitive wreath family.

Outside that exception, two anonymous terminal outputs already recover exactly `H` on the natural cross domain.

In the exceptional case, one `G`-fixed anchor cell such as `(o,o)` breaks the output swap, giving the previously proved exact nine-cell FCOA compiler.

## 9. Two-sided stabilizer formulation

For a diagonal-`A`-invariant relation

\[
R\subseteq\Lambda^2,
\]

define

\[
L_A(R)=
\{(\alpha,\beta)\in A^2:(\alpha\times\beta)R=R\}.
\]

For the corresponding uniform cross-branch fiber

\[
F_R=
\{((r,i),(s,j)):r\neq s,(i,j)\in R\},
\]

an element

\[
(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_b;\pi)
\]

stabilizes `F_R` iff

\[
(\sigma_r,\sigma_s)\in L_A(R)
\]

for every ordered pair `r!=s`.

Thus the base-group component of the stabilizer is the compatibility clique set

\[
C_b(R)=
\left\{
(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_b)\in A^b:
(\sigma_r,\sigma_s)\in L_A(R)\ \forall r\neq s
\right\}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Stab}_G(F_R)=C_b(R)\rtimes S_b.
}
\]

For the equality relation `R=Delta_Lambda`,

\[
L_A(\Delta_\Lambda)=\Delta A,
\]

because

\[
(\alpha\times\beta)\Delta_\Lambda=\Delta_\Lambda
\]

implies `alpha(i)=beta(i)` for every `i`, hence `alpha=beta`. Therefore

\[
C_b(\Delta_\Lambda)=\Delta A,
\]

recovering Theorem 2.1.

This two-sided stabilizer is the correct general object when future target subgroups are not the diagonal coherence subgroup.

## 10. What remains genuinely hard

The non-2-transitive case is **not** difficult for the specific target

\[
H=\Delta A\times S_b,
\]

because the diagonal orbital already gives the absolute orbit-size lower bound.

The genuinely new difficulty begins when the desired residual subgroup is different, for example:

- blockwise diagonal subgroups associated with partitions of the `b` branches;
- proper subgroups `K<A` retained inside the diagonal;
- graph-of-isomorphism subgroups between only selected branch pairs;
- iterated diagonal subgroups inside deeper wreath products;
- non-normal prescribed stabilizers for which no quotient reduction is available.

For these targets, one must optimize over unions of `H`-orbits and the two-sided compatibility relation becomes nontrivial.

## 11. Relation to existing theory

Regular sets, distinguishing colorings, wreath-product distinguishing numbers, subgroup-relative distinguishing numbers, and setwise stabilizers are classical nearby notions. In particular:

- a regular set is a subset with trivial setwise stabilizer;
- distinguishing-number theory minimizes the number of colors needed to kill symmetry;
- subgroup-relative distinguishing asks that color-preserving automorphisms lie in a specified subgroup;
- recent finite-permutation-group work studies structural properties of setwise stabilizers.

The quantity

\[
m_G(H;S)
\]

used here instead minimizes **support cardinality** under the exact equality

\[
\operatorname{Stab}_G(F)=H.
\]

No external priority claim is made for this exact extremal quantity without a dedicated literature audit.

The branch-coherence family now has an exact theorem at the natural level of generality:

\[
\boxed{
A\text{ transitive of degree }t,
\quad
G=A\wr S_b,
\quad
H=\Delta A\times S_b
}
\]

implies

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
m_G(H;S_x)&=b(b-1)t,\\
|S_x|&=b(b-1)t^2,\\
[G:H]&=|A|^{b-1},\\
I_{\rm coh}&=(b-1)\log_2|A|.

## 13. Next strike

The diagonal target is now solved for all transitive branch groups. The next sharp problem is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{classify }m_{A\wr S_b}(H;S_x)
\text{ for non-diagonal residual subgroups }H.
}
\]

The first tractable family should be **partition-diagonal stabilizers**: partition the branch set into blocks and require one common internal phase inside each block but allow independent phases between blocks. This interpolates between full wreath symmetry and global coherence and should produce an exact support-vs-residual-symmetry hierarchy.
