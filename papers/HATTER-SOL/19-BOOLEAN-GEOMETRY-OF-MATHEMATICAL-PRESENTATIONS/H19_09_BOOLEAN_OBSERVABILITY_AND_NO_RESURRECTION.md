## 2. Compiler states and deterministic transport

Fix two E0-equivalent mathematical presentations (M_1,M_2).

At compiler stage (i), let

[
C_i(M)
]

denote the complete stage state: the full object passed to the next frozen
compiler stage, including all structure on which that stage may depend.

Assume the frozen compiler tower is deterministic:

[
C_{i+1}(M)=F_i(C_i(M)).
]

The map (F_i) may perform optimization, rewriting, technology mapping, or
other deterministic transformation.

No injectivity is assumed.

## 4. Theorem 1 — monotonicity under observer refinement

If

[
O_apreceq O_b,
]

then

[
oxed{

u_i(O_a)le 
u_i(O_b).
}
]

### Proof

If (
u_i(O_a)=0), no conclusion is forced.

If (
u_i(O_a)=1), then

[
O_a(C_i(M_1))
e O_a(C_i(M_2)).
]

Were (O_b(C_i(M_1))=O_b(C_i(M_2))), applying the factor map (f) would give
equality under (O_a), a contradiction. Hence (
u_i(O_b)=1). (square)

### Consequence

The hidden-observable set

[
mathcal H_i=
{O:
u_i(O)=0}
]

is downward closed in the observer order, while the visible-observable set

[
mathcal V_i=
{O:
u_i(O)=1}
]

is upward closed.

For a finite observer poset, (mathcal V_i) is therefore determined by its
minimal visible antichain.

This is the first exact sense in which H19 obtains a **Boolean geometry of
observability**: one presentation pair induces a monotone (0/1) field over a
partially ordered family of observers.

### Proof

## 7. Theorem 2 — no resurrection of exact-state distinctions

For a deterministic compiler tower,

[
oxed{
eta_i=0
Longrightarrow
eta_{i+1}=0.
}
]

Equivalently,

[
oxed{
eta_{i+1}=1
Longrightarrow
eta_i=1.
}
]

### Proof

If

[
C_i(M_1)=C_i(M_2),
]

then determinism gives

[
F_i(C_i(M_1))
=
F_i(C_i(M_2)),
]

hence

[
C_{i+1}(M_1)=C_{i+1}(M_2).
]

The contrapositive gives the second form. (square)

### Corollary 2.1 — form of an exact-state survival word

Along a deterministic compiler tower, the full-state visibility word cannot
contain the transition

[
0	o1.
]

Therefore every exact-state survival word has the form

[
oxed{

}
]

after the first stage at which exact equality occurs.

A later stage cannot reconstruct a distinction that is absent from the full
input state supplied to that stage.

## 8. Theorem 3 — retrospective hidden-state witness

Suppose a later declared observable (O_j) distinguishes the pair:

[

u_j(O_j)=1.
]

Then for every earlier stage (ile j),

[
oxed{
eta_i=1.
}
]

### Proof

Since an observable cannot distinguish equal complete states,

[

u_j(O_j)=1
Longrightarrow
eta_j=1.
]

Repeated application of Theorem 2 backwards by contraposition gives

[
eta_{j-1}=cdots=eta_i=1.
]
(square)

Thus a later re-separation is a certificate that the earlier coarse equality
hid a real full-state distinction.

## 10. Visibility matrix

A single survival word suppresses observer resolution. H19 should therefore
treat the primary finite experimental object as a Boolean matrix

[
oxed{
Gamma_{i,alpha}
=

u_i(O_alpha)
}
]

whose columns are compiler stages and whose rows are declared observers.

At each fixed stage, observer refinement forces monotonicity along the observer
poset by Theorem 1.

Across stages, no such monotonicity is assumed for coarse observables.

The full-state row is special: by Theorem 2 it is monotone nonincreasing.

## 12. Claim boundary

The order-theoretic statements above are elementary consequences of observer
factorization and deterministic maps.

H19 does **not** claim novelty for those abstract facts in isolation.

The research contribution under investigation is their use as a disciplined
comparison language for mathematical presentations passing through real
compiler and FPGA flows, together with controlled finite examples where the
visibility frontier changes.

No circuit lower bound, technology-independent complexity invariant, or
irreversible information-loss claim follows from the H19-07/H19-08 data.
