# HATTER-SOL-09 research kernel · world-dependent two-interface factor networks

**Branch:** `research/hatter-sol-world-interface-operators`  
**Parent line:** HATTER-SOL-07 / HATTER-SOL-08  
**Status:** exploratory; no publication claim.

## 0. Research question

HATTER-SOL-07 assigns a scalar degree-capacity to each factor and studies network free boundary under arithmetic refinement. HATTER-SOL-08 keeps the factor capacities fixed while changing the admissible graph class.

The present branch asks a different question:

> what happens when changing the arithmetic/geometric world changes the factorization itself, and the resulting factors are then represented by a HATTER-SOL-type network?

The basic laboratory pair is `7` / `53`:

- in `Z[i]`, `53` splits and `7` is inert;
- in `Z[omega]`, `7` splits and `53` is inert.

Classical splitting theory is not the claimed contribution. The target is the composition

`world-dependent factorization -> two-interface factor representation -> HATTER-SOL network invariant`.

## 1. Minimal quadratic-world model

Let a quadratic world be generated by `F` with

\[
F^2=u+vF,
\qquad u,v\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Write a factor as

\[
\alpha=L+WF.
\]

For now `L` and `W` are neutral names for two virtual channels. They are **not yet declared to be nonnegative port counts**.

Multiplication is

\[
(L+WF)(A+BF)
=
(LA+uWB)
+
(LB+WA+vWB)F.
\]

Hence the two-channel composition law is

\[
\boxed{
(L,W)\star_F(A,B)
=
(LA+uWB,\;LB+WA+vWB).
}
\]

## 2. World generator

Multiplication by `F` on the coordinate column `(L,W)^T` is represented by

\[
\boxed{
J_F=
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & u\\
1 & v
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\]

It satisfies

\[
J_F^2=uI+vJ_F.
\]

For

\[
\alpha=L+WF,
\]

define the regular two-channel block

\[
\boxed{
M_F(\alpha)=LI+WJ_F
=
\begin{pmatrix}
L & uW\\
W & L+vW
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
M_F(\alpha\beta)=M_F(\alpha)M_F(\beta).
}
\]

Thus a factorization becomes an exact cascade factorization of two-interface integer blocks.

This is a cleaner interpretation of the temporary `L/W/F` notation than treating `|L|` and `|W|` immediately as ordinary port counts.

## 3. Norm and conjugation

The determinant is

\[
\boxed{
N_F(L,W)
=
\det M_F(L,W)
=
L^2+vLW-uW^2.
}
\]

The conjugate root is

\[
\bar F=v-F.
\]

Therefore

\[
\overline{L+WF}
=(L+vW)-WF,
\]

or in coordinates

\[
\boxed{
C_F(L,W)=(L+vW,-W).
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
M_F(\alpha)M_F(\bar\alpha)=N_F(\alpha)I.
}
\]

This identity is the first exact bridge between a scalar integer and a world-dependent two-channel cascade.

## 4. Square / Gaussian world

For `F=i`,

\[
i^2=-1,
\qquad (u,v)=(-1,0).
\]

Hence

\[
J_i=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\]

\[
M_i(L,W)=
\begin{pmatrix}
L&-W\\
W&L
\end{pmatrix},
\]

and

\[
N_i(L,W)=L^2+W^2.
\]

For `53`:

\[
53=(7+2i)(7-2i),
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
53I
=
M_i(7,2)M_i(7,-2).
}
\]

Explicitly,

\[
M_i(7,2)=
\begin{pmatrix}
7&-2\\
2&7
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M_i(7,-2)=
\begin{pmatrix}
7&2\\
-2&7
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The integer `7` is inert in `Z[i]`, so no corresponding nonunit Gaussian cascade factorization of `7I` exists inside this two-dimensional subalgebra.

## 5. Triangular / Eisenstein world

For `F=omega`,

\[
\omega^2+\omega+1=0,
\qquad (u,v)=(-1,-1).
\]

Hence

\[
J_\omega=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-1\\
1&-1
\end{pmatrix},
\]

\[
M_\omega(L,W)=
\begin{pmatrix}
L&-W\\
W&L-W
\end{pmatrix},
\]

and

\[
N_\omega(L,W)=L^2-LW+W^2.
\]

For `7`:

\[
7=(3+\omega)(3+\omega^2).
\]

Since

\[
3+\omega^2=2-\omega,
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{
7I
=
M_\omega(3,1)M_\omega(2,-1).
}
\]

Explicitly,

\[
M_\omega(3,1)=
\begin{pmatrix}
3&-1\\
1&2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M_\omega(2,-1)=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&1\\
-1&3
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The integer `53` is inert in `Z[omega]`, so the mirrored nonunit Eisenstein cascade factorization of `53I` does not exist.

## 6. The 7 / 53 mirror

The pair gives the first clean world discriminator:

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
& \mathbb Z[i] & \mathbb Z[\omega]\\
\hline
7  & \text{inert} & \text{split}\\
53 & \text{split} & \text{inert}
\end{array}
\]

In two-channel language this becomes:

- `53I` admits a nontrivial cascade decomposition in the Gaussian block algebra but not the Eisenstein block algebra;
- `7I` admits a nontrivial cascade decomposition in the Eisenstein block algebra but not the Gaussian block algebra.

This reformulation alone is classical quadratic-ring arithmetic in matrix clothing. It becomes a HATTER-SOL problem only after a network/free-boundary structure is added and compared across worlds.

## 7. First hostile test: basis / unit dependence

A naive interpretation

\[
(L,W)\mapsto (|L|,|W|)
\]

as two ordinary nonnegative port capacities is not automatically canonical.

Multiplication by a unit changes coordinates. In the Eisenstein world, for example,

\[
(3+\omega)\omega=-1+2\omega,
\]

so the same associate class is represented by `(3,1)` or `(-1,2)`. Their naive coordinate `l1` totals are `4` and `3`.

Therefore any final port-count or boundary functional must either:

1. be invariant under the unit action;
2. explicitly fix a gauge / representative;
3. or use the full two-channel block rather than raw coordinate magnitudes.

This is an open design constraint, not a cosmetic issue.

## 8. Candidate canonical data

Objects worth testing before choosing a HATTER-SOL boundary law:

1. the full block `M_F(alpha)`;
2. the quadratic norm `det M_F(alpha)` (canonical but likely too lossy);
3. the conjugation pair `M_F(alpha), M_F(bar alpha)`;
4. the lattice/ideal image `M_F(alpha) Z^2`;
5. Smith normal form of `M_F(alpha)` as an integer-lattice invariant;
6. a world-adapted positive quadratic form / Gram metric;
7. unit-orbit or a canonical fundamental-domain representative.

No scalarization is accepted yet.

## 9. Relation to HATTER-SOL-07

HATTER-SOL-07 uses a scalar factor `m` as a degree-capacity. Here a factor is instead a two-channel transfer block.

The unresolved bridge is therefore not `factorization -> matrix` (classical), but

\[
\boxed{
M_F(\alpha)
\longrightarrow
\text{typed HATTER-SOL network node / edge law}
\longrightarrow
\text{world-comparable boundary invariant}.
}
\]

A satisfactory bridge must not silently erase the difference between the square and triangular worlds.

## 10. Operator caution

`J_F` is already a natural **local world generator**: it encodes how the second channel acts on the two-channel state.

It is **not** being called a Laplacian.

A future Laplacian-like `operator of worlds` should only be introduced if a graph/category of worlds and natural transition/restriction maps emerge from the model. Matrix-weighted graph Laplacians, connection Laplacians, gain graphs, and sheaf Laplacians are established prior art; merely building a block Laplacian would not be novel.

## 11. Immediate proof / experiment obligations

1. Classify the unit action on `(L,W)` in the Gaussian and Eisenstein worlds and reject non-invariant port functionals.
2. Determine whether Smith-normal-form data gives any information beyond ordinary split/inert/ramified type.
3. Find a canonical two-channel capacity/boundary notion compatible with the HATTER-SOL-07 scalar boundary in an appropriate degeneration or forgetful limit.
4. Test `7`, `53`, and small products under that boundary.
5. Compare `factor first -> network` with `network first -> world lift`; if the two routes differ, define the exact commutation defect.
6. Only after (3)-(5), investigate a global operator on the space of worlds.

## 12. Publication discipline

No novelty claim is made for:

- Gaussian/Eisenstein splitting laws;
- norms or companion/regular matrix representations;
- gain graphs with complex/Eisenstein units;
- matrix-weighted, connection, or sheaf Laplacians.

A publication candidate requires a theorem specifically about the interaction of **world-dependent arithmetic factorization with the HATTER-SOL network/free-boundary construction**.
