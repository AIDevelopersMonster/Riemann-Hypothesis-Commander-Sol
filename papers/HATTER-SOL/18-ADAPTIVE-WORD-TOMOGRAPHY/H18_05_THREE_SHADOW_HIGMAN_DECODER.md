# Three projective shadows reconstruct the Higman trace

**Status:** CLOSED structural theorem layer.

## 1. The question

H18-03 showed that the four Nielsen worlds are exactly the fibers of

\[
\tau(A,B)=\operatorname{tr}([\widetilde A,\widetilde B])\in\mathbb F_7.
\]

H18-04 then showed that giving \(\tau\) explicitly to the adaptive classifier does not improve the optimal \(W_4\) decision depth.

Why can ordinary projective class observations already recover the information hidden in the \(SL(2,7)\) lift?

The answer is that three very special length-four word classes are **shadows of the Fricke coordinates shifted by the commutator trace**.

---

## 2. Fricke coordinates

Choose determinant-one lifts

\[
\widetilde A,\widetilde B\in SL(2,7),
\]

and put

\[
x=\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde A),\qquad
y=\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde B),\qquad
z=\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde A\widetilde B).
\]

Let

\[
\tau=\operatorname{tr}([\widetilde A,\widetilde B]).
\]

Fricke's identity gives

\[
\boxed{
\tau=x^2+y^2+z^2-xyz-2.
}
\]

The sign choices of the two lifts change \(x,y,z\) in the usual correlated way, but do not change \(\tau\).

---

## 3. The three shadow words

Define

\[
R_z=A^2B^2=\texttt{AABB},
\]

\[
R_x=ABAB^{-1}=\texttt{ABAb},
\]

\[
R_y=ABA^{-1}B=\texttt{ABaB}.
\]

Then in \(SL(2)\):

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}(R_x)=x^2-\tau,
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}(R_y)=y^2-\tau,
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}(R_z)=z^2-\tau.
}
\]

### Proof of the first identity

Let

\[
C=BAB^{-1}.
\]

Then \(\operatorname{tr}(C)=x\).  The basic \(SL(2)\) trace relation

\[
\operatorname{tr}(UV)+\operatorname{tr}(UV^{-1})
=
\operatorname{tr}(U)\operatorname{tr}(V)
\]

with \(U=A\), \(V=C\) gives

\[
\operatorname{tr}(ABAB^{-1})
+
\operatorname{tr}(ABA^{-1}B^{-1})
=
x^2.
\]

The second term is \(\tau\), hence

\[
\operatorname{tr}(ABAB^{-1})=x^2-\tau.
\]

The \(y\)-identity is symmetric.

For \(A^2B^2\), Cayley-Hamilton gives

\[
A^2=xA-I,\qquad B^2=yB-I,
\]

so

\[
\operatorname{tr}(A^2B^2)
=
xyz-x^2-y^2+2.
\]

Substituting Fricke's identity yields

\[
\operatorname{tr}(A^2B^2)=z^2-\tau.
\]

Thus the three shadows are not arbitrary short words: they are direct algebraic carriers of the three shifted Fricke squares.

## 4. Why projective classes suffice

For a projective element \(g\in PSL(2,7)\), choose either lift \(\widetilde g\in SL(2,7)\) and define

\[
\boxed{
\sigma(g)=\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde g)^2.
}
\]

This is well-defined because replacing \(\widetilde g\) by \(-\widetilde g\) changes the trace sign but not its square.

For the six H17 classes:

| PSL class | \(\sigma\) |
|---|---:|
| \(1A\) | \(4\) |
| \(2A\) | \(0\) |
| \(3A\) | \(1\) |
| \(4A\) | \(2\) |
| \(7A\) | \(4\) |
| \(7B\) | \(4\) |

Therefore an ordinary class observation already reveals the projective trace-square.

For the shadow words put

\[
S=
\bigl(
\sigma(R_z),\sigma(R_x),\sigma(R_y)
\bigr).
\]

## 5. The four-line decoder

For every generating H17 pair, \(\tau\) is reconstructed from \(S=(s_1,s_2,s_3)\) by:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{if some }s_i=0, & \tau=4,\\[2mm]
\text{else if }S=(2,2,2), & \tau=5,\\[2mm]
\text{else if at least two }s_i=4, & \tau=6,\\[2mm]
\text{otherwise}, & \tau=3.
\end{array}
}
\]

This rule succeeds on all 114 canonical generating-orbit states.

Even the distinction \(7A/7B\) is unnecessary here: the coarser projective trace-square values already determine \(\tau\).

## 6. Why the rule is not a 114-row accident

The certificate checks the rule first in the entire trace cube

\[
(x,y,z)\in\mathbb F_7^3.
\]

Restrict to the four Higman levels relevant to generating pairs,

\[
\tau\in\{3,4,5,6\}.
\]

Among all such Fricke triples, the decoder has exactly seven exceptions:

\[
(0,0,0),\qquad
(0,0,\pm1),
\]

and the permutations of \((0,0,\pm1)\).

Every exception therefore has **at least two zero Fricke coordinates**.

Now use the group structure.

If

\[
x=\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde A)=0,
\]

then Cayley-Hamilton gives

\[
\widetilde A^2=-I,
\]

so the projective element \(A\) is an involution.

Likewise \(y=0\) makes \(B\) an involution, and \(z=0\) makes \(AB\) an involution.

If two among \(x,y,z\) vanish, then one of the Nielsen-equivalent generating pairs

\[
(A,B),\qquad
(A,AB),\qquad
(B,AB)
\]

consists of two involutions.

But a group generated by two involutions is dihedral: if \(s^2=t^2=1\) and \(r=st\), then

\[
srs=r^{-1}.
\]

Hence it cannot be \(PSL(2,7)\).

Therefore none of the seven exceptional trace triples lies on the generating locus.

This proves the decoder structurally:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Fricke identity}
+
\text{projective trace-square}
+
\text{two-involution obstruction}
\Longrightarrow
\tau\text{ from three class observations}.
}
\]

## 9. Conceptual interpretation

The three shadow words are a discrete analogue of observing three shifted coordinates of the Fricke character surface:

\[
x^2-\tau,\qquad
y^2-\tau,\qquad
z^2-\tau.
\]

Projectivization removes signs, leaving their squares.

For \(q=7\), generatingness removes exactly the seven trace configurations that would make this sign loss ambiguous.

That is the mechanism.

It is much more informative than saying only that a 114-row lookup table happens to reconstruct \(\tau\).

## 10. New theorem statement

### Three-shadow reconstruction theorem for the H17 generating locus

Let \((A,B)\) be a generating ordered pair of \(PSL(2,7)\).  Define

\[
R_z=A^2B^2,\qquad
R_x=ABAB^{-1},\qquad

Then the Higman commutator-lift trace

\[
\tau(A,B)

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cl}(R_z),\quad

Moreover, among all class-valued observers induced by freely reduced words of length at most four, no one or two observers determine \(\tau\).

Thus the exact short-word observation complexity of the Higman trace is

\[

\]
