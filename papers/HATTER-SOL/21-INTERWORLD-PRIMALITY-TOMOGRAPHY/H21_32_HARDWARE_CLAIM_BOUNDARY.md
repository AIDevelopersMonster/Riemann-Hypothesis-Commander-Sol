# HATTER-SOL-21 · HARDWARE CLAIM BOUNDARY AFTER PROJECTIVE/NORM REDUCTION

Status: **ARCHITECTURE CORRECTION / REQUIRED NON-CLAIM**

## 1. Known runtime object

The runtime primality/factor observer receives

\[
n
\]

and world parameters

\[
(B,C,\Delta).
\]

For a composite semiprime

\[
n=pq,
\]

the factors \(p,q\) are not known in advance.

## 2. Local geometry is factor-conditioned

The quantities

\[
h_p,\ e_p,\ d_p,\ D_p,\ \lambda_p,\ \beta_p
\]

are defined only after reduction modulo an actual prime factor \(p\).

Therefore these quantities are excellent **analysis coordinates** for
understanding why a world exposes a factor, but they are not directly available
to the runtime hardware before factorization.

## 3. Required non-claim

H21 must not currently claim that

\[
\boxed{
\text{projective/norm/binary local compression replaces the runtime quadratic
Frobenius core}.
}
\]

That would require knowing the very local prime factor whose revelation is the
goal.

## 4. Legitimate hardware consequences

The current mathematics can affect hardware in three defensible ways.

### A. Offline world compiler

Use broad training/certification populations to estimate:

- silent worlds;
- world diversity \(G_W\);
- exact exposure \(Q_W\);
- complementarity;
- expected revelation time.

The compiler emits a short ordered world schedule.

Runtime hardware still executes the ordinary quadratic world observer, but on
fewer or better-ordered worlds.

### B. Schedule-level expected-cost reduction

For a declared input population, compare:

\[
\text{static schedule}
\quad\text{vs}\quad
\text{compiled schedule}
\]

in:

- average worlds tested;
- modular multiplications;
- cycles;
- energy;
- latency-to-first-certificate.

This is an operational consequence that does not require access to \(p,q\).

### C. Architecture pruning

Provably silent descriptors, such as the torsion \(D=-3\) defect world, can be
eliminated or replaced by cheap divisibility prechecks.

This gives a direct area/control/energy consequence without factor-local
metadata at runtime.

## 5. What remains possible but unproved

A future architecture might derive projective/norm information directly in the
composite ring \(A_n\) without knowing \(p,q\), or exploit CRT-compatible
metadata implicitly.

No such runtime construction has yet been proved.

Therefore it is outside the present claim set.

## 6. Publication consequence

A hardware publication route remains viable, but the comparison must be:

\[
\boxed{
\text{uncompiled multi-world runtime}
\quad\text{vs}\quad
\text{H21-compiled schedule/runtime}
}
\]

rather than a misleading comparison of full quadratic arithmetic against a
factor-conditioned local representation.

This correction narrows the hardware claim and strengthens reproducibility.
