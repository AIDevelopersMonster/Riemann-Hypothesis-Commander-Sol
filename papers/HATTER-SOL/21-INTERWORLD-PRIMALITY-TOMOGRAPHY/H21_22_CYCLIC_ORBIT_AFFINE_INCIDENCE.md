# HATTER-SOL-21 · CYCLIC-ORBIT / AFFINE-LINE INCIDENCE TARGET

Status: **NEXT ACTIVE THEOREM STRIKE**

## 1. Local orbit geometry

Fix a nonexceptional prime \(p\) and quadratic world

\[
A_p=\mathbf F_p[x]/(x^2-Bx-C).
\]

Let

\[
H_p=\langle x\rangle
\]

be the cyclic subgroup generated by the distinguished element.

The local Lucas clock is exactly the orbit length

\[
|H_p|=h_W(p).
\]

Every exponent phase \(m\) corresponds to one orbit point

\[
x^m=a_m+b_mx.
\]

## 2. Mask-defining affine lines

For fixed character sign of the incoming exponent, the two zero bits are affine
coordinate tests on \((a_m,b_m)\).

### Split local case

If

\[
\chi_p=+1,
\]

then for incoming sign \(\chi_m\),

\[
z_1=1
\iff
b_m=\chi_m,
\]

and

\[
z_0=1
\iff
a_m=\frac{1-\chi_m}{2}B.
\]

Thus the two zero conditions are incidence with one horizontal and one vertical
affine line in the coordinate plane of \(A_p\).

### Inert local case

If

\[
\chi_p=-1,
\]

the outer conjugation changes the constant-coordinate test to an affine line

\[
a_m+B b_m=\text{declared constant}.
\]

The \(x\)-coordinate condition remains

\[
b_m=\chi_m.
\]

Hence every local mask is determined by membership of the cyclic orbit
\(H_p\) in two affine lines.

## 3. Exact phase sets

For a mask \(z\), define

\[
A_{p,z}
=
\{m\bmod h_p:\mathcal I_{W,p}(m)=z\}.
\]

The phase-set size is therefore an exact orbit-line incidence count.

The previous compiler statistics used only

\[
|A_{p,z}|.
\]

The missing coupling information is the reciprocal placement of prime phases
inside these sets.

## 4. First theorem targets

### Orbit-line intersection bound

Bound

\[
|H_p\cap L|
\]

for the relevant affine lines \(L\).

In the split case, use the decomposition

\[
A_p\cong\mathbf F_p\times\mathbf F_p
\]

to convert the intersection problem into simultaneous multiplicative
congruences.

In the inert case, use

\[
A_p\cong\mathbf F_{p^2}
\]

and interpret affine lines as trace-type conditions.

### Exact full-mask criterion

The full mask \(\{0,1\}\) is exactly the intersection point of the two affine
conditions, hence corresponds to the prime-law target element.

This should recover the known local Frobenius pass condition as a one-point
orbit incidence.

### Partial-mask geometry

The masks \(\{0\}\) and \(\{1\}\) are the orbit points lying on exactly one of
the two affine lines.

These are precisely the phases capable of producing one-coordinate gcd
factor extraction.

## 5. Reciprocal incidence graph

For a finite prime population, create vertices

\[
(p,m)
\]

with \(m\in\mathbb Z/h_p\mathbb Z\), colored by mask.

A prime pair \((p,q)\) selects

\[
(p,q\bmod h_p)
\]

and

\[
(q,p\bmod h_q).
\]

Factor revelation occurs exactly when the selected vertex colors differ.

Same-mask coupling is excess reciprocal selection of equal colors.

This graph is the exact finite combinatorial object that all previous scalar
descriptors were approximating.

## 6. Why this is the correct next geometry

Earlier candidates failed because they retained only:

- orbit length;
- common orbit-period divisors;
- coarse residue locks.

The incidence code retains the **placement** of the orbit relative to the
observer hyperplanes.

That placement is precisely what zero-mask observation depends on.

## 7. Hardware relevance

If the incidence code has a compact description, the FPGA scheduler may not
need arbitrary quadratic exponentiation for world selection.

A compiled world descriptor could carry:

- orbit period metadata;
- compressed phase-to-mask tables;
- or algebraic predicates for the two affine-line incidences.

The expensive arithmetic core would then be reserved for final certification,
while world scheduling could operate on compact incidence metadata.
