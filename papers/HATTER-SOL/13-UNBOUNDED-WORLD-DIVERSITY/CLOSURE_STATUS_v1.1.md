## Final theorem spine

HATTER-SOL-13 closes with the following proved layers:

1. fixed imaginary-quadratic conductor towers have finite fixed-`n` element-divisor diversity;
2. for the fixed rational prime `2` in
   \[
   K_k=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^k-1}),
   \]
   the number of prime ideals above `2` is
   \[
   n_k=\frac{\varphi(2^k-1)}{k}\to\infty;
   \]
3. the residue-degree port model gives the exact unrestricted connected-carrier boundary spectrum;
4. bounded/subcritical orientable genus preserves asymptotically all of the port budget;
5. for every fixed `0<eta<=1`, the minimum orientable genus required to exhaust at least an `eta` fraction of the port budget satisfies
   \[
   \gamma_k(\eta)=\Theta(n_k k).
   \]

## Claim boundary

The arithmetic prime-decomposition data are canonical. The graph carrier is an additional model structure. No Shannon-information, payload, cryptographic, or physical-memory claim is made.

## Handoff to HATTER-SOL-14

The remaining structural weakness of H13 is deliberate: carriers were allowed to be arbitrary connected simple graphs and were not required to respect the Galois action on the prime ideals above `2`.

H14 therefore begins with the question:

> What survives of the H13 erasure theory when the carrier itself must be canonical with respect to arithmetic symmetry?

The natural vertex set is

\[
X_k=\operatorname{Gal}(K_k/\mathbb Q)/D_k,
\qquad D_k=\langle 2\rangle,
\]

and, because the cyclotomic Galois group is abelian, the quotient acts regularly on `X_k`. Galois-equivariant carriers are therefore expected to reduce to Cayley-type graphs on the quotient group.
