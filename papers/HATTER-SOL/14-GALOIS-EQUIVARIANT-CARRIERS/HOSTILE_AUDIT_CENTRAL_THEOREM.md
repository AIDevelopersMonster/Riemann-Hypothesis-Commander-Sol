# HATTER-SOL-14 · Hostile Audit of the Central Infinite Separation Theorem

**Status:** PASS with one mandatory claim-boundary refinement.

## 1. Infinite arithmetic progression

Take sufficiently large

\[
k\equiv15\pmod{30}.
\]

Then `3|k` and `5|k`, so

\[
7=2^3-1\mid2^k-1,
\qquad
31=2^5-1\mid2^k-1.
\]

Also `k` is odd.

## 2. Two canonical 2-primary factors survive the decomposition quotient

For any positive exponents `a,b`,

\[
(\mathbb Z/7^a\mathbb Z)^\times
\cong C_{6\cdot7^{a-1}},
\qquad
(\mathbb Z/31^b\mathbb Z)^\times
\cong C_{30\cdot31^{b-1}}.
\]

Hence each contributes exactly one `C_2` direct factor to the 2-primary part of the unit group.

The decomposition subgroup `<2>` has order `k`, hence odd order on this progression. Therefore it lies entirely in the odd-primary part and quotienting by `<2>` leaves the full 2-primary direct factor unchanged.

Thus

\[
\boxed{\Gamma_k\cong C_2\oplus C_2\oplus H_k}
\]

for some finite abelian `H_k`.

## 3. Small generating set for the remaining factor

Let `r=omega(2^k-1)`. The unit group modulo `2^k-1` is a product of `r` cyclic odd-prime-power unit groups, so every quotient and direct factor needed here can be generated by at most `r` elements.

Choose generators for `H_k` and symmetrize them under inversion. This gives an inverse-closed generating set `R_k` with

\[
|R_k|\le2r.
\]

Moreover

\[
r=o(k).
\]

An elementary proof is enough: the product of `r` distinct primes is at least of factorial size, while `2^k-1<2^k`; hence `r log r=O(k)` and therefore `r=O(k/log k)`.

Thus

\[
|R_k|+2\le k
\]

for all sufficiently large `k` in the progression, so the HATTER port-capacity constraint is satisfied.

## 4. Same abstract graph construction

Write

\[
\Gamma_k=C_2(a)\oplus C_2(c)\oplus H_k.
\]

Define

\[
S_{k,1}=R_k\cup\{a,c\},
\qquad
S_{k,2}=R_k\cup\{a+c,c\}.
\]

Both are inverse-closed generating sets of the same cardinality.

The automorphism

\[
\phi(a)=a+c,
\qquad
\phi(c)=c,
\qquad
\phi|_{H_k}=\operatorname{id}
\]

maps `S_{k,1}` to `S_{k,2}`. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Cay}(\Gamma_k,S_{k,1})
\cong
\operatorname{Cay}(\Gamma_k,S_{k,2})
\]

as abstract graphs. Hence degree, edge number, scalar boundary, Betti number, orientable genus, and every ordinary graph-isomorphism invariant coincide.

## 5. Fixed-action homology separation

Let `n_k=|Gamma_k|`. In the first carrier, `a` is an involution direction; in the second it is not. Any involutions contained in `R_k` lie in the direct factor `H_k` and therefore are distinct from `a`, `c`, and `a+c`.

The character fingerprint theorem therefore gives

\[
\chi_{1}(a)=1-\frac{n_k}{2},
\qquad
\chi_{2}(a)=1.
\]

Hence

\[
H_1(C_{k,1};\mathbb Q)
\not\cong
H_1(C_{k,2};\mathbb Q)
\]

as modules for the **fixed arithmetic group action** of `Gamma_k`. Integral module isomorphism is also impossible, since rationalization would contradict this separation.

## 6. Mandatory claim-boundary refinement

The graph isomorphism is induced by the group automorphism `phi`. Consequently the two cycle modules are related by twisting the `Gamma_k` action through `phi`.

Therefore H14 must claim:

\[
\boxed{
\text{the abstract graph does not determine the cycle module relative to the fixed arithmetic Galois action.}
}
\]

It must NOT claim that the two representations remain inequivalent after quotienting representation data by all automorphisms of `Gamma_k`.

Equivalently, the information being retained is the **arithmetic marking / regular action**, not an absolute representation type independent of that marking.

## 7. Refined classification principle

For fixed `Gamma`, equivariant rational `H_1` recovers the exact involution subset `T` together with the number of non-involutory inverse-pair directions.

If the arithmetic marking is forgotten and group automorphisms are allowed, the invariant descends to the `Aut(Gamma)`-orbit of that data.

This distinction should be explicit in the abstract, theorem statement, and interpretation section.
