# HATTER-SOL-11 · Orbit-Fusion Classification

Status: closed theorem layer.

This file classifies the natural direction orbits of the HATTER-SOL step set in every imaginary quadratic discriminant world and separates a canonical norm-shell filtration from the stronger, generally false, idea of sequential port activation.

## 1. Natural action

Let `K` be an imaginary quadratic field with discriminant `Delta`, ring of integers `O_K`, and the HATTER-SOL-09 additive step set

\[
S_\Delta=\{\pm1,\pm F_\Delta,\pm\bar F_\Delta\},
\]

with duplicates removed.

Pass to undirected step directions

\[
D_\Delta=S_\Delta/\{\pm1\}.
\]

Let `Gamma_Delta` be the permutation group on `D_Delta` generated by:

1. multiplication by units of `O_K`, whenever it preserves the step-direction set;
2. complex conjugation.

The orbit partition of `D_Delta` under `Gamma_Delta` is the natural port-orbit partition.

No ordering of these orbits is assumed.

---

## 2. Generic even worlds

Assume

\[
\Delta\equiv0\pmod4,
\qquad \Delta<-4.
\]

Write

\[
F=\frac{\sqrt\Delta}{2},
\qquad F^2=-q,
\qquad q=\frac{|\Delta|}{4}>1.
\]

Then

\[
D_\Delta=\{[1],[F]\}.
\]

The unit group is `+-1`. Conjugation fixes `[1]` and sends `F` to `-F`, hence also fixes `[F]` as an undirected direction.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
D_\Delta=A\sqcup T,
\qquad
A=\{[1]\},
\qquad
T=\{[F]\}.
}
\]

There are exactly two natural direction orbits.

Their element norms are

\[
|N(1)|=1,
\qquad
|N(F)|=q>1.
\]

Thus they are canonically distinguished by norm shell.

---

## 3. Gaussian fusion

For

\[
\Delta=-4,
\qquad F=i,
\]

we have

\[
D_{-4}=\{[1],[i]\}.
\]

The Gaussian unit `i` sends

\[
[1]\mapsto[i],
\qquad
[i]\mapsto[-1]=[1].
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
D_{-4}\text{ is a single }\Gamma_{-4}\text{-orbit}.
}
\]

The two generic-even orbits fuse exactly at the Gaussian world.

---

## 4. Generic odd worlds

Assume

\[
\Delta\equiv1\pmod4,
\qquad \Delta<-3.
\]

Put

\[
F=\frac{1+\sqrt\Delta}{2},
\qquad
\bar F=1-F,
\qquad
q=F\bar F=\frac{1+|\Delta|}{4}>1.
\]

Then

\[
D_\Delta=\{[1],[F],[\bar F]\}.
\]

The unit group is again `+-1`. Conjugation fixes `[1]` and exchanges `[F]` and `[bar F]`.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
D_\Delta=A\sqcup O,
\qquad
A=\{[1]\},
\qquad
O=\{[F],[\bar F]\}.
}
\]

There are exactly two natural direction orbits.

Again

\[
|N(1)|=1,
\qquad
|N(F)|=|N(\bar F)|=q>1,
\]

so the orbit partition is also a norm-shell partition.

---

## 5. Eisenstein fusion

For

\[
\Delta=-3,
\qquad
F=\frac{1+\sqrt{-3}}2,
\]

all three directions have norm one.

Modulo sign, multiplication by the unit `F` cycles the directions:

\[
[1]\mapsto[F],
\qquad
[F]\mapsto[F^2]=[-\bar F]=[\bar F],
\qquad
[\bar F]\mapsto[1].
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
D_{-3}\text{ is a single }\Gamma_{-3}\text{-orbit}.
}
\]

The two generic-odd orbits fuse exactly at the Eisenstein world.

## 6. The orbit-fusion theorem

### Theorem T11.5 — complete direction-orbit classification

For the HATTER-SOL additive direction set in an imaginary quadratic field:

\[
\boxed{
\#(D_\Delta/\Gamma_\Delta)=
\begin{cases}
1,&\Delta=-4,\\
1,&\Delta=-3,\\
2,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
\]

More precisely:

- generic even worlds: axial and transverse singleton orbits;
- Gaussian world: those two orbits fuse;
- generic odd worlds: axial singleton plus one conjugate oblique orbit;
- Eisenstein world: all three directions fuse.

Thus the only orbital-collapse points are precisely the two imaginary quadratic fields with extra roots of unity.

## 7. Canonical norm-shell filtration

For every generic world define

\[
\mathcal F_1
=\{d\in D_\Delta:|N(d)|=1\},
\]

and

\[
\mathcal F_2=D_\Delta.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_1\subsetneq\mathcal F_2
}
\]

in every generic imaginary quadratic world.

Explicitly:

- generic even: `F_1=A`, `F_2=A union T`;
- generic odd: `F_1=A`, `F_2=A union O`.

At the Gaussian and Eisenstein worlds all step directions already have norm one, so

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_1=\mathcal F_2=D_\Delta.
}
\]

### Corollary T11.5a — filtration collapse

The canonical two-level norm-shell filtration collapses to one level exactly at

\[
\boxed{
\Delta=-4,-3.
}
\]

This is a genuine invariant filtration. It is determined by arithmetic norm, not by the numerical convention `P>=Q`.

## 8. No universal strongest-first activation law

The existence of the norm-shell filtration does **not** imply a sequential activation rule.

### Theorem T11.6 — activation no-go

In every generic world there are elements whose unique/minimal HATTER-SOL step representation uses the outer norm shell while using zero capacity from the inner shell.

### Even proof

For any `m>0`, take

\[
\alpha=mF.
\]

Its minimal representation uses `m` transverse steps and no axial step. Thus the outer shell is active while the inner shell has zero usage.

### Odd proof

Likewise, for

\[
\alpha=mF
\]

in a generic odd world, the unique geodesic triple of T11.1 is

\[
(0,m,0),
\]

so only the oblique orbit is used.

Hence no intrinsic law of the form

\[
\text{outer orbit may activate only after inner orbit saturation}
\]

can hold for all factors/elements.

### Consequence

The correct universal statement is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{canonical orbit filtration exists}
\quad\text{but}\quad
\text{universal sequential activation does not}.
}
\]

Any future activation law must depend on additional data: host constraints, weights, witness residue sector, or another explicitly stated optimization principle.

## 10. Prior-art boundary

The classification of units in imaginary quadratic fields is classical: only the Gaussian and Eisenstein fields have more than the two units `+-1`. The new HATTER-SOL question is not that unit classification itself, but how it acts on the chosen network-interface direction set and whether the resulting orbit fusion survives into factor-network observables.

The next layer should therefore track arithmetic factorization and principalization residues across this orbit-fusion map rather than invent a universal activation order.
