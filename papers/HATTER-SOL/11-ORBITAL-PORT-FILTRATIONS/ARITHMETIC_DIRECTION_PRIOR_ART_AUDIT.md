# HATTER-SOL-11 · Specialized Arithmetic-Direction Prior-Art Audit

**Date:** 2026-09-14  
**Status:** focused negative search; suitable for freezing the v0.9 novelty paragraph with conservative wording, but not a proof of global novelty.

## 1. Search target

The target was not generic lattice symmetry. The search asked for an existing construction equivalent or close to

\[
\boxed{
\text{imaginary-quadratic direction-orbit datum}
\to
\text{magnitude-sorted finite forgetting fiber}
\to
\text{degree-constrained Pareto network response}
\to
\text{geometry-controlled collision filtration}.
}
\]

Keywords covered imaginary quadratic lattices, Gaussian/Eisenstein direction orbits, geodesic/shortest-path coordinate systems, symmetry quotients, arithmetic networks, periodic quotient graphs, and lattice-orbit classifications.

## 2. Nearby literature found

### 2.1 Bianchi/Farey/geodesic structures

There is active work on Farey graphs and geodesic expansions over imaginary quadratic fields, where vertices live in `Q(sqrt(-d))` and graph/geodesic structure is induced by Bianchi-type group actions.

This is genuinely adjacent arithmetic/geometric graph theory, but its object is hyperbolic/Farey adjacency and continued-fraction/geodesic expansion, not a finite local port-capacity forgetting fiber followed by HATTER Pareto optimization.

### 2.2 Schmidt arrangements / visual arithmetic of imaginary quadratic fields

Katherine E. Stange's work *Visualising the arithmetic of imaginary quadratic fields* studies the orbit of the real line under the Bianchi group and obtains circle arrangements encoding norms and ideal-class data.

This is important prior art for the general principle that arithmetic of an imaginary quadratic field can be encoded geometrically through group orbits. It does not appear to define the HATTER local direction-count fold or the network-memory filtration studied here.

Relevant preprint: arXiv `1410.0417`.

### 2.3 Orbits in lattices

Recent work titled *Orbits in lattices* develops algorithms for vector orbits under subgroups of orthogonal groups, with applications to Tits buildings and orthogonal modular varieties. DOI: `10.1016/j.jnt.2024.06.013`.

This confirms that lattice-orbit classification is a classical/active subject and must not be claimed as novel. The orbit notion and target application are nevertheless different from the symmetry quotient of the three HATTER step directions and its exact network response.

### 2.4 Periodic and labelled quotient graphs

Crystallographic/network literature uses quotient graphs and labelled quotient graphs for periodic nets, where quotient edges carry translational labels relative to a lattice basis. Recent work on continuous isometry classification of periodic point sets gives a clear modern formulation.

This is adjacent to the idea that a periodic lattice network can retain directional/translation information in edge labels. It does not match the HATTER construction, where the labels arise from arithmetic shortest-step orbit counts and are then aggregated into local degree capacities before Pareto optimization.

### 2.5 Eisenstein--Jacobi and Gaussian algebraic networks

There is substantial literature on Eisenstein--Jacobi and Gaussian quotient networks, including routing, Hamiltonian decompositions, spanning structures, broadcasting and fault tolerance. Recent publications continue this line.

This is highly relevant nomenclature-wise: algebraic integer rings have long been used to define network topologies. HATTER-SOL-11 must therefore avoid any statement suggesting that Gaussian/Eisenstein arithmetic networks themselves are new.

The present paper differs in the location of the arithmetic structure: the imaginary-quadratic world defines a **local factor-interface state**, and the theorem concerns when an external admissible host network can or cannot distinguish members of a local arithmetic forgetting fiber.

### 2.6 Perfect lattices over imaginary quadratic fields

Work on perfect lattices over imaginary quadratic fields studies Hermite-type invariants, equivalence under `GL(L)`, and direction-dependent quantities. This is adjacent arithmetic lattice theory but not an equivalent local direction-count / network-response construction.

## 3. What must not be claimed as new

The specialized search strengthens the following claim discipline.

Do not claim novelty for:

- using imaginary quadratic fields to construct graphs or networks;
- Gaussian or Eisenstein network topologies;
- group orbits of vectors/directions in lattices;
- labelled quotient graphs of periodic nets;
- Bianchi group geometry or Farey-type graphs;
- symmetry fusion caused by Gaussian/Eisenstein units;
- shortest-path coordinates on square/hexagonal lattices.

## 4. Distinctive composition that remained unmatched

No source found in this focused search stated an equivalent of the following package:

1. begin with the HATTER magnitude-sorted local pair `(P,Q)`;
2. canonically unfold it to the generic-odd symmetry-resolved orbital datum;
3. classify the complete finite fiber of the forgetting map;
4. map each member to an orbit-total degree-capacity state;
5. optimize a connected simple two-channel network via a full Pareto boundary;
6. classify exact collisions of the arithmetic fiber as geometry/host degree changes;
7. obtain the sharp regular-factorizable filtration

\[
\boxed{3\xrightarrow{d=P}2\xrightarrow{d=P+Q}1.}
\]

This is the strongest defensible novelty statement currently supported by the search.

## 5. Conservative novelty paragraph for v0.9

Recommended wording:

> The arithmetic, lattice-symmetry and graph-factor ingredients used here are individually classical. Imaginary-quadratic fields have long-standing geometric and network representations, while orbit problems in lattices and degree-constrained graph factors form established subjects. Our contribution is the specific composition developed for the HATTER interface: a symmetry-resolved finite fiber of the magnitude-sorted arithmetic state is coupled to an exact connected Pareto network response, and the resulting arithmetic distinctions are shown to undergo geometry- and degree-controlled collisions. We have not found an equivalent formulation in the focused literature search, but we do not claim novelty for the underlying lattice-orbit or factorization ingredients themselves.

## 6. Search verdict

**Arithmetic-prior-art gate: PASS for preprint-candidate wording.**

Meaning of PASS:

- no direct equivalent was found in the focused search;
- nearby literature has been identified and the novelty claim narrowed accordingly;
- the manuscript may proceed to v0.9 with conservative wording.

Meaning of PASS does **not** include:

- proof that no equivalent theorem exists anywhere;
- permission to claim a new general theory of lattice orbits;
- permission to claim Gaussian/Eisenstein networks as new.
