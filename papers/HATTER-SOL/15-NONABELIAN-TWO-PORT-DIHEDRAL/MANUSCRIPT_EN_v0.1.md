## Abstract

We study a minimal non-Abelian two-port system built from the dihedral action

\[
D_{2p}=\langle r,s\mid r^p=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle
\]

on the `p` Schreier sectors of a reflection subgroup. The induced ports are the translation and reflection

\[
R:i\mapsto i+1,
\qquad
S:i\mapsto-i,
\]

so that, with the convention `[A,B]=ABA^{-1}B^{-1}`,

\[
[R,S]=R^2.
\]

This elementary commutator becomes the organizing defect of the paper. We first show how the same noncommutativity appears as square holonomy, as the obstruction to a torus relation and the exact holonomy of a Klein-bottle relation, as pairing of Fourier channels `k` and `-k`, and as an object-dependent curvature observable in families of arithmetic worlds. At the coarse level, the flat/curved world profile reconstructs a projective arithmetic class; at finer spectral resolution, labelled real mirror channels retain a signless coordinate and oriented complex channels recover the full coordinate.

The main part of the paper then isolates a primitive Mahler observer. For the mirror block with angle `alpha` and spectral parameter `mu=4-lambda>=4`, its logarithmic Mahler response admits an expansion

\[
M_\mu(\alpha)
=C_\mu-\sum_{n\ge1}B_{\mu,n}\cos(2n\alpha),
\qquad B_{\mu,n}>0.
\]

We derive an exact Gauss-sum transform from these additive Mahler harmonics to multiplicative reaction modes on

\[
G_p=\mathbf F_p^\times/\{\pm1\}.
\]

The central theorem proves the uniform first-harmonic dominance inequality

\[
\boxed{
B_{\mu,1}>\sum_{n\ge2}B_{\mu,n}
\qquad(\mu\ge4).
}
\]

The proof uses Fourier/Parseval identities, a Legendre-polynomial representation of the resolvent moments, a finite exact rational certificate for the first seven layers, and a uniform Beta-integral estimate for the remaining tail. The finite certificate has been independently hostile-audited and the far-tail estimate was repaired in the audited v1.1 proof.

As a consequence, for every odd prime `p`, every nontrivial even Dirichlet character `chi mod p`, and every `mu>=4`, the primitive Mahler character response is nonzero. Equivalently, the centered primitive Mahler response operator is invertible on the full zero-sum signless reaction space

\[
\mathbf C[G_p]_0.
\]

Thus the complete family of labelled primitive Mahler responses gives full linear tomography of signless dihedral reactions for every odd prime world on the half-line `lambda<=0`.

The ingredients—Mahler measure, Gauss sums, Legendre identities, regulators, and finite Fourier analysis—are classical. We have not identified the exact first-harmonic dominance statement or the resulting all-prime reaction-tomography theorem in the literature searched; no priority claim is made beyond that targeted audit.

## 1. The question: what becomes visible only when the ports do not commute?

The purpose of HATTER-SOL-15 is not to replace one arithmetic invariant with another. The problem is more structural:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Which information channels exist specifically because two natural ports fail to commute?}
}
\]

In an Abelian two-port world the square

\[
ABA^{-1}B^{-1}
\]

closes. In a non-Abelian world it need not. The failure to close is therefore not an auxiliary error term: it is an observable.

The smallest laboratory in which this is explicit is dihedral. Let `p` be an odd prime and let

\[
D_{2p}=\langle r,s\mid r^p=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle.
\]

For the reflection subgroup

\[
D=\langle s\rangle\cong C_2,
\]

the left Schreier space

\[
X=D\backslash D_{2p}
\]

has `p` sectors, which we identify with `F_p`. The two canonical induced ports are

\[
R:i\mapsto i+1,
\qquad
S:i\mapsto-i.
\]

The reflection conjugates translation to its inverse:

\[
SRS^{-1}=R^{-1}.
\]

With

\[
[A,B]=ABA^{-1}B^{-1},
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{[R,S]=R^2.}
\]

Since `p` is odd, `R^2` is a full `p`-cycle. The square defect is therefore maximally nontrivial on the Schreier sectors.

The rest of the paper follows this defect through several observers. The main surprise is that after passing through a primitive Mahler observer the first harmonic generated by this non-Abelian square remains strong enough to dominate every higher harmonic uniformly on `mu>=4`.

## 2. The same defect seen by topology, Fourier analysis, and arithmetic worlds

### 2.1 Surface relations

For a torus the fundamental relation is

\[
[A,B]=1.
\]

The dihedral pair fails this relation:

\[
[R,S]=R^2\ne1.
\]

For the Klein bottle, however, the relation is

\[
ABA^{-1}=B^{-1}.
\]

Taking `A=S` and `B=R` gives

\[
SRS^{-1}=R^{-1},
\]

so the same non-Abelian pair is exact on the Klein bottle.

For orientable genus two, one can cancel two opposite square defects:

\[
[R,S][R^{-1},S]
=R^2R^{-2}=1.
\]

Thus noncommutativity can be stored either as local curvature or as global topological compensation.

A useful one-branch covering formula is the following. If a permutation commutator `K` acts on `n` sheets, the associated one-branch torus cover has genus

\[
g=1+\frac{n-c(K)}2,
\]

where `c(K)` is the number of cycles. For `K=R^2` on `p` sectors,

\[
\boxed{g=\frac{p+1}{2}.}
\]

### 2.2 Fourier pairing

Let

\[
\omega=e^{2\pi i/p},
\]

and choose Fourier vectors `e_k` with

\[
Re_k=\omega^k e_k.
\]

Reflection acts by

\[
Se_k=e_{-k}.
\]

Thus non-Abelianity forces the pairing

\[
\boxed{k\leftrightarrow-k.}
\]

A natural two-port Bloch operator is

\[
L(z,w)
=4I-zR-z^{-1}R^{-1}-(w+w^{-1})S.
\]

The zero mode is scalar, while every nonzero mirror pair gives a `2x2` block. Writing

\[
z=e^{i\theta},
\qquad
w=e^{i\phi},
\qquad
\alpha_k=\frac{2\pi k}{p},
\]

the block is

\[
L_k(\theta,\phi)=
\begin{pmatrix}
4-2\cos(\theta+\alpha_k)&-2\cos\phi\\
-2\cos\phi&4-2\cos(\theta-\alpha_k)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its eigenvalues are

\[
\lambda_{k,\pm}
=
4-2\cos\theta\cos\alpha_k
\pm2\sqrt{\sin^2\theta\sin^2\alpha_k+\cos^2\phi}.
\]

The non-Abelian port pair therefore creates a mirror-block spectral geometry rather than independent one-dimensional characters.

### 2.3 The first local spectral moment that sees the square

For closed walks generated by the two ports, length less than four cannot traverse a commutator square. At length four the words

\[
RSR^{-1}S^{-1}
\quad\text{and}\quad
SRS^{-1}R^{-1}
\]

appear. Consequently the fourth spectral moment contains

\[
\operatorname{Tr}K+\operatorname{Tr}K^{-1},
\qquad
K=[R,S].
\]

For permutation ports

\[
\operatorname{Tr}K=\#\operatorname{Fix}(K).
\]

The flat square `K=I` contributes `p`; the dihedral square `K=R^2` contributes `0`. Thus the fourth local moment is the first local spectral observable sensitive to this square holonomy.

This length-four fact will reappear analytically as the order `mu^{-4}` at which the first channel-dependent Mahler harmonic enters.

## 3. Object-dependent square curvature and arithmetic worlds

## 4. What a full invariant spectrum forgets

A crucial control result is that a sufficiently invariant observer can erase information even though the labelled primitive observer retains it.

For an odd modulus `n`, let

\[
L_a(z,w)
=4I-zR^a-z^{-1}R^{-a}-(w+w^{-1})S.
\]

If `u` is a unit modulo `n`, the permutation

\[
U_u:x\mapsto ux
\]

satisfies

\[
U_uR^aU_u^{-1}=R^{ua},
\qquad
U_uSU_u^{-1}=S.
\]

Therefore

\[
L_a\sim L_{ua}.
\]

For prime `p`, all nonzero `a` lie in one unit orbit. Hence:

### Proposition 4.1 — prime spectral blindness of the unlabelled full operator

For every nonzero `a,b in F_p`,

\[
\boxed{L_a(z,w)\sim L_b(z,w)}
\]

pointwise in `(z,w)`.

Thus every basis-invariant full spectral observable sees only

\[
\boxed{a=0\quad\text{versus}\quad a\ne0.}
\]

In particular, the full determinant and its full Mahler measure cannot distinguish nonzero reaction coordinates.

By contrast, a **labelled primitive mirror block** depends on

\[
\vartheta_{ak}=2\cos\frac{2\pi ak}{p}
\]

and therefore retains the signless coordinate `{a,-a}`. The loss occurs when all primitive labels are multiplied together into the Galois-invariant norm, not when one takes the scalar Mahler measure of an individual primitive block.

This is why the primitive observer, not the full determinant, is the correct object for tomography.

## 5. The primitive Mahler observer

where

\[
A_\mu(u)=\mu-2\cos u.

For `mu>=4` and `0<alpha<pi`, this determinant is strictly positive on the integration torus. Define its logarithmic Mahler response

\[
M_\mu(\alpha)
=
\frac1{(2\pi)^2}
\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}

\[

The coefficients are real, even in `n`, and strictly positive. Consequently

\[

A single labelled primitive Mahler value already recovers the signless reaction coordinate `{a,-a}`.

## 8. All-prime Mahler tomography

### Theorem 8.1 — nonvanishing of every nontrivial even character mode

Let `p` be an odd prime, let `chi mod p` be nontrivial and even, and let `mu>=4`. Then

\[
\boxed{\widehat f_\mu(\chi)\ne0.}
\]

### Proof

From Theorem 6.1,

\[
\widehat f_\mu(\chi)
=-\frac12\tau(\overline\chi)\chi(2)
\left(
B_{\mu,1}
+
\sum_{\substack{n\ge2\\p\nmid n}}B_{\mu,n}\chi(n)
\right).
\]

Since `|chi(n)|<=1`,

\[
\left|
\sum_{\substack{n\ge2\\p\nmid n}}B_{\mu,n}\chi(n)
\right|
\le
\sum_{n\ge2}B_{\mu,n}
< B_{\mu,1}.
\]

Therefore the bracket cannot vanish. The Gauss sum is nonzero for a nontrivial character modulo a prime, so the full coefficient is nonzero. `square`

### Corollary 8.2 — full linear reaction tomography

For every odd prime `p` and every `lambda<=0`, the centered primitive Mahler response operator is invertible on

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf C[G_p]_0,
\qquad
G_p=\mathbf F_p^\times/\{\pm1\}.
}
\]

For `p=3` this zero-sum space is zero-dimensional; for every `p>=5` the statement is a genuine nontrivial character-by-character theorem.

Thus the response family

\[
a\longmapsto \mathbf M_a
\]

is not merely injective on reaction classes. It linearly resolves arbitrary zero-sum superpositions of signless reactions.

## 9. What the theorem says about non-Abelian information

The article began with the elementary identity

\[
[R,S]=R^2.
\]

The final theorem should not be read as saying that every later analytic identity is caused solely by this one equation. The rigorous statement is more precise.

The dihedral noncommutativity creates the mirror pairing and the square-holonomy response. The first local spectral moment sensitive to this square occurs at length four. In the primitive Mahler expansion, the first channel-dependent contribution occurs correspondingly at order `mu^{-4}` and appears as the first harmonic `cos(2alpha)`. Theorem 7.1 then proves that this first harmonic dominates the aggregate of all higher harmonics on the entire half-line `mu>=4`.

Hence the proved chain is

\[
\boxed{
\text{noncommuting dihedral ports}
\to
\text{square commutator holonomy}
\to
\text{mirror-channel Mahler response}
\to
\text{first-harmonic dominance}
\to
\text{all-prime character nonvanishing}
\to
\text{full linear reaction tomography}.
}
\]

The information hierarchy is equally important:

\[
\boxed{
\text{coarse curvature}
\prec
\text{unlabelled full spectrum}
\prec
\text{labelled primitive real observer}
\prec
\text{oriented complex observer}.

\]

The full invariant prime spectrum collapses all nonzero reaction coordinates into one unit orbit, whereas the labelled primitive Mahler observer recovers the signless coordinate and its full orbit response is linearly invertible.

Non-Abelianity therefore does not merely create more spectral complexity. It creates a hierarchy of observer-dependent information, and the primitive observer retains information that Galois-invariant aggregation erases.

## 10. Secondary arithmetic and motivic structures

The main theorem above does not require the motivic branch of H15, but that branch clarifies what is lost at different stages of aggregation.

For a primitive mirror block the spectral curve admits a multiquadratic normalization. Under genericity its Jacobian decomposes, up to isogeny, into three elliptic factors and one genus-two factor. The natural regulator form transforms in the pure `abc` character and descends to the genus-two quotient. The symbol

\[
\{z,w\}
\]

is tempered and defines a rational `K_2` class on the curve; after transfer it descends to a rational `K_2` class on the genus-two quotient.

For prime `p`, the primitive sheets form a Galois orbit under

\[
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbf Q(\zeta_p+\zeta_p^{-1})/\mathbf Q)
\cong
(\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^\times/\{\pm1\}.
\]

A single primitive motivic sheet retains the signless coordinate `{a,-a}`. Multiplication over the full Galois orbit erases that label, exactly as the full determinant does spectrally.

This provides an independent motivic reflection of the observer hierarchy, but the universal tomography theorem itself is analytic and does not depend on unproved regulator conjectures.

## 11. Prior art and claim boundary

Mahler measure is connected to Jensen formulas, special values of `L`-functions, regulators, arithmetic geometry, Toeplitz determinants, and Fuglede–Kadison determinants. The use of finite Fourier transforms, Dirichlet characters, and Gauss sums at roots of unity is classical. Likewise, the Legendre representation of resolvent moments and the Beta-integral estimates used in Theorem 7.1 are classical analytic tools.

Relevant anchors include Boyd's work on Mahler measure and special `L`-values; Rodriguez Villegas on identities and modular Mahler measures; Lalín's regulator computations and later genus-two regulator work with Wu; Deninger's comparison of Mahler measure and Fuglede–Kadison determinants; Smyth's survey of Mahler measure; and the recent work of Cornelissen, Hokken and Ringeling on the asymptotic Mahler measure of Gaussian periods.

The targeted literature audit performed for this project found close prior art for all of these ingredients, but did not identify the exact inequality

\[
B_{\mu,1}>\sum_{n\ge2}B_{\mu,n}
\qquad(\mu\ge4)
\]

for the primitive dihedral Mahler family, nor the resulting all-prime linear reaction-tomography theorem. This is not a priority claim: terminology and parametrizations vary, and a closer antecedent may exist.

The publication-safe claim is therefore:

> For the dihedral primitive mirror-block Mahler family considered here, we prove a uniform first-harmonic dominance inequality on `mu>=4`. Combined with a Gauss-sum transform, this yields nonvanishing of every nontrivial even Dirichlet-character response mode for every odd prime. We did not identify this exact statement in the literature searched.
