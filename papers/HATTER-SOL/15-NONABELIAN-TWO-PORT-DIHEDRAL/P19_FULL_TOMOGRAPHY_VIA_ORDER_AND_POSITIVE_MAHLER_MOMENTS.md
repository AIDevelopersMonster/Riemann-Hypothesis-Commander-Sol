## 3. The primitive `q=1` mode

Let `chi` be the character of `G_19` defined by

\[
\chi([2])=\zeta.
\]

For the half-system representatives `1<=r<=9`, the discrete logarithms give

\[
\bigl(\chi(1),\ldots,\chi(9)\bigr)
=
\bigl(
1,\zeta,\zeta^4,\zeta^2,\zeta^7,\zeta^5,
\zeta^6,\zeta^3,\zeta^8
\bigr).
\]

Set

\[
x_r:=\cos^2\frac{\pi r}{19},
\qquad
x_1>x_2>\cdots>x_9>0.
\]

For `j>=1`, define the twisted cosine-square moment

\[
T_j:=\sum_{r=1}^{9}\chi(r)x_r^j.
\]

The real parts of the character values are

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Re\chi(1)&=1,\\
\Re\chi(2)&=\cos40^\circ,\\
\Re\chi(3)&=-\cos20^\circ,\\
\Re\chi(4)&=\cos80^\circ,\\
\Re\chi(5)&=\cos80^\circ,\\
\Re\chi(6)&=-\cos20^\circ,\\
\Re\chi(7)&=-\frac12,\\
\Re\chi(8)&=-\frac12,\\
\Re\chi(9)&=\cos40^\circ.
\end{aligned}}
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Re T_j
={}&
\bigl(x_1^j-\cos20^\circ\,x_3^j\bigr)
+
\bigl(\cos40^\circ\,x_2^j-\cos20^\circ\,x_6^j\bigr)
\\
&+
\cos80^\circ(x_4^j+x_5^j)
-
\frac12(x_7^j+x_8^j)
+
\cos40^\circ x_9^j.
\end{aligned}
\]

The first, second, and last groups are strictly positive for every `j>=1`.

Indeed,

\[
x_1>x_3,
\qquad
\cos20^\circ<1,
\]

and

\[
\cos40^\circ>\frac1{\sqrt2},
\qquad
x_2>\frac34,
\qquad
x_6<\frac12,
\]

so

\[
\cos40^\circ x_2
>
\frac{3}{4\sqrt2}
>
\frac12
>
\cos20^\circ x_6.
\]

Since `x_2>x_6`, the same inequality remains true after taking any positive power.

It remains to handle the middle pair.

### The case `j=1`

Use

\[
\cos80^\circ
=\sin10^\circ
>\frac16,
\]

the strict chord inequality for sine on `[0,30 degrees]`.

Also

\[
x_4+x_5
=
1+
\cos\frac{9\pi}{19}
\cos\frac{\pi}{19}
>1.
\]

Further,

\[
x_7<\frac14
\]

because `7pi/19>pi/3`, and

\[
x_8
<\cos^2\frac{5\pi}{12}
=\frac{2-\sqrt3}{4}
<\frac1{12}
\]

because `8pi/19>5pi/12`.

Hence

\[
\cos80^\circ(x_4+x_5)
>\frac16
>
\frac12(x_7+x_8).
\]

### The case `j>=2`

Now

\[
x_4>\frac12,
\qquad
x_7<\frac14,
\]

so

\[
\left(\frac{x_4}{x_7}\right)^j>4>3.
\]

Since `cos80 degrees>1/6`,

\[
\cos80^\circ x_4^j
>\frac12x_7^j.
\]

Likewise,

\[
x_5>\frac14,
\qquad
x_8<\frac1{12},
\]

so

\[
\left(\frac{x_5}{x_8}\right)^j>9>3,
\]

and therefore

\[
\cos80^\circ x_5^j
>\frac12x_8^j.
\]

Thus in every case

\[
\boxed{\Re T_j>0\qquad(j>=1).}
\]

## 4. The `q=2` mode from ordered Abel summation

Write the coefficient of `b_r` in `S_2` as `d_r`. Their arguments are

\[
-80^\circ,\ 0^\circ,\ -120^\circ,\ 80^\circ,
120^\circ,\ -40^\circ,\ 40^\circ,\ 160^\circ,\ -160^\circ.
\]

Let

\[
P_k:=\Re\sum_{r=1}^{k}d_r.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_1&=\cos80^\circ,\\
P_2&=1+\cos80^\circ,\\
P_3&=\frac12+\cos80^\circ,\\
P_4&=\frac12+2\cos80^\circ,\\
P_5&=2\cos80^\circ,\\

Every `P_k` is strictly positive; for the last one it is enough to note

\[

\[
\boxed{
\Re S_2
=-\sum_{k=1}^{8}P_k(b_{k+1}-b_k)<0.
}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{S_2\ne0.}

and therefore also

\[
\boxed{S_7\ne0.}

by conjugation.
