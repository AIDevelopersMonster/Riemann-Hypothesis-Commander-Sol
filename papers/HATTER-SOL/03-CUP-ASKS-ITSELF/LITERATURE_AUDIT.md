# HATTER-SOL-03 · Hostile Literature Audit

## Scope

This audit targets the mathematical spine of HATTER-SOL-03:

- the directed prime-chain relation `q | p-1`;
- exact exponent depth `v_q(p-1)`;
- Pratt trees / prime chains;
- random/Rado graph shadows of prime relations;
- expansions of Skolem arithmetic by successor-like information;
- the status of the adaptive prime-power query relation `J` and the prime-restricted predecessor relation `S_P`.

The audit is intentionally conservative. Failure to locate a theorem is not evidence of priority.

## 1. Prime chains and Pratt trees are classical

The hierarchy generated by prime divisors of `p-1` is classical.

- V. R. Pratt, **Every prime has a succinct certificate**, SIAM J. Comput. 4 (1975), 214-220. This is the classical source of Pratt certificates / Pratt trees.
- Prime chains of the form `p_1 | p_2-1 | ...` have been studied extensively in analytic and combinatorial number theory.

Therefore HATTER-SOL-03 must not claim novelty for the `p-1` descent itself, for Pratt trees, or for using factors of `p-1` recursively.

## 2. Jones's prime graph is direct prior art

Gareth A. Jones studies a directed labelled graph on primes with an arc

`q -> p` iff `q | p-1`,

labelled by the exact exponent `r` when `q^r || p-1`.

He proves strong universality statements for finite acyclic labelled directed graphs realized inside this prime graph. He also shows that, after deleting `2`, forgetting labels and directions, the resulting graph on odd primes is the countable random (Rado) graph.

Reference:

- G. A. Jones, **Regular embeddings of complete bipartite graphs: classification and enumeration**, Proc. London Math. Soc. 101 (2010), 427-453, DOI `10.1112/plms/pdp061`, especially Sections 13-14.

Consequences:

- the labelled prime graph itself is not new;
- the Rado-graph phenomenon after forgetting direction/depth is not new;
- HATTER-SOL novelty, if any, must be in the automorphism-rigidity analysis of expansions of the standard multiplicative monoid and in the information/minimality viewpoint.

## 3. Quadratic-residue random graph construction is classical

Peter J. Cameron discusses a number-theoretic realization of the random graph using quadratic residues on primes congruent to `1 mod 4`.

Reference:

- P. J. Cameron, **The Random Graph Revisited**, in *European Congress of Mathematics*, Vol. II, 2001, pp. 267-274, DOI `10.1007/978-3-0348-8268-2_15`.

This directly supports the HATTER-SOL-03 warning that anonymous uniform probes may preserve or restore large symmetry rather than destroy it.

The literary/structural interpretation is ours; the random-graph construction is classical.

## 4. Skolem arithmetic is mature prior art

The base structure `(N_{>0}, x)` is classical Skolem arithmetic.

Relevant references already used in HATTER-SOL-02 include:

- A. Mostowski, **On direct products of theories**, JSL 17 (1952), 1-31, DOI `10.2307/2267454`.
- A. Bès; D. Richard, **Undecidable Extensions of Skolem Arithmetic**, JSL 63 (1998), 379-401, DOI `10.2307/2586837`.
- A. Stonestrom, **Some model theory of Th(N,·)**, Math. Logic Quarterly 68 (2022), 288-303, DOI `10.1002/malq.202100049`.

Therefore broad claims such as “adding a weak relation to multiplication is new” are not defensible.

## 5. Full successor is much stronger than prime-restricted predecessor

Classically, multiplication together with the full successor relation is already strong enough to recover much more arithmetic structure; Julia Robinson's definability methods connect multiplication, successor, and addition.

HATTER-SOL-03 uses only

`S_P(n,p) iff p is prime and p=n+1`.

The current results establish rigidity of

`(N_{>0}, x, S_P)`

but do **not** establish definability of full successor, definability of addition, or undecidability of the complete theory.

Those stronger consequences must not be inferred from rigidity alone.

## 6. Prime-predecessor equivalence is the cleanest positive formulation

Within the multiplicative monoid, the following three relations are first-order interdefinable:

1. exact prime predecessor
   `S_P(n,p) iff p is prime and n=p-1`;
2. adaptive prime-power divisibility query
   `J(a,p) iff a=q^k is a nontrivial prime power and a | p-1`;
3. maximal exact prime-power predecessor
   `H(a,p) iff a=q^e and q^e || p-1`.

This equivalence is elementary from unique factorization and definability of divisibility in multiplication.

The literature audit did not locate this exact equivalence stated as an automorphism-rigidity mechanism for Skolem arithmetic. That is a negative targeted-search result only.

## 7. Directed support-only rigidity remains unresolved in the current search

For

`D(q,p) iff q,p are prime and q | p-1`,

Jones's results give substantial finite universality and an undirected Rado shadow, but the targeted search did not locate a theorem determining

`Aut(P,D)`

or equivalently the rigidity of the full directed support graph.

HATTER-SOL-03 therefore should state this as an **open frontier of the present work**, not as a solved classical fact or as a new theorem.

The rank-by-rank multiplicity reduction in `DIRECTED_SUPPORT_STRIKE.md` is our current exact reformulation of the obstruction.

## 8. Radical-predecessor reformulation

On the full multiplicative carrier, `D(q,p)` is equivalent to the relation

`R_P(r,p) iff r = rad(p-1)` and `p` is prime.

Thus the unresolved relational-minimality question is cleanly:

`Is (N_{>0}, x, R_P) rigid?`

This formulation appears more useful for future literature searches than the graph language alone.

No exact prior theorem settling this rigidity question was located in the current audit.

## 9. Query-ladder minimality is architecture-specific

The query-ladder gap theorem proves that, within a fixed threshold family `A subset N`, exact recovery of every valuation depth requires `A=N_{>=1}`; dyadic or zero-density ladders leave unavoidable gaps.

This is a theorem about a specific **threshold-query architecture**.

It must not be promoted into the stronger claim that every proper subrelation of `J` fails to rigidify. Other, non-threshold encodings may carry the same information more efficiently.

This distinction is important for publication safety.

## 10. Publication-safe novelty claim

Recommended wording:

> HATTER-SOL-03 studies when a finite relational mechanism added to the standard multiplicative monoid can generate enough internal asymmetry to rigidify its prime generators. Classical prime-chain and Pratt-tree structures supply the recursive arithmetic substrate. The paper's contribution is the automorphism-rigidity viewpoint, the finite-seed barrier, the contrast between anonymous homogeneous probes and anchored recursive predecessor data, the prime-predecessor rigidity theorem, and exact minimality laws within the threshold-query model. The directed support-only case is isolated as a separate arithmetic frontier rather than claimed solved.

## 11. Claims to avoid

Do not claim:

- first discovery of the graph `q | p-1`;
- first use of Pratt trees or prime chains;
- first construction of a Rado graph from primes;
- that full successor or addition is definable from the prime-restricted predecessor relation unless separately proved;
- that the support-only directed graph is non-rigid merely because its undirected shadow is Rado;
- that the full ladder is globally minimal among all possible finite relational encodings.

## 12. Current assessment

The audit strengthens rather than weakens the paper.

The best positive theorem is now most cleanly stated as

`multiplication + exact predecessor on prime outputs -> rigidity`,

with `J` and `H` as equivalent presentations of the same finite relational information.

The clean unresolved boundary is

`exact predecessor p-1 -> rigid`

versus

`radical predecessor rad(p-1) -> rigidity currently unresolved`.

That is a sharper and more publication-safe frontier than the earlier informal “how many bits of depth?” formulation.
