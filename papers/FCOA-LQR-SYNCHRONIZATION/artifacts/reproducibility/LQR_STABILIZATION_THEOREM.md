# QGE3 LQR — Exact Large-Alphabet Stabilization

**Branch:** `director/fcoa-rigidity-cost`  
**Phase:** post-publication continuation  
**Result:** exact large-`q` formula for every fixed phase number `r`.

## 1. Main theorem

Let

\[
n=r-1,
\qquad
B_n=2^n-1=2^{r-1}-1.
\]

### Theorem 1.1 — exact stabilization with exact threshold

For every `r>=2` and every alphabet size

\[
q\ge B_n,
\]

\[
\boxed{
L_q(r)
=nq-B_n
=(r-1)q-(2^{r-1}-1).
}
\]

Moreover the threshold is exact:

\[
q<B_n
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
L_q(r)>nq-B_n.
\]

Thus the large-alphabet linear law begins exactly at

\[
\boxed{q_0(r)=2^{r-1}-1.}
\]

The upper bound is the binary-cut gadget of `LQR_BINARY_CUT_GADGET.md`. The new content here is the matching universal lower bound and the proof that equality cannot occur before the threshold.

## 2. Forest reduction and defect partitions

Let `S` be a synchronizing point-image constraint system on `r` phases. For every source color `a`, let `Gamma_a` be its constraint graph on the phase set

\[
R=\{0,1,\dots,r-1\}.
\]

Replace `Gamma_a` by a spanning forest inside each connected component. This leaves the propagated equalities unchanged and cannot increase the number of constraints. Hence for lower bounds we may assume every `Gamma_a` is a forest.

Let `P_a` be the partition of `R` into connected components of `Gamma_a`. Write

\[
c_a=|P_a|.
\]

Since a forest with `c_a` components on `r` vertices has exactly `r-c_a` edges,

\[
|E(\Gamma_a)|=r-c_a.
\]

Relative to the fully connected per-color cost `r-1`, define the color defect

\[
d_a=(r-1)-|E(\Gamma_a)|=c_a-1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
|S|=(r-1)q-\sum_a d_a.
}
\]

The problem is now to bound the total defect.

---

## 3. Cut space attached to a partition

Distinguish phase `0` and identify the set of all normalized binary cuts of `R` with

\[
V=\mathbb F_2^{r-1}.
\]

For

\[
x=(x_1,\dots,x_{r-1})\in V,
\]

extend by `x_0=0`. The vector `x` represents the bipartition of `R` according to the two bit values. The zero vector is the trivial one-block cut; nonzero vectors are the

\[
2^{r-1}-1
\]

nontrivial bipartitions.

For a set partition `P` of `R`, define

\[
\boxed{
W(P)=\{x\in V:x\text{ is constant on every block of }P\}.
}
\]

Equivalently, `x` represents a bipartition coarser than `P`.

### Lemma 3.1 — partition defect equals cut-space dimension

If `P` has `c` blocks, then

\[
\boxed{\dim W(P)=c-1.}
\]

### Proof

The block containing phase `0` must receive bit `0`. Each of the other `c-1` blocks may independently receive bit `0` or `1`. Hence

\[
|W(P)|=2^{c-1},
\]

so its dimension is `c-1`. \(\square\)

Thus for the color partition `P_a`,

\[
\boxed{d_a=\dim W(P_a).}
\]

---

## 4. Join becomes subspace intersection

Let `P,Q` be partitions of `R`, and let `P\vee Q` denote their join in the partition lattice: the equivalence relation generated jointly by the blocks of `P` and `Q`.

### Lemma 4.1

\[
\boxed{
W(P)\cap W(Q)=W(P\vee Q).
}
\]

### Proof

A binary vector lies in both `W(P)` and `W(Q)` exactly when it is constant on every block of `P` and on every block of `Q`. This is equivalent to being constant on every equivalence class generated by those blocks, namely every block of `P\vee Q`. \(\square\)

Consequently,

\[
P\vee Q=\mathbf 1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
W(P)\cap W(Q)=\{0\},
\]

where `\mathbf 1` is the one-block partition.

## 5. Synchronization forces a partial subspace packing

The pair-union theorem from `LQR_LOWER_BOUNDS.md` says that for every two distinct source colors `a,b`,

\[
\Gamma_a\cup\Gamma_b
\text{ is connected}.
\]

In partition language,

\[
P_a\vee P_b=\mathbf 1.
\]

By Lemma 4.1,

\[
\boxed{
W(P_a)\cap W(P_b)=\{0\}
\qquad(a\ne b).
}
\]

Thus the cut spaces attached to different colors have pairwise disjoint sets of nonzero vectors.

This is a mixed-dimension partial subspace packing inside

\[
V=\mathbb F_2^{r-1}.
\]

No finite-geometry novelty is claimed for the notion of pairwise trivially intersecting subspaces; the point here is that it is forced canonically by the LQR synchronization constraints.

## 6. Mersenne packing inequality

A `d`-dimensional binary subspace contains exactly

\[
2^d-1
\]

nonzero vectors. Since the nonzero parts of the spaces `W(P_a)` are pairwise disjoint and `V` has exactly `B_n=2^n-1` nonzero vectors,

### Theorem 6.1 — Mersenne defect inequality

Every synchronizing system satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\sum_a\bigl(2^{d_a}-1\bigr)
\le
2^{r-1}-1.
}
\]

Because

\[
d\le2^d-1
\qquad(d\ge0),
\]

we immediately obtain

### Corollary 6.2 — universal defect cap

\[
\boxed{
\sum_a d_a\le2^{r-1}-1.
}
\]

Substituting into

\[
|S|=(r-1)q-\sum_a d_a
\]

gives the global lower bound

\[
\boxed{
L_q(r)
\ge
(r-1)q-(2^{r-1}-1)
}
\]

for every `q>=2`, whether or not the right-hand side is positive.

---

## 7. Equality conditions and the exact threshold

Suppose equality holds in the global lower bound. Then

\[
\sum_a d_a=2^{r-1}-1.
\]

But Theorem 6.1 gives

\[
\sum_a d_a
\le
\sum_a(2^{d_a}-1)
\le
2^{r-1}-1.
\]

Hence equality must hold in every term of

\[
d_a\le2^{d_a}-1.
\]

For nonnegative integers this happens only for

\[
d_a\in\{0,1\}.
\]

Therefore every positive-defect color contributes exactly one nonzero cut vector. To attain total defect

\[
2^{r-1}-1,
\]

there must be at least that many positive-defect colors. Thus

\[
q\ge2^{r-1}-1.
\]

So if

\[
q<2^{r-1}-1,
\]

the lower bound is necessarily strict.

Conversely, when

\[
q\ge2^{r-1}-1,
\]

`LQR_BINARY_CUT_GADGET.md` uses all nonzero cut vectors, one per active color, and makes every remaining color connected. Its cost is exactly

\[
(r-1)q-(2^{r-1}-1).
\]

This proves Theorem 1.1. \(\square\)

The theorem specializes to:

\qquad

### r=3

\[
2^{r-1}-1=3,
\qquad
L_q(3)=2q-3
\quad(q\ge3).

### r=4

### r=5

A new exact infinite family follows immediately:

\[
\boxed{

### r=6

Likewise,

\[
\boxed{
L_q(6)=5q-31
\qquad(q\ge31).

Thus every fixed phase number has an exactly determined linear tail.

## 9. Stronger finite-q lower-bound interface

The proof gives more than the eventual linear bound. For every synchronizing system the defect dimensions satisfy

\[
\sum_a(2^{d_a}-1)\le2^{r-1}-1.
\]

Hence the unresolved finite sector can be attacked through a finite packing problem in `F_2^{r-1}`.

Define the **partition-subspace defect capacity**

\[
\mathcal D_r(q)
=
\max\left\{
\sum_{i=1}^q \dim W(P_i):
W(P_i)\cap W(P_j)=\{0\}\ (i\ne j)
\right\},
\]

where the `P_i` range over partitions of the `r` phase indices and zero-dimensional spaces may be used to pad the family.

Then

\[
\boxed{
L_q(r)\ge(r-1)q-\mathcal D_r(q).
}
\]

The exact LQR problem is potentially stricter, because pairwise trivial intersection is necessary but does not by itself prove that the corresponding constraint system is synchronizing. For `r<=4`, the previously solved cases show that the relevant optimum can be realized by synchronizing gadgets.

This isolates the next finite-geometry/FCOA interface cleanly.

## 10. Literature boundary

Pairwise trivially intersecting subspaces are classical finite-geometry objects. Constant-dimension families are partial spreads; mixed-dimension families and exact coverings belong naturally to vector-space partition theory. Useful references include:

- O. Heden, **A survey of the different types of vector space partitions**, *Discrete Mathematics, Algorithms and Applications* 4(1) (2012), 1250001. DOI `10.1142/S1793830912500012`.
- T. Honold, M. Kiermaier, S. Kurz, **Partial spreads and vector space partitions**, in *Network Coding and Subspace Designs* (2018), 131–170. DOI `10.1007/978-3-319-70293-3_7`.

The partition-lattice/subspace-lattice viewpoint is also classical in lattice theory. Therefore no claim is made that subspace packings, partition lattices, or binary cut spaces themselves are new.

The programme-specific theorem is the chain

\[
\text{LQR synchronization}
\Longrightarrow
\text{pairwise joining component partitions}
\Longrightarrow
\text{pairwise trivially intersecting cut spaces}
\Longrightarrow
\text{exact defect cap}
\Longrightarrow
\text{exact stabilization threshold}.
\]
